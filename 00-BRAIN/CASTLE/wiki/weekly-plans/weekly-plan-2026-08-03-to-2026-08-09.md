## Move-On Gate — Advance on Proof, Not the Clock (added 2026-08-02, Chris-directed)

The block lists below are an **ordered queue, not a calendar**. Day labels
are a pacing default only. Chris sits down, takes the next item in the
queue, and this gate — not the clock — decides when he moves on:

1. **Enter cold:** try to state the relationship from memory first; missing
   here is fine — it just means start at the worked example.
2. **One worked rep, then one changed-parameter transfer** solved cold, no
   notes.
3. **Explain-back aloud:** what does the math predict, and what would that
   prediction look like physically?
4. **Pass = transfer + explain-back both clean → move on immediately**, even
   mid-block. Lingering on passed material is a gate violation, not diligence.
5. **Cap = two blocks maximum per item on this first pass.** Still not clean
   after a second block (worked with the matching crash-course module)?
   Write the exact miss to the miss log and move on anyway — Weeks C–D own
   repair, and the Aug 15/19 full sweeps plus the Aug 22 dress rehearsal are
   the safety net that catches anything that slips through.
6. **Miss log, one line per wobble:** the item, plus what broke — concept,
   setup, calculus mechanics, or boundary condition. Moving on is safe
   because nothing is dropped; it is routed.

Daily pacing check: items cleared versus days remaining to Aug 23. The aim

## Physics Block Contract — Daytime, Handwritten

Every block uses the live PHYSICS method:

`physical system -> calculus relationship -> symbolic construction -> physical meaning -> 1–2 applications -> light cold check`

| ID | Semester-wide calculus connection | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Motion chain and 2D components: `x(t) -> v(t) -> a(t)`, integration back with initial conditions, x/y sharing time | Derive and explain one 2D motion pair from a sketch |
| P2 | Circular motion: direction change, radial acceleration, tangential acceleration | Work the open circular-motion rep and explain what each derivative changes physically |
| P3 | Newton's second law as a differential relationship: `F_net = dp/dt`, reducing to `F = ma` when mass is constant | Construct `F_net = dp/dt` from a real force system, show the constant-mass reduction to `F = ma`, and connect each term to the mechanism |
| P4 | Work and power: force accumulated over distance; energy changing over time | Interpret one `F-x` area and one power derivative |
| P5 | Impulse and momentum: force accumulated over time | Interpret one `F-t` area and connect it to momentum change |
| P6 | Rotation and angular momentum: angular derivatives, torque, moment of inertia, `tau = dL/dt` | Map each linear quantity to its rotational counterpart; use the right-hand rule physically |
| P7 | Springs and oscillations: second derivatives and sinusoidal behavior — SHM is the calculus connection; waves are light preview only (no wave calculus-link page exists yet, by design) | Explain why acceleration points back toward equilibrium, plus a one-sentence preview of how oscillation becomes a wave |
| P8 | Full-map retrieval | Given eight physical situations, identify the calculus operation and governing relationship without notes; score 2 points per situation (operation + relationship, 16-point map) and mark every miss for Weeks C–D |

P4, P6, and P7 each span two calculus-link pages. Cap each at one pass of
the five-part structure — relationship map, one physical system, one
symbolic construction, one changed-parameter transfer, one explain-back —
and let depth roll to Weeks C–D rather than stretching the block.

This is breadth-first exposure, not eight stage passes. Stage 4 remains the
learner-truth frontier until its own gate moves.
