## 3. The correction worth remembering

**Flag #104 was raised on three instances and git supported one.** Chris asked for the proof, and
the proof refuted the framing:

- **Instance ②** — the Week 1 plan was committed **Aug 21 12:12**; its owner changed **Aug 22
  10:57**, twenty-two hours later. **The plan was correct when written.** A propagation failure,
  not a reading failure.
- **Instance ③** — the session that wrote the 17-week plan **provably read the pathway**: the same
  commit adds a fifth syllabus defect the pathway had missed and cross-links the new plan into it.

**Chris's own hypothesis — concurrent windows — was tested and is half right.** Not a race
(sequential sessions 16–22 h apart, one author, no overlapping writes), but correct one level
down: different sessions with no shared context, and no scannable way to rank two plans.

**Root cause as finally stated:** subordination was declared in body prose, in a *Mission and
authority* paragraph, where a page-assembling session does not look. **Fixed with a banner under
the H1 of all three 17-week plans** — no schema change, no new instrument, and the original
counter-move withdrawn as a tax on every future session for a failure that mostly did not happen.

**The transferable lesson is the method, not the finding: challenging a flag's evidence changed
its priority, its root cause, and its fix.**
