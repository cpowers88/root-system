# Review 01 — David Gasquez's Handbook

## Source

- Repository: [davidgasquez/handbook](https://github.com/davidgasquez/handbook)
- Primary learning page: [Learning.md](https://github.com/davidgasquez/handbook/blob/main/Learning.md)
- Accessed: 2026-08-07

## Why it was selected

This is the closest match to `.ROOT`'s education mission. It treats a public
handbook as living knowledge and gives unusually explicit guidance for turning
information into understanding.

## What the system does well

- Starts with the whole system or base model before details, reducing isolated
  fact collection.
- Treats learning as compression: a learner should be able to reconstruct the
  idea from a smaller mental model.
- Uses explanation, analogy, examples, diagrams, and plain language as tests of
  understanding rather than as decoration.
- Makes gaps visible through Feynman-style explanation and iterative rewriting.
- Mixes guided instruction with unguided construction and practice.
- Connects teaching to doing: demonstrate, let the learner perform, then tighten
  feedback.

## What `.ROOT V2` should take

1. Give every substantial learning unit a progression:
   `map -> explanation -> worked example -> learner attempt -> proof -> transfer`.
2. Require at least one physical, workflow, or machinery analogy for new
   abstract concepts, consistent with current `.ROOT` education rules.
3. Store a learner's explanation and failed attempt as evidence of the current
   knowledge gap, not just the final polished note.
4. Make the next practice action computable from the gap.

## What not to copy

The handbook is strong as a teaching philosophy but does not visibly provide
the operational controls `.ROOT` needs: one active-state record, evidence
provenance, business opportunity routing, validation gates, or measurable
value realization. Treat it as the teaching layer, not the operating system.

## Root-specific inference

`.ROOT` already contains large amounts of explanation. The V2 improvement is
not “more educational pages”; it is a repeatable learning transaction that
ends in learner proof and a transfer task tied to school, technology, or an SMB
workflow.
