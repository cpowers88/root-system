## Purpose

Test whether a parent-governed recommendation packet can help a caregiver choose
one worthwhile, age-appropriate media interaction that transfers into real-world
learning without creating another large curation task.

This protocol belongs to CASTLE opportunity `OPP-20260730-01`. It tests content
selection and caregiver usability only. It does not prove product demand,
learning durability, parental time savings, child safety at scale, or business
viability.

## Parent Input

Use only:

- age band, not birth date;
- one current interest or concept;
- parent-approved platform, provider, source, or specific link;
- maximum co-viewing time;
- available household objects for an off-screen activity.

Do not store:

- child name, image, voice, account, location, school, health information,
  behavior profile, browsing history, or persistent child identifier.

## Hard Gates

Reject or replace a candidate if any answer is no:

1. Is the age band explicit and appropriate?
2. Can the parent preview or co-view the complete item?
3. Is it free of open-web browsing, autoplay dependency, advertising, and
   manipulative purchase prompts during the test?
4. Does it teach one concrete idea rather than provide undirected stimulation?
5. Can the idea be connected to a real object, action, sound, word, or
   caregiver interaction immediately afterward?
6. Is the duration bounded before viewing begins?
7. Does the plan preserve sleep, movement, meals, face-to-face interaction, and
   unstructured play?

For the 18–24-month age band, caregiver co-viewing is mandatory for this test.
Do not recommend unattended “educational entertainment” as a learning trial.

## Candidate Score

After every hard gate passes, score each item from 0–2:

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Developmental fit | unclear/wrong | broadly suitable | clearly suitable for the age band |
| Pace and distraction | rapid/cluttered | mixed | slow, simple, focused |
| Active response | passive watching | occasional prompt | repeated pointing, movement, imitation, or vocal response |
| Real-world transfer | none | weak connection | direct object/action activity |
| Parent usability | burdensome | manageable | previewable and runnable in minutes |

Recommendation threshold: **8/10 with every hard gate passed**. A lower score
may be discussed but is not labeled recommended.

## 18–24-Month Content Lens

Prefer:

- familiar objects and people;
- naming and receptive-language prompts;
- animal sounds, body parts, simple actions, shape and color language;
- cause and effect;
- stacking, rolling, filling, emptying, matching, sorting, and movement;
- songs with gestures;
- repetition with pauses that allow a response;
- a short real-world activity immediately afterward.

Do not treat formal coding, abstract mathematics, rapid sensory stimulation, or
long passive viewing as age-appropriate STEAM merely because the publisher uses
educational language. At this age, STEAM foundations are observation, language,
cause and effect, spatial play, prediction, comparison, and exploration.

## Saturday Rep

Timebox: one item, maximum 10 minutes.

1. Chris supplies a parent-approved link or names an approved provider and one
   concept.
2. AI screens the item using the hard gates and score.
3. Chris co-views while the child is on his lap and uses the three prompts.
4. The screen stops at the predetermined endpoint.
5. Chris runs the immediate off-screen transfer activity.
6. Record only aggregate observations in the recommendation packet.

Success for this rep:

- the packet took no more than five minutes of parent setup after content
  selection;
- at least one observable response occurred during co-viewing;
- at least one related response was attempted off-screen;
- stopping did not require extending the content;
- Chris judges whether the packet reduced or increased his curation burden.

One success remains a usability signal, not a learning or market claim.

## Review

After each rep:

- **KEEP** only the parts that produced observable use;
- **MODIFY** the content lens or prompts after a classified miss;
- **REJECT** content that fails a hard gate or produces only passive fixation;
- do not automate recommendations until repeated manual packets establish which
  inputs, gates, outputs, and observations are actually necessary.
