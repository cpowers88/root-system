## 1. Contract Decision

Research and, only after the gates below pass, incrementally build a transparent
advisory capability that converts permitted evidence into traceable findings and
proposed next tests. The engine organizes evidence for human judgment; it does not
make consequential decisions or take action.

This is a supporting diagnostic capability for the current Advisor-Builder vehicle,
not a new revenue lane, product commitment, or substitute for observing real work.

### Two-horizon contract

**Permanent mission:** `.ROOT` converts information, learning, judgment, and
technical capability into useful output, compounding business assets, profit, and
eventual self-sufficient family financial security.

**V1 mission:** improve one bounded opportunity decision at a time while Chris has
limited capacity from school and family. V1 does not choose a career, sector, or
business from public data alone. It helps Chris reject weak avenues early, identify
the smallest reachable proof, and learn which skills or tools real use justifies.

The engine therefore strengthens this permanent flow:

```text
unfamiliar problem
→ find what costs time, money, quality, or opportunity
→ structure and validate the evidence
→ identify the decisive exception or constraint
→ recommend the smallest justified response
→ test it in real use
→ measure the result
→ harvest reusable capability
```

## 3. Decision the Engine Supports

The engine supports this decision:

> Given a bounded opportunity, a named user or workflow, permitted evidence, and
> Chris's current capacity, is there enough evidence to reject it, investigate it,
> or run one smallest real-world test—and which capability, if any, should receive
> more investment next?

For an operational dataset, the narrower decision remains:

> Which evidence-backed exception or pattern deserves Chris's attention next, what
> is still unknown, and what smallest test could determine whether real value
> exists?

The engine compares opportunities only after each has a named user, problem,
economic mechanism, reachable proof, evidence quality, and displacement cost. It
must not rank vague sectors, skills, or business ideas as though scores were market
proof.

It must distinguish:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Observation | A source-grounded fact about a record or population |
| Finding | A calculated pattern or exception with provenance and limitations |
| Recommendation | A proposed next test or response based on a finding |
| Decision | Chris's disposition of the recommendation |
| Action | Work explicitly authorized after the decision |
| Outcome | What actually happened after authorized use |
| Learning | A rule, threshold, method, or assumption changed by the outcome |

No earlier item may be presented as a later one.

## 4. Evidence-to-Value Interface

Structured information becomes potential value only when it reduces uncertainty
around a real decision. It becomes verified value only when use produces an
observed improvement, better decision, saved time, reduced error/risk, qualified
demand, reuse, or received revenue.

Before any value-proof claim, the engine must pass two analytical checks:

1. **Calculation validation** — the result is reproducible from cited records,
   rules, thresholds, and an explicit analytical date.
2. **Interpretation check** — Chris confirms that the finding says only what the
   available evidence supports, with uncertainty and alternative explanations
   visible.

These are pre-proof checks, not a competing proof ladder. Evidence then advances
only through the canonical ladder in
`01-NORTH_STAR\Goals & Milestones\value_production_goal.md`:

```text
capability proof → use proof → problem proof → outcome proof
→ demand proof → revenue proof → repeatability proof
```

Research, a score, a report, public data, or a working program cannot independently
claim use, problem, outcome, demand, revenue, or repeatability proof.

For Chris's objective, personally received income is the gold-standard economic
proof. Earlier ladder stages remain necessary filters and learning signals, but
they are not interchangeable with income. Conservative defaults apply: uncertain
economics, unreachable users, weak evidence, or excessive displacement lower an
opportunity's priority rather than creating another venture to investigate.

## 5. Strategy and Economic Mechanism

The engine serves the current Advisor-Builder method:

```text
observe → diagnose → engineer smallest response → deploy/use
→ train/retain when justified → harvest reusable capability
```

Its proposed economic mechanism is:

> Improve the speed, consistency, traceability, and reuse of an observation-based
> operational diagnosis so a workflow owner can understand where time, money,
> quality, risk, or opportunity may be leaking and decide what to test next.

The project potentially generates evidence for:

- **S-01** — owners value a concise, evidence-backed waste diagnosis;
- **S-05** — a completed engagement can produce a reusable compounding asset.

KSU and public-data work cannot prove either assumption. S-01 requires a qualified
workflow owner recognizing the finding/problem or showing demand. S-05 requires
reuse or validated deployment learning from real work.

The engine also exercises existing capability gaps in reliable data handling,
workflow diagnosis, decision communication, and later integration. It does not open
a new curriculum, market, offer, or product.

Sector selection is evidence-led and access-first. The next sector earns attention
through a reachable person, an observable workflow, a recognized costly problem,
and willingness to test or pay—not because public information makes the sector
look large or interesting.

## 6. V1 Proof Environments and Claim Ceilings

### KSU Academic Tracker

Purpose: deterministic internal validation against an existing SQLite structure
and, only when paired with declared learning-method trials and outcome measures,
evidence about which study or teaching approaches help Chris learn.

Permitted claim ceiling:

- the engine can read a database without changing it;
- detect declared data-quality and due-state exceptions;
- trace a finding to its source row;
- produce a reviewable decision packet.
- compare declared learning-method trials when the method, baseline, result, and
  confounders are recorded.

Not permitted:

- calling sample records current academic truth;
- inferring Chris's best teaching method from tracker records alone;
- calling the tracker a business workflow;
- claiming market demand or economic value.

### NYC 311

Purpose: transfer test against foreign, official, frequently updated operational
data with identifiable cases, dates, categories, agency ownership, and resolution
information. It may reveal operational problem patterns that exercise economically
relevant diagnostic skills; it cannot determine the market value of those skills
without buyer, labor-market, spending, demand, or real-workflow evidence.

Permitted claim ceiling:

- the adapter works on an external operational schema;
- calculations reveal workload, aging, cycle-time, concentration, or data-quality
  patterns inside the selected sample;
- the engine clearly distinguishes evidence from missing context.

Not permitted:

- declaring an agency ineffective from volume alone;
- assuming due dates or resolution codes have meanings not established by metadata;
- converting an operational proxy into dollars;
- ranking skills or sectors by economic value from 311 records alone;
- claiming a sellable service.

### Future Atlanta or Other Market

Purpose: test geographic relevance and adapter portability.

Entry condition: an official source with enough documentation and equivalent
operational fields to support a bounded decision question. Local relevance does not
lower the evidence standard.

### Required value sequence

The project may advance only in this order:

```text
internal calculation validation
→ foreign public-data transfer
→ permitted real workflow observation
→ engine-assisted finding
→ workflow-owner problem validation
→ bounded response/test
→ measured outcome
```

KSU and NYC can prove capability and transfer. They cannot replace the real-workflow
observation required by the value-production goal. No platform, dashboard, or
general product is built in search of a workflow.

## 7. Human Disposition Contract

The engine may rank attention. Chris assigns the final disposition:

| Disposition | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `test_next` | Evidence supports one bounded next test |
| `implement` | Prior test evidence supports authorized implementation |
| `collect_more_data` | A material missing fact prevents a sound test or action |
| `monitor` | Valid signal, no current action; named trigger/check date required |
| `save_for_later` | Potentially useful but displaced or phase-inappropriate |
| `reject` | Evidence, fit, economics, or risk does not justify continuation |

Every disposition records Chris's rank, reason, review date, and next check when
applicable. The engine never upgrades `test_next` to `implement`.

## 8. Decision Policy

For every finding:

1. Show the source and calculation.
2. Show missing or contradictory evidence.
3. Separate measured consequence, proxy consequence, and unknown consequence.
4. Apply the Recommendation Ladder from eliminate through custom build.
5. Recommend the cheapest rung supported by evidence.
6. State the smallest test, success signal, stop rule, owner, and review trigger.
7. Return the recommendation to Chris.

Unknown consequence remains unknown. It may lower priority or require more data; it
may not be filled with an invented estimate.

## 12. Operation Stop Conditions

Stop and return to research if:

- the decision question is absent;
- source semantics cannot support the intended calculation;
- validation falls below the Gate B threshold;
- economic consequence is necessary but unavailable;
- a simpler non-code method answers the question;
- implementation would require private data or credentials not yet authorized;
- project work displaces protected school work without Chris's explicit choice;
- a generated component is being treated as learner mastery.

## 13. Engine Continuation and Kill Criteria

After each approved slice, continue only when the evidence shows the engine still
earns its complexity.

### Continue when

- the same input, configuration, rule versions, and analytical date produce
  reproducible findings;
- Chris can trace every important finding to records and calculations;
- transfer to the second source requires mapping rather than core redesign;
- the decision packet improves a real review decision or reduces review effort;
- one reachable, permitted workflow exists for the next value-proof step; and
- the expected value of the next slice exceeds its displacement and maintenance
  cost.

### Stop, simplify, or redesign when

- a spreadsheet, checklist, or manual review produces equal value more simply;
- source-adapter work dominates useful analysis;
- findings are technically correct but operationally useless;
- no reachable workflow owner can validate the problem;
- the engine repeatedly recommends `collect_more_data` without improving the
  decision;
- a different implementation rung is cheaper and sufficiently reliable;
- continued work threatens protected school capacity without evidence strong enough
  to justify Chris's explicit tradeoff; or
- portability requires a general framework before the first real workflow use.

A stopped engine may still return a reusable checklist, schema, validation method,
or documented negative result. Stopping is a valid measured outcome.
