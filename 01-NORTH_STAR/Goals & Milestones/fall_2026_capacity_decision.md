## Codex — Independent Position

### Direct conclusion

Chris appears capable of earning A grades in this schedule; the unresolved
question is whether his life can reliably supply the hours. My honest planning
number is **56 hours of strict school time in a normal week**, with the ability
to absorb **62–65 hours in collision weeks**. “Strict school time” here includes
driving, parking/walking, class, work performed during campus gaps, homework,
reading, retrieval practice, projects, and exam preparation. It does not
include `.ROOT` maintenance that is not directly completing schoolwork.

For a **B-floor completion plan**, the defensible range is approximately
**49–55 hours/week**. For an **all-A plan**, use **52–60 hours/week**, centered
on 56, rather than planning from the best case. This is not a prediction that
every week will consume 56 hours. It is the capacity that should be reserved
so that a hard week does not have to borrow from sleep, recovery, or family.

### Reconstructed time model

| Component | B-floor plan | All-A plan | Basis |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Scheduled class/contact | ~12.5 hr | ~12.5 hr | Current registration; ENGR asynchronous work is counted below |
| Outside academic work | ~25–31 hr | ~29–36 hr | Credit-hour baseline adjusted upward for PHYS, CSE lab, and TCOM projects |
| Minimum driving + parking/walking | ~7 hr | ~7 hr | Five round trips at 30 minutes each way, plus a conservative campus-access buffer |
| Weekly strict-school footprint | **~49–55 hr** | **~52–60 hr** | Normal-week planning range |
| Collision-week reserve | **~58–62 hr** | **~62–65 hr** | Exams, debugging, and TCOM group-report deadlines overlapping |

The federal credit-hour baseline is approximately one instructional hour plus
at least two out-of-class hours per credit each week. Thirteen credits therefore
start near **39 academic hours/week before commuting**. That baseline is not an
A-grade estimate, but it is an important lower-bound check. It means the earlier
“~35 hours in a good week” total is too low as a semester planning number when
travel is included.

The unusual split-day schedule matters as much as the credits. If Chris stays
on campus between classes, the Monday-through-Friday campus-bound footprint is
approximately **41–42 hours/week**. About 22 of those hours are gaps rather than
class or travel. After meals, transitions, and normal loss of focus, a realistic
goal is to convert **15–17 gap hours** into real coursework. That leaves roughly
**12–20 additional focused hours at home or on the weekend** for an A-grade
target. Driving home during the long gaps generally does not create capacity;
it trades campus waiting for additional travel and transition cost.

### Course-by-course outside-work allowance for an A target

- **PHYS 2211:** 8–10 hours normally; 10–12 near exams. Calculus I and II are
  completed, so this is not a missing-prerequisite problem. The live gap is
  translating calculus and vectors into mechanics quickly and reliably.
- **CSE 1321 lecture:** 5–6 hours. Existing Python progress is a meaningful
  head start, but timed recall and independent problem construction are not yet
  fully automatic.
- **CSE 1321L:** 5–7 hours. This must remain separate from the lecture estimate:
  debugging, assignments, and closed-book assessments create their own load,
  and the syllabus prohibits AI assistance on submitted work.
- **TCOM 2010:** 5–7 hours on average; 8–10 in report/project weeks. The group
  work reduces control over timing and can create coordination overhead even
  when the writing itself is manageable.
- **ECON 1000:** 2–3 hours. It is the lightest known course, but its no-makeup
  structure makes deadline reliability important.
- **ENGR 1000:** 2–3 hours provisionally. This remains an estimate until the
  exact Fall syllabus is available.

These allowances total **27–36 outside hours**. The bottom of that range is
possible when prior knowledge converts efficiently; the top is the honest
planning case when unfamiliar physics, debugging, or group work resists the
first attempt.

### Material policy correction

KSU's current Industrial and Systems Engineering page states that Engineering
Standing requires a **minimum 2.7 GPA in designated math, science, and
engineering courses**. It does not publicly state that every Fall course must
earn a B. A B average (3.0) creates sensible margin above 2.7, but one C does
not necessarily defeat Engineering Standing if the designated-course GPA still
meets the rule. The exact application spreadsheet controls which courses enter
that calculation and should be confirmed with the advisor.

The stronger “B or better in every course plus 100% completion” rule is
currently recorded **per Chris as a scholarship condition**; I found no local
copy naming the scholarship or reproducing its award terms. Until that document
is obtained, it should remain a high-consequence working constraint, not a
verified KSU-wide rule. KSU's ordinary financial-aid SAP rule is materially
different (2.0 undergraduate GPA and 67% completion), so the scholarship must
be governed by a separate award-specific condition if Chris's recollection is
correct.

### Decision threshold

My recommendation is **do not quit on capability grounds, and do not commit on
hope**. Commit to the 13 credits only if a real weekly calendar—built around
family, recovery, sleep, and unavoidable appointments—can demonstrate:

1. **56 reserved school hours in a normal week**, including travel;
2. **62–65 available hours in identified collision weeks** without sacrificing
   protected minimums;
3. at least **15 productive campus-gap hours** rather than assuming all 22 gap
   hours will perform like desk time; and
4. a named contingency for a two-day disruption that does not require an
   all-nighter or abandoning another course.

If the calendar can protect **52 hours but not 56**, completing every course
with B-or-better may still be plausible, but an all-A expectation is not an
honest operating target. If it cannot reliably protect **about 50 hours**, the
current five-day, 13-credit schedule is not a responsible scholarship-safe
load regardless of aptitude. That is the point at which the schedule—not the
degree or Chris's ability—should be reconsidered before Drop/Add closes.
