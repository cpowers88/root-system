# OBSERVATION ONE

July 14, 2026
Small to lower end Medium size construction companies
Theory from past experience

**What I observed:**
Phone call comes in, estimator, likely owner in smaller companies, hand writes a synopsis of task and sets appointment to go to job site personally for existing construction or starts a pen and paper estimate process either based off job experience or just what is learned in observations coming up in the ranks.

**Where the friction is:**
The pricing for the construction industry is all over the place partly due to this and the lesser experienced folks claiming they know what they are doing and charging less making a mess and then clients call a real contractor or get turned off contractors completely.

**The root cause:**
There is no real course or book for the industry making a standard that is trusted to set prices, prices per county fluctuate depending on where you are in the country or the world. It is a very cut throat industry and folks are always worried about losing jobs to people under pricing them keeping cost down for homeowners, sort of like the rest of industry used to be run.

**What state lives where:**
The business usually only stores this information on pen and paper, sometimes in a crm type application or quick books is a very common one.

**What feedback exists:**
They do not know until they get paid for job, getting the job isn't enough in this market as the "unknowns" come up and they are always a fight to get the homeowner to pay for and the business owner usually feels ashamed to bring them up in fear of bad customer feedback online or in the community.

**What breaks if I remove this:**
If you removed the owner from most of these companies they just don't exist, the owners of these small business' work 16 hours a day almost everyday, on the jobsite doing the work, taking calls, writing emails, doing estimates, etc. and most with pen and paper.

**Potential solution:**
We could unify the pricing process and come up with an easy to use and information capturing system to ease the time taken to do estimates, make an intake application on a website to set appointments for viewing jobs or getting new customers.

**Revenue potential:**
This varies, if we could make a working estimation software that doubles as a on-site information capturing system for pricing workflow and eventually design (interior, structural, and architectural) the app is almost priceless, it would become industry standard after proven, we could charge 500 a month in testing stages after running the application with a couple test friends still in the business that would kill for something like this, and 1000-5000 dollars a month isn't out of the realm of possibilities, I see companies charging for leads and producing no work if we could help with all of the above for the construction industry we instantly make a huge profit.

**Follow-up action:**
I speak to friends in this section of the business all the time, but I can reach out to some Project managers of larger companies and see if they can tell me where they think most of their time is "wasted". My guess would be time on decisions for the efficient ones and keeping more organized notes for those that are not, there is lots of in my head or pen and paper or worse even in very large construction companies, they often have two project managers on-site one that knows how to deal with the guys, usually a former carpenter, and one that does all the computer and paper work and he is usually just a college guy that don't even understand how to build and honestly IMO doesn't belong on the job sometimes.

## Amendment Follow-up Questions

- What exact information is captured on the first call—and what is missed? The first call in this industry is usually just a name, what the customer thinks the scope of the work is, adress and meeting time to review the job in person (this is usually a free estimate).
- Where is it recorded, and is it re-entered later? This is recorded on whatever the contractor can reach at the time, sometimes a piece of a 2 x 4, these owners are usually working when they get these calls, can't afford a secratary and don't know a better way that is easy enough to be available at all times and not easily broken.
- How long from first contact to estimate sent? This depends on meeting time, but you try the fastest turn around possible as they are obviously calling other contractors to get pricing.
- Who reviews or approves pricing? This like most jobs is on the owner of these small business' who has little to no experience putting prices on tasks at all nevermind in a setting where anything can go wrong and it is on you to fix it, it is nerve wrecking and folks still call me to kick quotes around before sending them, and I haven't priced a job to actually work in 4 years now.
- What commonly causes an estimate to be wrong? The unknowns are the biggest thing, then after that the owners fear of feeling like he insults the customer, lots of these contractors will take a money loss if they think asking for what they deserve will make the customer unhappy, not to mention all of them don't like to ask for money on unknown things that come up with every job, even new construction.
- How are job unknowns/change orders documented, approved, and billed? In smaller business' is usually just a phone call to the owner of the property and a simple ok over the phone without any documentaion ever taking place.
- How often do completed jobs get paid less or later than expected? This happens on almsot every job, late pay is very common probably in the 80-90th% of jobs you need to wait for pay. Less money than expected is another thing that happens very often, home owners feel like after a job is complete is the time to negotiate money and the contractor is always the one taking the financial hit, often as court is just either too expesive to even pursue, so not worth it, or they are just too busy and defeated to even try and recoop the lost or cheated wages for task completed.

  1. What did you discover, and how did you capture it?
  2. How did you explain scope and price to the customer?
  3. What proof of approval existed?
  4. Did work proceed before approval?
  5. How was it invoiced and collected?
  6. Where did time, money, or trust leak?
