# Audit Sales Call Script

The discovery-to-close conversation from the [[sales-system|Sales System]], expanded to a working script. 25–35 minutes. Goal: **an audit sold or a clear no** — not "sending some info." Rehearse out loud until the questions come without reading.

---

## Before the Call (5 min prep)
- [ ] Their website, reviews, team size, visible tools (booking widget? web forms?)
- [ ] If they have a public intake channel: submit a test inquiry the day before and note the response time — ammunition
- [ ] Have your calendar and audit one-pager ready to send while still on the call

## 1. Frame (2 min)
> Thanks for the time, [Name]. Here's how I'd suggest using it: I'll ask about how work flows through [Company] — where things pile up, what's costing you time. If it sounds like there's real money on the table, I'll explain how my audit works and you can decide if it's worth it. If not, I'll tell you that too. Fair?

*Sets you as diagnostician, not pitcher. Get the "fair."*

## 2. Discovery (15–20 min) — listen 80%, write down their exact words

**The flow:** "Walk me through what happens from a customer first contacting you to money in the bank."
**The pile-up:** "Where does work get stuck? What's chronically late?"
**The person:** "Who spends the most time on repetitive computer work? Doing what?"
**The leak:** "When a lead comes in and everyone's busy — honestly, what happens to it?"
**The wand:** "If one operational headache could disappear tomorrow, which one?"
**Their number:** "What do you think that's costing you a year?" *(Always get THEIR estimate first — the audit will usually prove it's bigger, and their own number anchors the close.)*
**The dig (for each pain):** How often? How long per instance? Who's involved? What breaks?

## 3. Mirror (3 min) — where credibility is won
> Let me play back what I heard: [pain 1] is eating roughly [their numbers]; [pain 2] means [consequence they implied but didn't say]; and the thing that worries you most is [the wand answer]. Did I get that right?

*Sharper than they said it. If yes: they've just heard themselves describe a problem worth money. If corrected: even better — they're now co-diagnosing.*

## 4. Bridge to the Audit (3 min)
> These are exactly the leaks my audit quantifies. Over two weeks I map how [Company] actually runs — I interview you and [the person they named], watch the real workflow, and put a conservative dollar figure on every leak. You get a written report: every problem priced, ranked by payback, with a fix-list you could hand to anyone — me or someone else. Most audits find $[50–200K]/yr; yours sounds like [reference their own estimate]. The audit is $[X], takes two weeks, and the findings are yours regardless.

## 5. Close (1 min, then silence)
> I have capacity to start [specific date]. The next step is a short agreement and a 20-minute intake form. Want me to send those over today?

**Then stop talking.** The silence is the close.

## 6. Objection Handling

**"I need to think about it."**
> Completely fair. So I can be useful — what specifically would you want to be sure of? *(Surface the real objection; answer that one.)*

**"That's expensive."**
> Compared to what it's examining, it's small: you estimated [pain] costs you $[their number]/yr — the audit is [X]% of that, and it's the only step here with a fixed price. The expensive thing is another year of the current process.

**"Can you just fix it without the audit?"**
> I could guess, and guessing is how businesses end up with software nobody uses. The audit is why my fixes work: we'll know exactly what the problem costs and what fixing it is worth before you spend a real dollar. It also means you never buy a fix you don't need.

**"We tried automation/software before and it flopped."**
> That's the most common story I hear, and it's usually the same cause: a tool bought before anyone diagnosed the workflow. That failure is evidence *for* auditing first, not against it. What did it flop on? *(Their answer becomes discovery material.)*

**"Send me some information."**
> Happy to — I'll send the one-pager and a sample report today. In my experience though, "info" doesn't answer the real question, which is whether there's money on YOUR table. Can we pencil [date] and you cancel if the one-pager doesn't land?

## 7. After the Call (same day, always)
- [ ] Log verbatim pains + their cost estimate in the CRM
- [ ] Send recap email: their 3 pains in their words, audit price/timeline, agreement + intake link
- [ ] If no close: schedule the follow-up NOW (task in CRM, +4 days)
- [ ] Update the script — note which question opened them up and which fell flat

---

*Disqualify cheerfully:* if they fail the [[market-map|Market Map]] profile or the [[north-star-alignment|alignment filter]] — no budget, no decision authority, "just make us an AI strategy" — say so kindly and ask who they know that fits. A fast no protects the [[financial-model|funnel math]].
