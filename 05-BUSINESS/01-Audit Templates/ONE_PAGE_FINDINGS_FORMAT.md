# ONE_PAGE_FINDINGS_FORMAT.md — The Owner-Facing One-Pager
### NORTH_STAR.md "built before March 2027" deliverable: "a one-page findings format a business owner can act on"
### Status: SKELETON v1 (July 5, 2026) — test on practice audits, refine
### Rule: ONE page. If it doesn't fit, it isn't ready.

---

## Why One Page

The full audit report proves the work. The one-pager gets read.
A business owner decides in 90 seconds whether you saw something real.
Numbers first, jargon never, and at least one recommendation that
costs them nothing — that's what makes the advice trustworthy.

---

## THE FORMAT

```
[BUSINESS NAME] — Operations Findings
Chris Powers | [date] | Based on [X hours] of direct observation

WHAT I WATCHED
One sentence: which operation, which day, what I followed.

THE HEADLINE
The single biggest leak, in money-per-week terms.
"Your crew loses ~6 hours/week waiting on material decisions
that could be answered from a shared job sheet. At your loaded
rate that is roughly $___/month."

THREE FINDINGS (ranked by $ impact — no more than three)
1. [WHAT I SAW — one sentence, concrete, with the number]
   → COSTS YOU: [hours/week or $/month]
   → THE FIX: [one sentence — start with the free fix if one exists]

2. [same shape]

3. [same shape]

WHAT I'D DO FIRST (this week, costs nothing)
The single highest-leverage free action. Eliminate or simplify —
never "buy something" as the first move. This line is what earns
the second conversation.

WHAT IT'S WORTH
Fixing items 1–3: roughly [$X–$Y/month] recovered.
Doing nothing: that number, every month, indefinitely.

NEXT STEP (one line, low pressure)
"If you want the full breakdown — process map, all findings, and a
prioritized fix list — that's the full audit report. Happy to walk
you through it."
```

---

## WRITING RULES

- **Numbers over adjectives.** "6 hours/week" beats "significant waste."
  Every finding carries a number, even if it's an honest estimate —
  say "roughly" and stand behind the math.
- **Their words, not yours.** Use the vocabulary you heard on-site
  ("job sheet," "punch list"), never systems-engineering terms.
  VSM, TOC, and "feedback loop" stay in your notebook.
- **The free fix leads.** Recommendation Ladder order
  ([TECHNOLOGY_LIBRARY_STRATEGY.md](../../02-LIBRARY/08-AI-AUTOMATION/TECHNOLOGY_LIBRARY_STRATEGY.md)):
  if the first fix in every one-pager is
  "eliminate/simplify — costs nothing," you are the vendor-neutral
  advisor, not another software salesman.
- **No tool names on the one-pager** unless they already own the tool.
  Category language only ("a shared shift schedule" not "Jobber").
  Tool specifics belong in the full report.
- **One page. Ever.** Cut the third finding before you shrink the font.

## PRACTICE

Every practice observation (OBSERVATION_METHODOLOGY.md cadence)
produces one of these, even with invented numbers clearly marked.
The genre takes reps: month 1 output will read like an engineer;
the goal is that by first-client day it reads like a builder who
happens to know systems.
