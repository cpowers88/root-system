## 1. Do I have to read for every class every night?

**No — and the evening block was never going to cover it.** Here is the arithmetic, which
had not been written down:

| Course | Typical week | Estimated reading time |
|---|---|---:|
| **PHYS 2211** | 3 lectures × ~10–15 printed pages = **30–40 pages** of dense text | **2.5–3.5 h** |
| **TCOM 2010** | one section or short chapter, **15–25 pages** of accessible prose | 1–1.5 h |
| **CSE 1321** | **10–15 pages** of *Think Python* per module, modules run ~2 weeks | 0.75–1 h |
| **ECON 1000** | one chapter | 1–1.5 h |
| **ENGR 1000** | no textbook across BWB/BWC/BWF; read/open the current D2L module, directions, and quiz instructions | ~0.25–0.5 h provisional |
| | **Total** | **6–8 h/week** |

**The evening block carries 2.3–3.5 h of that** (20–30 min × 7 nights). So **3–5 hours of
reading a week has to land in the campus gaps** — of which you have **16.33** at the
realistic figure.

**Three consequences, and they are the whole answer:**

1. **Reading is a planned campus activity, not a leftover.** It cannot be "whatever is left
   after the desk work." Roughly **a third of your campus gap time is reading time.**
2. **The evening block is the anchor, not the plan.** Its job is the *one* reading that
   primes tomorrow's first class — which is why the rotation was rebuilt onto the timetable.
   It is one course a night by design, because the other four were read during the day.
3. **Reading is only ~25% of the outside-class load.** The other ~20 h/week is WebAssign,
   labs, drafting, and retrieval drills. Do not let a comfortable reading plan feel like a
   finished plan.

> **⚠️ This corrects `semester-workload-plan.md` §7 item 3**, which read *"the campus blocks
> are for the work that needs a desk; reading fits the evening brief."* At 6–8 h/week it
> does not fit. Corrected there on 2026-08-19.

**Per course, per week, how many nights?** PHYS 2, TCOM 2, CSE 2, ECON 1 in the evening
rotation — plus daytime. **No course needs reading every single night.** PHYS is the one
that comes closest, because it is the only course where **pre-class reading is graded**
(WebAssign/D2L reading quizzes plus unannounced in-class quizzes).

## 2. How to use this page

**Sunday, ~10 minutes:** read this week's block below alongside `semester-workload-plan.md`
§3's row for the same week. The workload page tells you what is **due**; this page tells you
what to **open**. Together they are the week's plan.

**Then place it:** put PHYS into the Monday/Tuesday/Wednesday gaps (it is the longest and
needs a desk), TCOM and ECON into the evening block, CSE alongside its module work.

**Three conventions that will otherwise cost you time:**

- **PHYS:** every page number is a **printed** page. `PDF page = printed + 30`. The "local"
  column gives the page inside the named split file directly — use that one.
- **TCOM:** **no offset, no printed numbers.** The number below *is* the number in your
  reader's page box. ⛔ Never open `2e_Word\` — its Ch 3 is the wrong chapter.
- **CSE:** every page is a **physical** PDF page of `raw\books\thinkpython.pdf`
  (printed + 22). Cite physical.

**PHYS runs one week ahead by design.** Its cell each week is *next* week's lectures. That
is not an error — it is the rule that converts every pre-exam week into pure retrieval
instead of first-pass learning.
