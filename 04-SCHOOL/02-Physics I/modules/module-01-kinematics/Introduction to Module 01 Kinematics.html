Have you ever watched a ball roll down a hill, thrown a frisbee across a field, or noticed how a car speeds up and slows down as it navigates a curve? These everyday experiences (though seemingly unrelated) are all governed by the exact same underlying physics: kinematics, the study of how objects move by describing their position, velocity, and acceleration without yet considering the forces causing that motion.

In this module, we will pull back the curtain on these everyday motions by establishing the core foundation of classical mechanics. You will learn that analyzing physical phenomena begins with fundamental measurements—length, time, and mass—and how breaking complex paths into vector components allows us to predict trajectories from a simple walking person to a satellite orbiting Earth.

The Framework of the Module
As we progress through Chapters 1, 2, 3, and 4, we will unpack the fundamental laws that govern physical movement:

  • Measurements and Dimensions: We will begin by exploring the three fundamental measurements of mechanics (length, time, and mass) along with unit conversions, dimensional analysis, and significant figures to establish a precise mathematical foundation.
  • One-Dimensional Kinematics: You will learn to describe and distinguish position, velocity, and acceleration along a single axis using motion diagrams, graphical relationships, calculus derivatives, and derived kinematic equations.
  • Vectors and Two-Dimensional Motion: We will transition from linear paths to multidimensional space by mastering vector components and coordinate systems. Finally, we will apply these tools to analyze 2D free-fall in projectile motion, investigate uniform circular motion, and calculate relative motion between moving reference frames.

Understanding kinematics is not just a prerequisite for mastering future topics in physics; it is your gateway to making the invisible rules of motion visible and building the foundation for tackling advanced physics concepts. Let's begin!
