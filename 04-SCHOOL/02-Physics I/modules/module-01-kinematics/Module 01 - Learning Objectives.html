Module Learning Objectives

After successful completion of this module, you will be able to:

  • 1.1 Describe the three fundamental measurements of mechanics: length (distance), time, and mass, and explain their significance in analyzing motion.
  • 1.2 Define and distinguish position, displacement, velocity, and acceleration in one- and two-dimensional motion.
  • 1.3 Use kinematic equations to develop models and solve problems involving motion with constant acceleration in one dimension.
  • 1.4 Apply calculus (differentiation and integration) to analyze motion and relate position, velocity, and acceleration mathematically.
  • 1.5 Solve problems involving projectile motion and uniform circular motion, interpreting the motion both quantitatively and conceptually.
  • 1.6 Interpret motion using kinematic graphs, diagrams, and vector decomposition, and recombine vector components to analyze two-dimensional motion.

Module Context & Significance

These module learning objectives establish a foundation in the principles of motion, providing the tools to describe, model, and predict how objects move in the physical world. By focusing on fundamental concepts such as displacement, velocity, acceleration, and the use of kinematic equations, students learn to represent motion both conceptually and mathematically, including the application of calculus for more precise analysis.

Through exercises involving projectile motion and uniform circular motion, students develop the ability to analyze complex motions in one and two dimensions. Interpreting motion using graphs, diagrams, and vector decomposition reinforces the connection between physical intuition and quantitative reasoning.

By mastering these concepts, students gain the analytical skills necessary to approach more advanced topics in mechanics, waves, and relativity, while developing a deeper understanding of the underlying patterns of motion in everyday phenomena.
