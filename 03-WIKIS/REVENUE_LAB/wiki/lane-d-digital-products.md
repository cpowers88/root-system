# Lane D — Digital Products (Contractor Templates / Estimating Assets)
### Evidence page | Scan Session 3 — July 14, 2026 | Status: EVIDENCE GATHERED, scored (evidence-only standard)

## The Hypothesis

The pain documented in `05-BUSINESS\02-Field Notes\observation_one.md` (pricing
chaos, pen-and-paper estimating, undocumented change orders) can be partially
served by cheap sellable digital assets — estimate worksheets, change-order
forms, pricing checklists — with no app build.

## Tier 1 Evidence (platform primary sources)

- **[Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing):** flat **10% + $0.50 per
  direct sale** (plus card processing ~2.9% + $0.30); **30% flat** when the sale
  comes through Gumroad's own Discover marketplace; no monthly fee; Gumroad is
  merchant of record (handles sales tax).
- **Etsy digital-download fees:** $0.20 listing + **6.5% transaction** +
  3% + $0.25 processing — on a $10 product Etsy takes ~$1.25
  ([fee schedule breakdown](https://craftybase.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-etsy-fees)).

**What this proves:** distribution is cheap, instant, and structurally solved.
A $25 change-order template kit nets ~$21 on Gumroad direct.

## What the Evidence Does NOT Establish (the honest gap)

- **Demand is unverified.** No Tier 1–2 source in this scan establishes that
  contractors buy estimating/change-order templates at meaningful volume, at
  what price, or through which channel. Marketplace listings exist, but sales
  volumes are not public.
- **Discoverability is the real bottleneck.** With no audience (Lane A) and no
  ad budget, a listed product has no traffic source. Etsy's internal search and
  Gumroad Discover (at 30%) are the only organic channels, and neither is
  quantified here.

## The Structural Finding — D Is Downstream, Not a Starting Lane

Two dependencies make D a sequencing decision rather than a candidate first lane:
1. **Lane B proves which templates matter.** Real paid estimating/change-order
   work for real contractors reveals exactly which artifacts they'd pay for —
   D's product spec falls out of B's deliverables at near-zero extra cost.
2. **Lane A solves D's traffic problem.** An audience of contractors/trades
   viewers is the only free distribution channel Chris could own.
