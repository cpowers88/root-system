# Community-Governed AI, Data Sovereignty, and Power Redistribution

**Summary**: AI governance can be tested by where it moves power, not only by
whether its builders claim broad benefit. The Epilogue contrasts centralized,
general-purpose development with community-controlled, task-specific systems
whose data is gathered by consent, governed through continuing stewardship, and
used for a defined local benefit. It then organizes counter-power along three
reinforcing axes - knowledge, resources, and influence - and argues that durable
alternatives require independent research, supply-chain transparency, labor and
data rights, community participation, and broad public education.

**Source**: `raw/empireofAIDreamsandNightmares.pdf` (Karen Hao, *Empire of AI:
Dreams and Nightmares in Sam Altman's OpenAI*, 2025), Epilogue, “How the Empire
Falls” (physical PDF pp. 387-398), reviewed as one complete chunk. The Epilogue
opening, final page, and Acknowledgments opening on p. 399 were visually verified.

**Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Source Posture

This is Hao's normative conclusion, supported by reported case studies rather
than a comparative evaluation of every governance model. The examples show that
different development arrangements are possible; they do not by themselves prove
that every small, open, nonprofit, community-led, or decentralized project is
safe, effective, representative, or scalable. Use the power test and design
questions as a governance framework, then verify each case and current policy
claim independently.

## The Primary Test: Where Does Power Move?

Terms such as “good,” “benefit,” and “humanity” allow institutions to define
success on behalf of everyone. Drawing on Ria Kalluri's framing, Hao proposes a
more observable question:

> Does the AI system consolidate power or redistribute it?

Apply that question across the full system:

- Who defines the problem and decides whether AI is appropriate?
- Who controls the data, model, infrastructure, and resulting knowledge?
- Who supplies labor, land, energy, water, culture, and creative work?
- Who receives the benefit, bears the failure, and can refuse participation?
- Who can inspect, contest, change, or stop the system after deployment?

A nominally beneficial output can still centralize the authority needed to
produce, interpret, and govern it.

## Te Hiku: Data Stewardship Before Model Building

Hao presents Te Hiku Media's te reo Maori speech-recognition project as the main
counterexample to extractive development. The organization began with a bounded
community need: transcribing archival and newly recorded speech to support
language revitalization. Its design commitments preceded model selection:

1. ask the community and elders whether the project should exist;
2. collect contributions only from people who understood and accepted the use;
3. design resources around needs identified by the community;
4. keep data under continuing stewardship rather than treating publication as
   irrevocable permission for any future use;
5. license data only for uses compatible with community values and consent;
6. own local compute where practical to reduce dependence on a platform owner;
7. build a small model for a specific job instead of maximizing generality.

The governing concept is *kaitiakitanga*, or guardianship. Data is not framed as
an ownerless input that becomes legitimate once scraped or released. It remains
embedded in relationships, duties, context, and the community's continuing
authority.

Hao reports that Te Hiku gathered about 310 hours of transcribed audio through a
community campaign and produced an early speech-recognition model with reported
86 percent accuracy using two GPUs and an open-source starting model. Those
figures are historical case claims, not a general performance guarantee. The
durable lesson is that a clearly bounded task plus high-quality, trusted data can
change the assumed need for indiscriminate scale.

## Community Control Is an Operating Model

“Community-led” is too vague unless authority appears in the workflow. A credible
implementation specifies:

- who legitimately represents affected groups and how disagreement is handled;
- the permitted purpose, prohibited uses, retention period, and revocation path;
- whether consent applies only at collection or continues as uses change;
- custody, access controls, licensing terms, and derivative-model rights;
- compensation and credit for data, annotation, expertise, and creative labor;
- performance measures defined with the people the system is meant to serve;
- incident response, appeal, exit, and model-retirement authority;
- dependencies that could quietly transfer control back to a cloud or model
  provider.

Participation without decision rights can legitimize a system while leaving its
power structure unchanged.

## Distributed Research and Worker Knowledge

The Epilogue adds two related counter-power mechanisms:

- DAIR is presented as a research institution designed to keep researchers
  embedded in affected communities, compensate knowledge-producing labor, and
  let communities refuse, interrogate, or reshape AI systems.
- The Data Workers' Inquiry treats data workers as researchers who formulate and
  document their own questions about platform labor rather than merely serving
  as subjects described by outsiders.

Worker organizing extends that principle from knowledge production to bargaining
power. Hao's cases from Kenya and Latin America show why local action alone is
often insufficient: AI supply chains cross jurisdictions, while platforms can
move contracts, tasks, and infrastructure when one community gains leverage.
Cross-border information sharing, common standards, and enforceable buyer-side
responsibility are therefore part of the governance system.

## Three Reinforcing Axes of Power

Hao's concluding model describes knowledge, resources, and influence as a loop:

`knowledge control -> influence -> resource accumulation -> stronger knowledge control`

### 1. Knowledge

Redistribution requires independent capacity to evaluate corporate models,
explore alternatives to dominant architectures, document local effects, and
conduct journalism and civil-society research. It also requires disclosure
sufficient to inspect training inputs, system behavior, technical dependencies,
and material infrastructure.

### 2. Resources

Data, creative work, annotation, compute, energy, water, land, and minerals are
governance inputs, not invisible externalities. Transparency must connect each
input to consent, compensation, environmental burden, contractual control, and
the people able to challenge its use. Labor protection must include direct data
workers and people whose work is captured or displaced through deployment.

### 3. Influence

Public understanding determines which claims receive trust and which futures seem
inevitable. Plain-language education about model mechanics, limitations, supply
chains, incentives, and developer fallibility reduces dependence on company
narratives and enables meaningful consent and oversight.

No axis works alone. Transparency without independent expertise can create data
without accountability; research without resources remains marginal; education
without decision rights produces informed spectators.

## Power-Redistribution Gate

Before approving or expanding an AI system, record:

1. the current distribution of knowledge, resources, and influence;
2. the expected shift on each axis and the evidence supporting it;
3. whose consent is required and what refusal changes;
4. which data and labor rights persist after collection or contracting;
5. what affected communities can inspect, decide, appeal, and stop;
6. how suppliers and infrastructure dependencies change practical control;
7. how independent evaluators obtain access and funding;
8. what benefits and burdens will be measured, by whom, and when;
9. how gains remain durable if a vendor, funder, government, or platform exits;
10. the review trigger for reducing, transferring, or ending the system.

If the project offers access or consultation while ownership, interpretation,
infrastructure, and exit rights remain centralized, it has not redistributed
power.

## Limits and Recency

The case details largely run through 2024 and may have changed. Verify current
organizational status, labor conditions, model performance, language-support
statistics, laws, and corporate disclosure requirements from primary or current
independent sources before operational use. The durable artifact is the
community-control and three-axis power framework.
