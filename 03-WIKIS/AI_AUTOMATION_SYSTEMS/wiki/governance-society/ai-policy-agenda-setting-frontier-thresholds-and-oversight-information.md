# AI Policy Agenda-Setting, Frontier Thresholds, and Oversight Information

**Summary**: AI firms can shape regulation before rules are written by defining
which risks count, which technical proxy measures them, and which experts receive
access to policymakers. A focus on future “frontier” systems and compute
thresholds can crowd out present labor, copyright, privacy, environmental, and
discrimination harms while advantaging incumbents already able to comply with a
licensing regime. Inside a firm, the same information asymmetry appears when a
mission board depends on the CEO and informal networks for safety, conflict, and
operational information.

**Source**: `raw/empireofAIDreamsandNightmares.pdf` (Karen Hao, *Empire of AI:
Dreams and Nightmares in Sam Altman's OpenAI*, 2025), Chapter 13, “The Two
Prophets” (physical PDF pp. 288-310), and Chapter 14, “Deliverance” (pp. 311-324),
reviewed as two complete connected chunks. Physical p. 325 is the Part IV
divider; Chapter 15 begins p. 326. Boundaries visually verified.

**Last updated**: 2026-07-16

## Source Posture

Hao's account combines public policy documents, testimony, interviews, internal
reporting, and a long disputed section about Altman's family. This page does not
adjudicate personal allegations. It retains only the governance-relevant mechanism:
company communications resources and executive narrative control can blur the
boundary between institutional and personal interests. Treat private motives and
disputed behavior as attributed reporting, and verify current policy independently.

## Agenda-Setting Happens Before Regulation

In 2023, Altman advocated a licensing agency, safety standards for dangerous
capabilities, and independent audits for models above a capability or compute
threshold. These proposals sound restrictive, but they also changed the policy
agenda:

- from existing harms to future catastrophic capabilities;
- from training-data transparency to compute measurement;
- from rules applying across deployed systems to licenses for a narrow frontier;
- from affected workers and creators to executives and specialist safety networks;
- from open scientific scrutiny toward controlled model access.

Agenda-setting power determines which evidence lawmakers request and which
problems become legible. The practical question is not only whether a proposal is
good, but what it displaces.

## Compute Is an Administrative Proxy, Not a Risk Measure

A 2023 policy paper proposed 10^26 floating-point operations as a possible
threshold for “frontier” models. Hao reports that the number was selected partly
because it appeared just above then-estimated GPT-4 training compute and carried
substantial caveats. It nevertheless propagated into the US AI executive order,
European policy, and California debate.

Compute is attractive because it is countable. It is weak as a general risk proxy:

- specialized small models can have dangerous capabilities;
- distillation can transfer capabilities into smaller systems;
- training data and architecture determine which capabilities emerge;
- more compute does not guarantee a particular capability;
- inference-time tools and access can change effective risk;
- a fixed number becomes stale as hardware and methods improve.

Thresholds may still support reporting or administrative triage, but they should
not substitute for capability-, use-, data-, and context-specific evaluation.

## Data Transparency Was the Crowded-Out Alternative

Training-data disclosure received less policy traction despite supporting several
immediate governance needs:

- copyright and creator consent;
- privacy and sensitive-data review;
- benchmark-contamination analysis;
- investigation of bias and harmful content;
- explanation of where specialized dangerous knowledge came from;
- independent assessment of environmental and labor inputs.

Data disclosure is not sufficient by itself and may require privacy/IP safeguards.
But without meaningful lineage, regulators must often accept vendor claims about
performance and risk.

## Closed Versus Open Is a Portfolio Decision

Restricting model weights can reduce some forms of misuse and proliferation. It
can also entrench dominant firms, limit independent research, weaken startups,
and reduce the ability to measure environmental and social impacts. Open models
have supported cross-border scientific collaboration and downstream innovation;
they can also distribute capabilities beyond the original developer's controls.

The decision should not be reduced to “open good” or “closed safe.” For each
release layer - paper, data, code, weights, API, product - assess:

1. specific capability and abuse pathway;
2. marginal harm created by additional access;
3. accountability and innovation lost through restriction;
4. whether materials, tools, or operational access are the real bottleneck;
5. conditions, monitoring, or staged access that change the tradeoff.

## Expertise Concentration Enables Policy Capture

Policymakers faced a field where frontier firms employed much of the relevant
talent and had large policy teams, while independent academic and civil-society
capacity had atrophied. Executives could meet lawmakers repeatedly; affected
artists and workers struggled for brief access. Industry-aligned concepts could
therefore become default policy language before scientific consensus existed.

A credible public process needs an expertise balance sheet: who testified, who
funded them, which affected groups were absent, which claims were independently
replicated, and which alternatives received equal analysis.

## Internal Oversight Has the Same Information Problem

Hao describes OpenAI's independent board losing members while the company's
capabilities, products, and partnerships expanded. Directors sought more regular
written reporting, employee access, safety information, and additional independent
expertise. They still relied heavily on the CEO and personal networks.

Reported warning signs included:

- a Microsoft GPT-4 rollout that bypassed the Deployment Safety Board and was
  not disclosed during a board safety review;
- prolonged difficulty appointing independent directors;
- attempts to remove or resist directors who posed conflicts or asked difficult
  questions;
- delayed discovery that the OpenAI Startup Fund was legally owned by Altman
  rather than OpenAI;
- significant gaps between executive accounts and reports arriving through
  informal channels.

The core failure is structural: an oversight body cannot check management if
management controls its agenda, evidence, employee access, conflicts register,
and flow of adverse information.

## Institutional and Personal Boundaries

Chapter 14 documents disputed and deeply sensitive allegations involving Altman's
family. Those allegations are outside this wiki's competence to resolve. The
institutional issue is narrower: Hao reports that OpenAI communications personnel
became involved in managing reporting about the CEO's personal affairs and in
delivering characterizations about a family member's mental health.

When executive reputation and company reputation are tightly coupled, staff,
legal resources, and information channels can be redirected toward the leader's
personal narrative. Governance should require explicit authorization, conflict
review, privacy protection, and board visibility whenever company resources are
used for a senior leader's personal matter.

## Policy and Oversight Gate

Before adopting an AI regulatory proposal or relying on a mission board, require:

1. a problem statement that lists present harms alongside future risks;
2. scientific justification, sensitivity analysis, and sunset/revision rules for
   every numerical threshold;
3. alternatives based on capability, data, use, access, and deployment context;
4. an incumbent-advantage and open-science impact assessment;
5. balanced participation from independent researchers, workers, creators,
   communities, small firms, and civil society;
6. direct board access to employees, safety bodies, incident records, contracts,
   conflicts, and partner protocol breaches;
7. routine written reporting and mandatory escalation of adverse information;
8. a boundary policy for use of company resources in executives' personal affairs.

## Limits and Recency

Policy texts, thresholds, laws, executive orders, board membership, litigation,
and company structures are highly time-sensitive. This page captures agenda and
information-control mechanisms from the reported period, not current legal or
corporate status. Verify any live claim from official and independent sources.
