# Glossary — TCOM 2010 Terms

Each definition is one exact meaning, per Term Anchoring in `HAT_EDUCATOR.md`. Use
the term in real work the same session it is introduced; a term never used is a
term not learned.

## Technical communication

Writing and speaking that helps a specific reader **do or decide something** in a
work context. Distinguished from academic writing by its purpose: use, not
demonstration of knowledge.

**Common confusion:** it is not "writing about technical subjects." A recipe is
technical communication; an essay about engineering is not.

## Audience analysis

Determining who will read a document, what they already know, and what they need
to do with it — before drafting.

**Anchor:** measuring the opening before cutting the board.

## Purpose

What the reader must be able to do after reading. Stated as an action, not a topic.

## Genre

A recognized document type carrying reader expectations about structure and
convention — memo, proposal, instructions, recommendation report.

**Common confusion:** genre is a *reader expectation*, not a template file. The
template exists because the expectation does.

## Executive summary

A standalone miniature of an entire report — problem, findings, recommendation —
written for a decision-maker who may read nothing else.

**Common confusion:** not an introduction. See `../concepts/technical-report-anatomy.md`.

## Transmittal

The short professional message that hands a completed document to whoever
commissioned it.

## Proposal

A document arguing that a specific problem should be solved a specific way, usually
seeking approval or resources.

**Why it matters beyond the course:** this is the client-facing document that sells
work. Directly reusable in the company.

## Technical description

A precise account of an object, system, or process — what it is, what it is made
of, how its parts relate.

## Instructions / procedure

A sequential document enabling a reader to perform a task. Success is measured by
whether they can, which is why the course tests them by **usability testing**.

## Usability testing

Watching a real person attempt a task using your document, and recording where they
fail. The document is the thing being tested, not the person.

**Anchor:** you are not grading the reader; the reader is grading your instructions.

## Document design

The visual arrangement of information — headings, white space, typography, lists,
graphics — treated as part of meaning rather than decoration.

## Peer review

Structured feedback on a draft against stated criteria. In this course it is graded
work in its own right, not an optional courtesy.

## Technical editing

Correcting grammar, style, consistency, and mechanics so the document does not
undermine its own credibility. Book Ch 8.

## Ethics in technical communication

The obligation not to mislead through selection, emphasis, omission, or visual
distortion — including in graphs. Week 1 material, and it is assessed.

## Reflective memo

A short memo in which the writer assesses their own process and results. Week 12,
and no ready template ships with the course.
