# Part A — File naming

## The pattern

```
Lastname_Section#_AssignmentName.docx      ← the syllabus's template
Powers_04_AssignmentName.docx              ← Chris's substitution
```

Section is **04**. Underscores between fields. `.docx` — Word.

**`Section#` is a placeholder. On the actual file it is `04`.** Copying the
template literally is a submitted-file error and costs the same as not knowing it.

## ⚠ The pattern does not generate the real filenames

**This is the whole test.** Every printed string breaks the pattern differently:

| Assignment | Exact string, as printed | What breaks |
|---|---|---|
| Business Email **Draft** *(D2L route)* | `Powers_04_Draft.docx` | Assignment name is just `Draft` |
| Business Email **Draft** *(email subject)* | `Powers_04_Business Email draft` | No extension — it's a subject line. **`draft` lowercase** |
| Business Email **Final** | `Powers_04_Business Email Final.docx` | Spelled out, spaces, **`Final` capitalised** |
| Document Redesign | `Powers_04_DocRedesign.docx` | **Abbreviated, closed up, camel case** |
| Instructions Group Project | `PowersChenAlvarez_04_Instructions draft.docx` | **Three surnames, no separator.** `draft` lowercase again |

Same unit, opposite casing: `Business Email draft` vs `Business Email Final`.
Same course, opposite style: `DocRedesign` vs `Business Email Final`.

> ### The rule is: copy the exact string the assignment gives you. Character for character.
>
> There is no generating rule, and reaching for one is how the points go. The
> filename is also **not the assignment's title** — the Instructions Group Project
> is filed as `Instructions draft`.
>
> **Anchor — submittals.** You don't name the part; the spec names the part. If the
> sheet says `1/2" CDX`, you submit `1/2" CDX`, not "half-inch plywood" — same board,
> clearer name, still rejected. Match the string.

**Why she built a quiz for this:** Week 2 Tuesday's class topic is literally
*"Following directions class discussion."* The naming quiz is that thesis in
miniature. She is not testing filenames; she is testing whether instructions get
executed exactly.

## When no filename is printed — order of operations

> *"Each assignment has a section entitled **SUBMISSION** which details how the
> assignment needs to be submitted."*

1. **Read the assignment's SUBMISSION section in D2L.** She says every assignment has one.
2. If it genuinely doesn't specify → `Powers_04_AssignmentName.docx`.
3. **Email her only if 1 and 2 both fail.**

In a course whose week-2 topic is *following directions*, asking about something the
assignment already answers reads as not having read it. Asking is free once you have.

# Part B — Course policies

## Attendance

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Free unexcused absences | **3** |
| From the 4th | **~3.5 points off the final grade, each** |
| 6 unexcused | **−1 letter grade** |
| 9 unexcused | **−2 letter grades** |
| 3 tardies | **= 1 unexcused absence** |

> *"If you choose to miss class due to work schedule, class reunion, going to the
> beach, job fair, car trouble, studying for a test (or midterm, etc.)… then you will
> receive a ZERO for all assignments completed in class during the day you have missed."*

Medical or genuine emergency → tell her, **be prepared to provide documentation.**
Excused absence or lateness must be arranged **ahead of time**.

**Not an online course.** D2L does not substitute for being in the room.
Nonattendance is **not** a withdrawal — enrollment is Chris's to manage.

## Deadlines and late work

- Due **midnight**, with a **grace period until 8:00 AM** the following morning.
  **→ Cue: midnight due, 8 AM dead.**
- Late major work: **−10% per day.**
- **The penalty is not the real constraint:**

> *"I will only accept assignments until I have graded the entire class set. That
> could be in two hours or two days."*

Once the folder closes it is a zero, and she does not announce when that happens.
Her own advice: submit anyway — *"A lower grade is better than a zero."*
**There is no extension mechanism in this course.** Nothing to request, nobody to ask.

- **Zero late credit, no exceptions: Discussion Posts · Quizzes · Extra Credit.**
  **→ Cue: DQE.** Backwards from instinct — the 20% report can be late; the 2-point
  discussion post cannot.

> ⚠ **Cross-course contamination watch.** PHYS 2211 grants WebAssign extensions *with
> penalty if requested before the deadline.* **TCOM has no such rule.**
> **PHYS rewards foresight; TCOM rewards speed.** Missed cold 2026-08-19 by importing
> the PHYS rule into TCOM.

## Submission channel

> *"I do NOT accept email attachments for any assignment!"*

Everything goes to the **D2L Assignments folder** — except work that *is* an email,
where the email body is the deliverable and nothing is attached. Three of those:
the Week 1 email, the Business Email unit, and the Progress Report (wk 10).
Right document, wrong channel = not submitted.

## Academic integrity — the strictest of the five courses

Stated **twice**, verbatim:

> *"Using AI to write your assignments is considered PLAGIARISM. You may only use AI
> for editing or proofreading, if you cite the usage."*

- **Blanket, not per-assignment.** AI may not draft the Week 1 email, the Business
  Email, the memo, the report, or an outline that becomes one.
- AI **may** edit or proofread work Chris has already written — **and he must cite it.**
- **No re-writes are permitted for plagiarized work, under any circumstances.**
- *"Misunderstandings, miscommunication, oversights or lack of comprehension as to what
  constitutes plagiarized materials are not accepted in this course."*
- **All D2L submissions run through Turnitin.**
- An F is assigned for *"any evidence of plagiarism or from omission of proper APA
  standards."*

## Grades and appeals

- Assignments worth **2 points** = pass/fail. Anything larger has a rubric.
- Grading starts **after 8:00 AM** following the due date — never before.
- Directive comments *("You need to review your work for subject/verb agreement")*
  are **action items, not suggestions.** Repeating the error after one →
  *"a dramatically reduced grade."*
- **Appeal procedure — all three steps required:**
  1. Re-read your assignment **twice**.
  2. Re-read **both** rubrics — the assignment's and the course grading rubric.
  3. Email her with **substantive evidence**, referencing her feedback.
- ⚠ *"I also reserve the right to **lower a grade** should I find errors I overlooked."*
  Appealing carries downside. No attachment — the argument goes in the email body.
- **Participation is expected and graded.**
- **Extra credit can raise the final grade by up to 2 points.** At least one
  opportunity per semester.
- Grade bands are described by manager reaction: A = *"would remember the work when a
  promotion is discussed"*; F = *"would start looking for someone to replace you."*
- **Early Alerts** are submitted in the first few weeks — not grades, but they notify
  advisors.

## Technology — new to the vault 2026-08-19

> *"Any request for make-ups due to technical issues MUST be accompanied by the ticket
> number received from UITS when the problem was reported to them… You MUST e-mail your
> instructor within 24 hours of the technical difficulty if you wish to request a make-up."*

**Two hard requirements, both easy to fail after the fact:** get a **UITS ticket
number** at the time (470-578-6999 / service@kennesaw.edu), and **email her within 24
hours.** Reconstructing either later is impossible.

- *"Computer problems are not accepted as an excuse for late work."* Back up your data.
- *"Lack of access to a computer is not considered an excuse for the non-submission of work."*

> **This lands directly on a known `.ROOT` risk.** The campus laptop carries ~58% of
> outside-class study time (`04-SCHOOL\semester-workload-plan.md` §1). If it fails
> during a TCOM deadline, the ticket-plus-24-hours rule is the only recourse that exists.

## Communication

- **Email is the preferred and only reliable channel.** Both addresses work:
  `ldiamon@kennesaw.edu` **or** `lisa.diamond@kennesaw.edu`.
- *"Any phone number listed in the directory does not actually reach me in any way.
  Also, I do not have access to the mail drop on campus."*
- **Office hours: MS Teams, evenings, weekdays, by appointment only.** Email first —
  an appointment is required.
- **Check KSU email every day** — *"You have the responsibility to check your KSU email
  every day."* Class emails carry assignment changes.
- Netiquette applies to all email, discussion posts, and chats.

## Her background — useful for audience calibration

BA (English minor), Masters of Library Science, **10+ years teaching Technical Writing
at KSU.** Precision-trained reader. Format and mechanics are not incidental to her.
