# Technical Report Anatomy

The technical report is the largest single deliverable in TCOM 2010 — **35% of the
grade across two artifacts** (written 20%, oral presentation 15%) — and is built
across Weeks 6–12, one section at a time, with draft review. It is not written the
week it is due; the course is structured so that it cannot be.

**Because it is assembled incrementally, the failure mode is not a bad report. It
is a missed weekly section**, after which every later section arrives late.

> following sections for review by professor" — no points attach. That is precisely

> them concentrates the entire 35% on **week 12 (Nov 9–15), the worst week of the
> semester** — the same week as CSE Test 2. The checkpoints are not admin. They are
> the whole defence.

## The sections, and what each one is for

| Section | Reader | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Transmittal** | The person who commissioned it | Hands the document over. Short, professional, states what is enclosed |
| **Executive summary** | The decision-maker | The whole report in miniature — problem, findings, recommendation. Must stand alone |
| **Introduction** | The full reader | Scope, purpose, background, how the document is organized |
| **Project description / body** | The implementer | The actual work: method, data, analysis |
| **Recommendation / conclusion** | The decision-maker | What to do, and why the evidence supports it |
| **References** | The skeptic | Where it came from |
| **Slides** | The live audience | A different medium, not the report pasted onto pages |

## The distinction most often collapsed

**Executive summary ≠ introduction.** The summary is for someone who will read
*nothing else* and must still be able to decide. The introduction is for someone
about to read *everything* and needs orientation. Writing one and relabeling it as
the other is the most common structural error in the assignment.

Anchor: the executive summary is the whole house seen from the street. The
introduction is the floor plan you hand someone at the front door.

## How to study it

Retrieval matched to use, per `HAT_EDUCATOR.md`: for a report, the matching
retrieval is **drafting a section for a real audience and taking feedback** — not
recalling what an executive summary is. Read one worked example, then draft the
same section for a different scenario without the example visible, then compare.

## Two gaps the semester map already found

Neither has a ready-made template in the course ancillaries. Build them early
rather than discovering it in the week they are due:

- **Report Group Charter & Task Schedule** — due **Thu Oct 1, midnight**. The syllabus
  names three samples (*Sample Group Charter*, *Roles & Tasks within Your Team*,
  *Sample Task Schedule*) but all three sit behind D2L auth. **Download them on Aug 24**,
  nine weeks before they are needed; the nearest local substitute is the generic
  `Activities\Schedule.docx`, which is not a charter.
- **Reflective Memo** — due **Thu Nov 12**, the same day as the report itself. Build it
  from `Activities\Memo.docx` plus the syllabus's own framing. **Start it in week 11**,
  when the syllabus first tells you to — landing it on Nov 12 alongside the report and
  the presentations is how a 10% component gets written in an hour.
