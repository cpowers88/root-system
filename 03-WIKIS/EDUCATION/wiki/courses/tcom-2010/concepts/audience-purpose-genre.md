# The Four-Part Decision Model — Audience, Purpose, Genre, Constraints

The single transferable idea in TCOM 2010. Every document decision in the course —
and every proposal or report written for a client later — resolves to these four
questions answered *before* drafting.

Memorizing document formats is lower-order learning and it does not survive the
semester. Encoding this model does, because it **generates** the format instead of
recalling it.

## The model

| Question | What it decides |
|---|---|
| **Audience** — who reads this, and what do they already know? | Vocabulary, how much background, what can be assumed |
| **Purpose** — what must they be able to *do* after reading? | What goes first, what can be cut, what the document is for |
| **Genre** — what document type does this situation call for? | Structure, conventions, expected sections |
| **Constraints** — length, format, medium, deadline, rubric | Everything the rubric grades that is not content |

## The question that unsticks any draft

> **"Who is reading this, and what do they need to decide?"**

From `HAT_TCOM.md`. It works because it collapses audience and purpose into one
answerable question. A stuck draft is nearly always a draft whose author has not
decided who it is for.

## Why audience is the hinge

The same technical content becomes a different document for each reader:

- **A decision-maker** needs the recommendation first, evidence second. They may
  never read past page one — so the conclusion cannot live at the end.
- **A technician** needs the procedure in order, with no summary judgments.
- **A mixed audience** — the usual real case — needs a layered document: executive
  summary for the decider, body for the implementer.

This is why the technical report carries an executive summary *and* an
introduction. They are not redundant; they are written for different readers.

## Higher-order use

Do not stop at classifying a document. Ask:

- What changes about this document if the audience becomes non-technical?
- Which section would a busy reader skip, and does that break comprehension?
- What assumption about the reader would make this document fail?

Those are the prompts that turn TCOM from a compliance course into a capability.

## Connection to the North Star

Audience → purpose → genre → constraints is the same reasoning used to write a
client proposal, a statement of work, or an audit report. `NORTH_STAR.md` §2 names
communication in the permanent capability base, and this model is the part of TCOM
that outlives the course. **This is why TCOM has a wiki at all** (plan ruling J-2).
