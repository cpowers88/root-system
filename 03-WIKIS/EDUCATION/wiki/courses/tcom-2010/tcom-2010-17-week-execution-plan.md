## Academic-integrity boundary

> Diamond’s syllabus treats using AI to write assignments as plagiarism. AI may
> edit or proofread Chris’s already-written work only when that use is cited.

- AI does not draft an email, outline, proposal, memo, report section, slide, or
  instructions that could become submitted work.
- Chris reads the live prompt/rubric and creates every skeleton and draft himself.
- AI may teach genre concepts with unrelated examples, quiz policy knowledge, or
  proofread Chris’s completed prose after he explicitly requests it; cite that use.
- Never place protected live assignment content in an AI prompt unless the task is
  clearly permitted proofreading of Chris’s own draft.

## Weekly operating system — 5–7 hours outside class

1. **Sunday control — 25 minutes:** inspect D2L and KSU email seven days forward;
   record each deadline, channel, exact filename/subject, rubric, and materials.
2. **Before Tuesday — 45–60 minutes:** read the assigned book section and one
   mapped example. Close both and explain audience, purpose, genre, constraints.
3. **Tuesday start — 60–90 minutes:** Chris creates the assignment skeleton and
   first content independently. Start at least 72 hours before the deadline when
   the release window allows.
4. **Before Thursday — 30–45 minutes:** reread the rubric, check the exact required
   elements, and prepare the question or draft section for class feedback.
5. **Thursday/Friday production — 90–150 minutes:** revise from instructor/peer
   feedback, run a reader test, then proofread format and mechanics.
6. **Submission control — 15 minutes:** exact channel, exact filename, successful
   upload/email, reopened-file check, and receipt/sent-message evidence.
7. **Reusable capability — 10 minutes:** after grading, record one audience,
   structure, or design lesson worth reusing in client work—never copy protected
   assignment content into the wiki.

**Deadline rule:** work is nominally due at midnight with an 8:00 AM grace period,
but do not plan around the grace. Major work loses 10% per late day and becomes a
zero as soon as the class set is graded. Discussion posts, quizzes, and extra
credit accept no late work at all.

## A-grade scorecard

- **Control:** all live requirements inspected within 24 hours of release and
  started at least 72 hours before due when possible.
- **Compliance:** exact filename/subject, channel, format, rubric, and receipt on
  every submission; never infer a filename when one is printed.
- **Audience:** document decisions can be defended from audience, purpose, genre,
  and constraints rather than preference.
- **Draft quality:** each feedback opportunity receives substantive work, not a
  placeholder; directive comments are repaired before the next submission.
- **Report defense:** all four ungraded checkpoints met; full draft assembled by
  Nov 6; technical report/presentation/memo delivered Nov 12.
- **Transfer:** after each unit, one independently stated lesson is retained for
  future proposals, audit reports, instructions, or client communication.
