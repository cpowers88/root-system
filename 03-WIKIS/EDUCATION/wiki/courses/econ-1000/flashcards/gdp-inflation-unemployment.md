# Flashcards - ECON 1000 GDP, Inflation, Unemployment, and Banking

Use these when Chapters 7-9 open. Attempt each answer cold before turning it over.

## GDP

**Front:** What does GDP measure?

**Back:** The market value of final goods and services produced within a country's
borders during a stated period.

---

**Front:** Why distinguish nominal GDP from real GDP?

**Back:** Nominal GDP can rise because output, prices, or both rose. Real GDP adjusts
for price changes so it better tracks changes in production.

---

## CPI and Inflation

**Front:** Is the CPI index level the inflation rate?

**Back:** No. CPI is a price-index level; inflation is the percentage change in that
level over a period.

---

**Front:** What does `1982-1984 = 100` mean for CPIAUCSL?

**Back:** The average index level during the reference period is defined as 100. It
does not mean the current basket costs $100.

---

**Front:** What is the difference between disinflation and deflation?

**Back:** Disinflation is slower positive inflation - prices still rise. Deflation
is a fall in the general price level.

---

**Front:** How is an approximate year-over-year CPI inflation rate calculated?

**Back:** `(current CPI / CPI 12 months earlier - 1) * 100`.

---

**Front:** Why can deflation hurt borrowers even though prices are falling?

**Back:** Fixed-dollar debts do not shrink automatically, so falling prices and
incomes increase the real burden of repayment.

---

## Unemployment and Contraction

**Front:** Why can cheaper goods coexist with severe hardship during a depression?

**Back:** If jobs, income, and production collapse faster than prices, households
can have less ability to buy even cheaper goods.

---

**Front:** Does GDP falling at the same time unemployment rises prove one caused the
other?

**Back:** No. The pattern is consistent with economic contraction, but causation
requires a mechanism and stronger evidence.

---

## Banking and Policy

**Front:** What is a bank reserve?

**Back:** Cash held by a bank plus eligible deposits it holds at the central bank,
used to meet withdrawals and settlement needs.

---

**Front:** What is a banking panic?

**Back:** A loss of confidence that causes many depositors to withdraw funds at
once, putting pressure on banks that hold only part of deposits as reserves.

---

**Front:** How can bank failures shrink the money stock in Wheelock's explanation?

**Back:** Withdrawals and lost reserves force loan and deposit contraction, reducing
spending and creating further defaults and failures.

---

**Front:** What problem does deposit insurance try to solve?

**Back:** It reduces the incentive for insured depositors to run on a bank out of
fear that they will lose their deposits.

---

**Front:** What makes an economic policy explanation complete?

**Back:** Name the tool, the behavior it changes, the causal mechanism, the intended
result, and a possible tradeoff or unintended effect.

---

**Front:** What is the difference between correlation and causation?

**Back:** Correlation means variables move together; causation means a supported
mechanism shows that changing one helps produce the change in the other.
