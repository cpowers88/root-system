# Drill - CPI and Great Depression Economic Reasoning

## Use Point

Do not complete this now. Use Part A during Chapter 8, Part B during Chapters 8-9,
and Part C during Chapters 10-11 or final review.

## Part A - Read the Measurement

Suppose a price index is `250` in one month and `260` in the same month one year
later.

Before calculating:

1. Predict whether the inflation rate is positive or negative.
2. State what the two index numbers do and do not mean.
3. Write the year-over-year percentage-change skeleton with labels, not numbers.
4. Calculate the rate.
5. Explain whether the result proves every household experienced exactly that rate.

## Part B - Rebuild the Feedback Loop

Without the reading guide open, put these events into a defensible causal order:

- bank reserves fall
- borrowers default
- spending falls
- unemployment rises
- depositors withdraw funds
- loans and deposits contract
- production and prices fall

Then:

1. Draw arrows and explain every arrow in one sentence.
2. Circle the point where deposit insurance could alter behavior.
3. Mark one link that would need evidence rather than assumption.
4. Explain why falling prices can worsen fixed-dollar debt burdens.

## Part C - Policy Claim Test

Evaluate the statement: “The government increased spending, so the economy
recovered.” Do not decide whether it is true until you complete this frame:

1. Which government action and which date range?
2. What behavior was supposed to change?
3. What is the proposed mechanism from action to recovery?
4. What indicator would measure recovery?
5. What alternative explanation must be considered?
6. What unintended effect or tradeoff is possible?

## Part D - Mixed Indicator Cold Check

For each scenario, identify the indicator, direction, most plausible vocabulary,
and one conclusion that the information does **not** justify:

1. CPI rises 2 percent after rising 6 percent the prior year.
2. CPI falls while unemployment and defaults rise.
3. Nominal GDP rises while the price level rises by a similar amount.
4. GDP and unemployment move in opposite directions for several quarters.

## Explain-Back Finish

In two minutes, connect GDP, CPI, inflation/deflation, unemployment, banks, and
policy without reading. If one arrow is vague, reread only the matching chunk in
[[econ-1000-great-depression-cpi-reading-guide]].
