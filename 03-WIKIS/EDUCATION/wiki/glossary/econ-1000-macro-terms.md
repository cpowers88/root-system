# Glossary - ECON 1000 Macro and Great Depression Terms

Use this bank during Chapters 7-11. Each definition is deliberately short; the
reading guide supplies the relationships among the terms.

## Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

The market value of final goods and services produced within a country during a
specified period.

**Anchor:** the country's production scoreboard, not a count of money in bank
accounts.

**Common confusion:** GDP measures production, not personal well-being by itself.

## Nominal GDP

GDP measured using the prices current in the measured period.

**Common confusion:** it can rise because production increased, prices increased,
or both.

## Real GDP

GDP adjusted for changes in the price level so different periods can be compared
more meaningfully as production quantities.

**Anchor:** comparing loads after using the same measuring scale.

## Price Index

A number that tracks the average price of a defined basket relative to a reference
period.

**Common confusion:** an index value of 100 does not mean the basket costs $100.

## Consumer Price Index (CPI)

A price index tracking a weighted basket of goods and services purchased by the
population represented by that CPI series.

**Course source:** local series `CPIAUCSL` covers urban consumers, all items,
seasonally adjusted.

## Inflation

A sustained increase in the general price level, normally expressed as the
percentage change in a price index over a period.

**Common confusion:** one product becoming more expensive is not enough to prove
general inflation.

## Disinflation

A decrease in the inflation rate while the general price level is still rising.

**Anchor:** the car is still moving forward but has taken its foot off the gas.

## Deflation

A sustained decrease in the general price level.

**Common confusion:** lower prices are not automatically beneficial when wages,
employment, output, and borrower income are also falling.

## Unemployment Rate

The percentage of the labor force that is without a job, available for work, and
actively seeking work under the survey definition.

**Common confusion:** it is not the percentage of the entire population without a
job; people outside the labor force are treated separately.

## Money Stock

The quantity of money available in the economy under a stated measure, including
important forms of bank deposits as well as physical currency.

## Bank Reserve

Cash a bank holds plus eligible balances it maintains at the central bank to meet
withdrawals and payment obligations.

**Common confusion:** reserves are not the same thing as all customer deposits.

## Banking Panic

A widespread loss of confidence that leads many depositors to demand funds at once,
pressuring banks that keep only part of deposits as reserves.

## Deposit Insurance

A government-backed guarantee protecting eligible deposits up to a stated limit,
designed partly to reduce the incentive for panic withdrawals.

## Real Debt Burden

The purchasing-power cost of repaying debt. Deflation can increase this burden
because a fixed number of dollars becomes harder to earn as prices and incomes fall.

## Monetary Policy

Central-bank action affecting financial conditions, money and credit, and ultimately
economic activity and prices.

**Common confusion:** monetary policy is central-bank policy; taxation and government
spending are fiscal policy.

## Market Failure

A situation in which unregulated market outcomes fail to allocate resources
efficiently under the model being used, such as because of externalities, public
goods, market power, or information problems.

## Government Failure

A situation in which government action produces inefficient or harmful results,
often through weak information, incentives, capture, implementation limits, or
unintended consequences.

**Common confusion:** recognizing government failure does not prove that every
market outcome is good, just as recognizing market failure does not prove every
intervention is good.

## Correlation

A measurable pattern in which variables move together.

## Causation

A relationship supported by evidence and a credible mechanism in which changing
one factor helps produce a change in another.

**Decision rule:** never promote correlation to causation without naming the
mechanism, competing explanations, and evidence needed.
