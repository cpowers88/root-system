# Flashcards — Stage 11: Angular Momentum (Ch 11)

## Cards

### Card 1
**Q:** What is the equation for angular momentum of a rigid body? What are its units?
**A:** L = Iω; units kg·m²/s.

### Card 2
**Q:** What is the equation for angular momentum of a single particle?
**A:** L = mvr sin θ, where θ is the angle between the position vector r and the velocity v. Equivalently, |L| = mvr⊥ where r⊥ is the perpendicular distance from the axis to the line of v.

### Card 3
**Q:** What is the general form of Newton's second law for rotation?
**A:** Στ_ext = dL/dt. Torque equals the rate of change of angular momentum.

### Card 4
**Q:** What condition must be met for angular momentum to be conserved?
**A:** The net external torque on the system must be zero: Στ_ext = 0.

### Card 5
**Q:** Write the conservation of angular momentum equation for a single body changing shape.
**A:** I_i ω_i = I_f ω_f.

### Card 6
**Q:** A skater pulls her arms in, reducing I from 4.0 to 1.0 kg·m². Her initial ω = 2 rad/s. What is her final ω?
**A:** ω_f = I_i ω_i / I_f = (4.0)(2.0)/(1.0) = 8.0 rad/s.

### Card 7
**Q:** How do you find the direction of angular momentum L for a rotating object?
**A:** Right-hand rule: curl the fingers of the right hand in the direction of rotation; the thumb points in the direction of L (along the rotation axis).

### Card 8
**Q:** Is kinetic energy conserved when a skater pulls her arms in (Στ_ext = 0)?
**A:** No. Angular momentum L is conserved, but kinetic energy increases because the skater's muscles do work. KE = L²/(2I), so when I decreases, KE increases.

### Card 9
**Q:** A 60 kg person stands at radius r = 2.0 m on a rotating disk (I_disk = 100 kg·m²). What is I_total?
**A:** I_total = I_disk + mr² = 100 + (60)(2.0²) = 100 + 240 = 340 kg·m².

### Card 10
**Q:** What is the rotational analogue of F = dp/dt?
**A:** τ = dL/dt. Torque is to angular momentum what force is to linear momentum.

### Card 11
**Q:** Why does a planet move faster when it's closer to the Sun (Kepler's 2nd law)?
**A:** Because angular momentum is conserved (gravity exerts no torque — it acts along r). When r decreases, v must increase to keep L = mvr constant.

### Card 12
**Q:** When does L = Iω NOT apply?
**A:** When the object is not a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis. For a particle, use L = mvr sin θ. For a deformable system mid-deformation, track total L = ΣL_i for each part.
