# Flashcards — Stage 9: Linear Momentum and Collisions (Ch 9)

## Cards

### Card 1
**Q:** What is the definition of linear momentum, and what are its SI units?
**A:** p⃗ = mv⃗. Units: kg·m/s. It is a vector — direction matches velocity.

### Card 2
**Q:** What is Newton's 2nd law written in terms of momentum?
**A:** ΣF⃗ = dp⃗/dt. The F = ma form is the special case when mass is constant.

### Card 3
**Q:** What is impulse, and what are its units?
**A:** J⃗ = ΔP⃗ = FΔt (for constant force), or J⃗ = ∫F dt (for a time-varying force). Units: N·s = kg·m/s.

### Card 4
**Q:** What is the impulse-momentum theorem?
**A:** The impulse delivered to an object equals its change in momentum: J⃗ = Δp⃗ = p⃗_f − p⃗_i.

### Card 5
**Q:** What condition is required for the total momentum of a system to be conserved?
**A:** The net external force on the system must be zero: ΣF_ext = 0. Internal forces between parts of the system cancel by Newton's 3rd law.

### Card 6
**Q:** What is the difference between elastic and inelastic collisions?
**A:** Elastic: both momentum AND kinetic energy are conserved. Inelastic: momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is NOT (some converted to heat, sound, deformation). Perfectly inelastic: objects stick together after collision.

### Card 7
**Q:** Two objects of mass m₁ and m₂ collide perfectly inelastically. Write the momentum conservation equation.
**A:** m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = (m₁ + m₂)vf

### Card 8
**Q:** For a 1D elastic collision where m₂ is initially at rest, what are the final velocities?
**A:** v₁f = [(m₁ − m₂)/(m₁ + m₂)]v₁ᵢ and v₂f = [2m₁/(m₁ + m₂)]v₁ᵢ

### Card 9
**Q:** What are the two conservation equations used to solve an elastic collision?
**A:** (1) Momentum: m₁v₁ᵢ + m₂v₂ᵢ = m₁v₁f + m₂v₂f. (2) Kinetic energy: ½m₁v₁ᵢ² + ½m₂v₂ᵢ² = ½m₁v₁f² + ½m₂v₂f²

### Card 10
**Q:** How do you solve a 2D collision problem?
**A:** Apply momentum conservation independently in x and y directions. Set up two equations: p_x,before = p_x,after and p_y,before = p_y,after. Solve simultaneously.

### Card 11
**Q:** Where is the center of mass of a two-object system?
**A:** x_cm = (m₁x₁ + m₂x₂)/(m₁ + m₂). It lies closer to the heavier object.

### Card 12
**Q:** In a collision, can a larger (heavier) object bounce backward off a smaller one?
**A:** No. For m₁ > m₂ in a 1D elastic collision, v₁f = [(m₁ − m₂)/(m₁ + m₂)]v₁ᵢ is positive — the heavier object slows down but does NOT reverse. Only if m₁ < m₂ does the incoming object reverse.
