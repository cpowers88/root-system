# Flashcards — Stage 3: Vectors (Ch 3)

## Cards

### Card 0a
**Q:** How do you convert polar coordinates (r, θ) to Cartesian (x, y)?
**A:** x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ.

### Card 0b
**Q:** How do you convert Cartesian (x, y) to polar (r, θ), and what's the one thing you must always check?
**A:** r = √(x²+y²), θ = tan⁻¹(y/x) — then check the quadrant against a sketch, since the calculator only returns −90° to +90°.

### Card 1
**Q:** What is the difference between a scalar and a vector?
**A:** A scalar has magnitude only (e.g., speed = 60 m/s). A vector has magnitude AND direction (e.g., velocity = 60 m/s north).

### Card 2
**Q:** Given vector A at angle θ from the +x axis, what are its x and y components?
**A:** Ax = A cos θ, Ay = A sin θ. (θ measured counterclockwise from the +x axis.)

### Card 3
**Q:** Given components Ax and Ay, how do you find the magnitude and direction of A?
**A:** Magnitude: A = √(Ax² + Ay²). Direction: θ = tan⁻¹(Ay/Ax). Always sketch to verify the correct quadrant.

### Card 4
**Q:** What do the unit vectors î, ĵ, k̂ represent?
**A:** î = unit vector in the +x direction, ĵ = +y direction, k̂ = +z direction. Each has magnitude 1 and no units.

### Card 5
**Q:** How do you add two vectors A⃗ = Axî + Ayĵ and B⃗ = Bxî + Byĵ?
**A:** Add components separately: R⃗ = (Ax+Bx)î + (Ay+By)ĵ. Never add magnitudes directly unless the vectors point in the same direction.

### Preview cards (Chapter 7 material, not required for the Ch3 exam)

### Card 6
**Q:** What is the dot product formula, and what kind of quantity does it produce?
**A:** A⃗·B⃗ = AB cos θ = AxBx + AyBy. It produces a scalar (one number, no direction).

### Card 7
**Q:** What does it mean geometrically when the dot product A⃗·B⃗ = 0?
**A:** The two vectors are perpendicular (θ = 90°, cos 90° = 0).

### Card 8
**Q:** What is the dot product of two parallel unit vectors (e.g., î·î)?
**A:** 1. Because cos 0° = 1.

### Card 9
**Q:** What is the dot product of two perpendicular unit vectors (e.g., î·ĵ)?
**A:** 0. Because cos 90° = 0.

### Card 10
**Q:** Why can't you just add the magnitudes of two vectors (A + B) to get the resultant?
**A:** Vectors have direction. You must add components. A + B gives the maximum possible resultant only when both vectors point in the exact same direction.

### Card 11
**Q:** How does the cross product A⃗×B⃗ differ from the dot product A⃗·B⃗?
**A:** Cross product produces a vector (with direction perpendicular to both A and B); dot product produces a scalar. Cross product is used for torque and angular momentum (Stages 10–11); dot product is used for work (Stage 7).

### Card 12
**Q:** What is the resultant vector R⃗ = A⃗ + B⃗ if A⃗ = 3î + 4ĵ and B⃗ = 1î − 2ĵ?
**A:** R⃗ = 4î + 2ĵ. Magnitude: √(4² + 2²) = √20 ≈ 4.47. Angle: tan⁻¹(2/4) ≈ 26.6° above +x axis.
