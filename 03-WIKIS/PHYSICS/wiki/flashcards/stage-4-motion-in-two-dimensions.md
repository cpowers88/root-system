# Flashcards — Stage 4: Motion in Two Dimensions (Ch 4)

## Cards

### Card 1
**Q:** What is the core independence principle of projectile motion?
**A:** Horizontal and vertical motions are completely independent. They share only the time variable t. Solve them as two separate 1D problems.

### Card 2
**Q:** What is the horizontal acceleration of a projectile (no air resistance)?
**A:** Zero. There is no horizontal force, so vₓ is constant throughout the entire flight.

### Card 3
**Q:** What is the vertical acceleration of a projectile near Earth's surface?
**A:** ay = -g = -9.80 m/s² (downward). It is the same as free fall.

### Card 4
**Q:** How do you find v₀ₓ and v₀ᵧ from v₀ and θ₀?
**A:** v₀ₓ = v₀ cos θ₀ (horizontal); v₀ᵧ = v₀ sin θ₀ (vertical).

### Card 5
**Q:** At the peak of a projectile's trajectory, what are vₓ and vᵧ?
**A:** vₓ = v₀ₓ (unchanged — horizontal velocity never changes). vᵧ = 0 (vertical velocity is zero at the peak). The object is still moving horizontally.

### Card 6
**Q:** What is the range formula, and when does it apply?
**A:** R = v₀² sin(2θ₀) / g. Applies ONLY when the launch and landing heights are equal (symmetric flight). Do not use for cliff problems.

### Card 7
**Q:** At what launch angle is the range maximum (same launch and landing height)?
**A:** 45°. This maximizes sin 2θ = sin 90° = 1.

### Card 8
**Q:** What is centripetal acceleration? What is its formula and direction?
**A:** The acceleration of an object in circular motion at constant speed. a_c = v²/r, directed inward toward the center of the circle.

### Card 9
**Q:** Why is centripetal acceleration non-zero even when speed is constant?
**A:** Acceleration is the rate of change of the velocity vector, not just its speed. The direction of velocity changes continuously in circular motion, so the acceleration is non-zero.

### Card 10
**Q:** What is "centripetal force" and how do you draw it on a free body diagram?
**A:** Centripetal force is the net inward force — it is not a separate force type. Draw the real force that provides it (tension, gravity, friction, normal) on the FBD, not "centripetal force."

### Card 11
**Q:** What is the period T of circular motion?
**A:** T = 2πr/v — the time to complete one full revolution. Unit: seconds.

### Card 12
**Q:** Write the relative velocity equation relating frames A, B, and object P.
**A:** v⃗_PA = v⃗_PB + v⃗_BA. The middle subscripts cancel. Reversing subscripts negates: v⃗_AB = -v⃗_BA.

### Card 13
**Q:** For a projectile launched horizontally from height h, find the time of flight.
**A:** t = √(2h/g). Comes from y = h - ½gt² = 0, solved for t.

### Card 14
**Q:** What is the equation for maximum height in a projectile launched at angle θ₀?
**A:** h_max = (v₀ sin θ₀)² / (2g) = v₀ᵧ² / (2g). Derived by setting vᵧ = 0 and solving.

### Card 15
**Q:** An object moves along a curved path and its speed is changing. What two acceleration components does it have, and which direction does each point?
**A:** Radial (centripetal) a_r = v²/r, pointing toward the center. Tangential a_t = dv/dt, pointing along the direction of motion (same direction as v if speeding up, opposite if slowing down).

### Card 16
**Q:** How do you combine a_r and a_t into the total acceleration magnitude?
**A:** a = √(a_r² + a_t²) — the Pythagorean theorem, since the two components are perpendicular. Never just add them.
