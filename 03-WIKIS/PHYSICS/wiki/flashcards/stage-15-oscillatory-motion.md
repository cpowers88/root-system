# Flashcards — Stage 15: Oscillatory Motion / SHM (Ch 15)

## Cards

### Card 1
**Q:** What is the position equation for simple harmonic motion?
**A:** x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ). A = amplitude, ω = angular frequency (rad/s), φ = phase constant (initial phase), t = time.

### Card 2
**Q:** What is the defining condition for SHM (in terms of acceleration and position)?
**A:** a = −ω²x. Acceleration is proportional to displacement and opposite in sign — always directed back toward equilibrium. This is the restoring condition.

### Card 3
**Q:** For a spring-mass system, what is the period T and angular frequency ω?
**A:** T = 2π√(m/k), ω = √(k/m). Larger mass → longer period. Stiffer spring (larger k) → shorter period. Period does NOT depend on amplitude.

### Card 4
**Q:** What is the period of a simple pendulum?
**A:** T = 2π√(L/g) (valid for small angles, θ < ~15°). L = pendulum length, g = gravitational acceleration. Does not depend on mass or amplitude (for small angles).

### Card 5
**Q:** In SHM, where is the kinetic energy maximum and where is it zero?
**A:** KE is maximum at the equilibrium position (x = 0) where speed is maximum. KE is zero at the turning points (x = ±A) where the mass momentarily stops.

### Card 6
**Q:** What is the total mechanical energy of a simple harmonic oscillator?
**A:** E = ½kA² = constant. Always equal to the maximum potential energy (at the turning point). It can also be written as ½mv_max² (at equilibrium). E does not change during oscillation.

### Card 7
**Q:** How do you find the speed of a mass-spring system at position x?
**A:** Use energy conservation: ½mv² + ½kx² = ½kA². So v = √[(k/m)(A² − x²)] = ω√(A² − x²).

### Card 8
**Q:** If the amplitude of an SHM oscillator doubles, what happens to its total energy and period?
**A:** Energy quadruples (E = ½kA², so E ∝ A²). Period stays the same (T = 2π√(m/k) does not depend on A).

### Card 9
**Q:** What is the relationship between frequency f, angular frequency ω, and period T?
**A:** ω = 2πf = 2π/T. So f = 1/T and T = 1/f. Units: ω in rad/s, f in Hz, T in s.

### Card 10
**Q:** What differential equation does SHM satisfy?
**A:** d²x/dt² = −ω²x. This is Newton's 2nd law applied to a spring: F = ma gives −kx = m(d²x/dt²), so ω² = k/m. The solution is x(t) = A cos(ωt + φ).

### Card 11
**Q:** What is the velocity equation in SHM, and where is velocity maximum?
**A:** v(t) = −Aω sin(ωt + φ). Maximum speed v_max = Aω occurs at x = 0 (equilibrium). At the turning points x = ±A the velocity is zero.

### Card 12
**Q:** What is the acceleration equation in SHM, and where is acceleration maximum?
**A:** a(t) = −Aω² cos(ωt + φ) = −ω²x. Maximum magnitude a_max = Aω² occurs at the turning points x = ±A. Acceleration is zero at equilibrium.

### Card 13
**Q:** For a simple pendulum, what variables does the period depend on — and what doesn't it depend on?
**A:** T = 2π√(L/g). Depends on L (length) and g (gravitational field). Does NOT depend on mass or amplitude (for small angles). This is why all pendulum clocks of the same length tick at the same rate.

### Card 14
**Q:** What is the period formula for a physical pendulum, and what does each symbol mean?
**A:** T = 2π√(I/mgd). I = moment of inertia about the pivot; m = total mass; g = 9.8 m/s²; d = distance from pivot to center of mass. Reduces to T = 2π√(L/g) only when the whole mass is concentrated at distance L from the pivot (simple pendulum).

### Card 15
**Q:** What is resonance, and why is it important?
**A:** Resonance occurs when a driving (external) frequency matches the system's natural frequency ω₀. At resonance, energy is transferred most efficiently into the oscillation, so amplitude grows very large (limited only by damping). Examples: Tacoma Narrows Bridge, tuning a radio receiver, MRI machines.
