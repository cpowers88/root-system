# Flashcards — Stage 17: Superposition and Standing Waves (Ch 17)

## Cards

### Card 1
**Q:** State the superposition principle.
**A:** When two or more waves overlap in the same medium, the total displacement at any point is the algebraic sum of the individual displacements. The waves do not affect each other — they pass through.

### Card 2
**Q:** What is constructive interference vs. destructive interference?
**A:** Constructive: waves add (same phase → amplitude doubles for equal waves). Destructive: waves cancel (opposite phase → zero amplitude for equal waves).

### Card 3
**Q:** What is a standing wave and how does it form?
**A:** A pattern that appears stationary — formed by two identical waves traveling in opposite directions (e.g., one wave and its reflection). It has fixed nodes (zero displacement) and antinodes (maximum displacement).

### Card 4
**Q:** What are nodes and antinodes?
**A:** Node: a point that never moves (zero displacement at all times). Antinode: a point of maximum oscillation (displacement swings between +A and −A). Nodes and antinodes are fixed in space.

### Card 5
**Q:** For a string fixed at both ends (length L), what wavelengths are allowed?
**A:** L = n(λ/2), so λ_n = 2L/n for n = 1, 2, 3, … Both ends must be nodes.

### Card 6
**Q:** What are the harmonic frequencies for a string fixed at both ends?
**A:** f_n = n·v/(2L) = n·f₁, where f₁ = v/(2L) is the fundamental frequency. All integers n are allowed (all harmonics present).

### Card 7
**Q:** For an air column with ONE closed end, what harmonics are present?
**A:** Only odd harmonics: f_n = n·v/(4L) for n = 1, 3, 5, … The closed end is a node; the open end is an antinode.

### Card 8
**Q:** For an air column with BOTH ends open, what harmonics are present?
**A:** All harmonics: f_n = n·v/(2L) for n = 1, 2, 3, … Both open ends are antinodes.

### Card 9
**Q:** What is beat frequency and how is it produced?
**A:** f_beat = |f₁ − f₂|. Beats are the slow amplitude variation heard when two slightly different frequencies interfere. The sound grows louder and softer at the beat frequency rate.

### Card 10
**Q:** What is resonance in the context of standing waves?
**A:** Resonance occurs when the driving frequency matches one of the natural harmonic frequencies of the system. Energy is efficiently transferred, causing large-amplitude standing waves. This is why bridges or wine glasses can vibrate dramatically at specific frequencies.
