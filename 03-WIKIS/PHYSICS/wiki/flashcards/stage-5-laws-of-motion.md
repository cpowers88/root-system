# Flashcards — Stage 5: The Laws of Motion (Ch 5)

## Cards

### Card 1
**Q:** State Newton's First Law in plain English.
**A:** An object at rest stays at rest, and an object moving at constant velocity keeps moving at constant velocity, unless acted on by a net external force. (Inertia: no net force → no change in motion.)

### Card 2
**Q:** State Newton's Second Law as an equation. What are the units of each quantity?
**A:** ΣF = ma. F in newtons (N = kg·m/s²), m in kg, a in m/s².

### Card 3
**Q:** State Newton's Third Law in plain English.
**A:** If object A exerts a force on object B, then object B exerts a force on object A that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction. Action-reaction pairs always act on DIFFERENT objects.

### Card 4
**Q:** What is the difference between mass and weight?
**A:** Mass (kg) is the amount of matter — it doesn't change with location. Weight (N) is the gravitational force on that mass: w = mg. On the Moon, your mass is the same but your weight is ~1/6.

### Card 5
**Q:** What is a normal force and what direction does it always point?
**A:** The contact force a surface exerts on an object perpendicular to the surface. It always pushes away from the surface — it can push but never pull.

### Card 6
**Q:** What is the difference between static friction and kinetic friction? Which is larger?
**A:** Static friction prevents sliding (acts while object is stationary); kinetic friction opposes sliding (acts while object is moving). Static is larger: μ_s > μ_k always.

### Card 7
**Q:** Give the equations for static and kinetic friction. What does each symbol mean?
**A:** f_s ≤ μ_s · n (static, maximum value); f_k = μ_k · n (kinetic, exact value). μ = coefficient of friction (dimensionless), n = normal force (N).

### Card 8
**Q:** On an inclined plane at angle θ, what are the components of weight along and perpendicular to the slope?
**A:** Along slope (down): w_∥ = mg sinθ. Perpendicular to slope (into surface): w_⊥ = mg cosθ. This means n = mg cosθ when no other vertical forces act.

### Card 9
**Q:** For a free body diagram, what is the golden rule about forces?
**A:** Draw only forces acting ON the chosen object. Never include forces the object exerts on other things. Each force gets one arrow from the object.

### Card 10
**Q:** For an Atwood machine (two masses m₁ and m₂ over a frictionless pulley), what is the acceleration of the system?
**A:** a = (m₂ − m₁)g / (m₁ + m₂). The heavier mass accelerates downward. Derived by writing ΣF = ma for each mass separately and solving the pair of equations.

### Card 11
**Q:** What is tension in a string and can it push?
**A:** Tension is the pulling force that a taut string, rope, or cable exerts along its length. Strings can only pull — they cannot push. If a string goes slack, tension is zero.

### Card 12
**Q:** If a block sits on a flat surface with no acceleration, what can you say about the normal force?
**A:** n = mg (by Newton's 2nd Law in the vertical direction: ΣF_y = n − mg = 0). This is only true on a horizontal surface with no other vertical forces.

### Card 13
**Q:** Why do Newton's Third Law pairs NOT cancel each other out?
**A:** They act on different objects. The net force on an object is only the sum of forces acting on it — forces it exerts on other objects don't appear in its own ΣF = ma equation.

### Card 14
**Q:** When you set up ΣF = ma for a system, what sign convention do you need?
**A:** Choose a positive direction (usually in the direction of expected acceleration) and assign + and − consistently to every force. The sign of a tells you which way the object actually accelerates.

### Card 15
**Q:** A 10 kg block is pulled across a surface with μ_k = 0.3 by a horizontal force of 50 N. What is the acceleration? (g = 9.80 m/s²)
**A:** n = mg = 98 N, f_k = 0.3 × 98 = 29.4 N. ΣF = 50 − 29.4 = 20.6 N. a = F/m = 20.6/10 = 2.06 m/s².
