## Course Outcomes Applied Across Every Stage

Every activated stage must require Chris to:

1. explain the physical situation in plain language;
2. construct an appropriate sketch, graph, physical model, or free-body diagram;
3. identify assumptions and choose a symbolic relationship before substituting;
4. solve symbolically and numerically with units and a reasonableness check;
5. use vectors or calculus when the physical relationship requires them;
6. connect the result to a real situation and explain what changes when one input
   changes; and
7. complete the work independently before any answer comparison.

This implements the page 3 outcomes: inquiry and analysis, problem solving,
mathematical/computational methods, representations, modeling, real-world transfer,
and teamwork. The course lists zero laboratory hours, so no separate laboratory
stage is implied.

## Stable Course Operations Recorded from the PDF

- Three credits: three class hours plus one recitation hour; zero laboratory hours.
- Prerequisite: C or better in MATH 1190, or MATH 1179 and MATH 1189.
- Homework uses WebAssign; group work is allowed, copying is not.
- Quizzes are described as weekly, 15 minutes, one attempt per question, with
  LockDown Browser and webcam; verify the live release/due schedule in D2L.
- Participation comes from active lecture/recitation engagement and group work.
- Published weights: homework 10%, quizzes 5%, participation 10%, three exams
  45%, cumulative final 30%. The lowest homework, quiz, and participation item is
  described as dropped.
- Published grade floors: A 90%, B 80%, C 70%, D 60%.
- AI is permitted only for studying, explanations, guided technique, examples, and
  clarification; no AI-generated content may be submitted for credit.
- Technical make-up requests require a UITS ticket number and instructor notice
  within 24 hours of the problem.
