# Uniform Circular Motion

## How to Recognize This Problem Type

An object moves in a circle (or arc) at constant speed. You are asked to find centripetal acceleration, the force required, speed, radius, or period.

Key phrases: "constant speed around a circle," "rotating," "orbiting," "period of revolution," "radius of curvature," "what force is needed to keep the car on the track."

## Given Information Usually Present

Two of the three quantities: speed v, radius r, centripetal acceleration a_c, or period T.

## Unknown Usually Requested

The third of those quantities, or the net inward force F_c = mv²/r.

## Diagram to Draw

```
      v (tangent, shown at one point)
      ↑
      |
  ←——O——→ a_c points inward (toward center)
      |
      |
    center
```

Always draw the object on the circle, the velocity tangent to the circle, and the centripetal acceleration pointing inward toward the center.

## Equations Commonly Used

```
a_c = v²/r = ω²r        (centripetal acceleration)
F_c = mv²/r             (centripetal force = net inward force)
T = 2πr/v               (period)
v = 2πr/T               (speed from period and radius)
ω = v/r = 2π/T          (angular speed)
```

## Step-by-Step Solving Pattern

1. Identify: what is the radius r? What provides the inward force (gravity, tension, friction, normal)?
2. Write F_c = mv²/r and set it equal to the actual inward force.
3. Solve for the unknown (usually v, r, or a force magnitude).
4. Check: does the direction of acceleration point inward? Does the force identified actually point inward?
5. Check units.

## Unit Checks

- a_c = v²/r → (m/s)²/m = m/s² ✓
- F_c = mv²/r → kg·(m/s)²/m = kg·m/s² = N ✓
- T = 2πr/v → m/(m/s) = s ✓

## Common Traps

- Listing "centripetal force" as a separate, additional force on the free body diagram. It is not a new force — it is what you call the net inward force once you've identified what real force provides it.
- Thinking centripetal acceleration is zero because speed is constant. Constant speed ≠ constant velocity. The direction is always changing.
- Using the wrong value for r — it must be the radius of the circular path, not a diameter or some other length in the problem.
