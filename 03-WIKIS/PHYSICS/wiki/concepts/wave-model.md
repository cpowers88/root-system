# The Wave Model

## What is the physical idea?

A wave is a disturbance that travels through space or a medium, carrying energy from one place to another — without permanently moving the matter of the medium along with it.

## What real-world situation does it describe?

A ripple spreading across a pond. Sound traveling through air. A vibration moving along a guitar string. Light crossing empty space. In every case, the medium itself oscillates locally while the disturbance pattern moves forward.

## Objects / System Involved

The **wave source** (what creates the disturbance) and the **medium** (what the disturbance travels through). For mechanical waves (sound, string waves), a material medium is required. Electromagnetic waves (light) need no medium.

## Two Types of Mechanical Waves

**Transverse wave:** particles of the medium move perpendicular to the direction of wave travel.
- Example: wave on a string — string moves up-down; wave moves along the string.
- Example: light waves (electromagnetic).

**Longitudinal wave:** particles of the medium move parallel to the direction of wave travel.
- Example: sound in air — air molecules compress and expand in the same direction the sound travels.
- Example: compression wave in a spring (slinky).

```
Transverse:
  Direction of wave travel →
  ↑↓ particle motion (up-down)

Longitudinal:
  Direction of wave travel →
  ←→ particle motion (back-forth, same direction)
```

## Quantities That Change

The position of each particle in the medium oscillates in time. The disturbance pattern moves through space at wave speed v. Energy flows in the direction of wave travel.

## Key Distinction

**Wave speed ≠ particle speed.** The wave pattern moves at v = λf. Each individual particle of the medium oscillates with its own velocity vy = ∂y/∂t. These are completely different quantities.

## Calculus Connection

The wave equation describes how the disturbance evolves: ∂²y/∂x² = (1/v²) ∂²y/∂t². Solutions are sinusoidal functions — this is why waves are naturally described with sin and cos.

## Common Beginner Mistake

Thinking the medium moves along with the wave. Watch a leaf floating on water when a ripple passes — the leaf bobs up and down; it does not travel sideways with the wave. The disturbance travels; the medium stays (on average) in place.
