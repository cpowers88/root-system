# Spring Energy Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Applying Hooke's Law (F_s = −kx) and spring potential energy (U_s = ½kx²) to find forces, energy storage, and speeds.

## Prerequisites

[[../equations/spring-pe]], [[../concepts/hookes-law]]

## Instructions

Draw a spring diagram for each problem. Label x (displacement from equilibrium), F_s direction, and any conversions needed (cm → m). Show your calculation in full.

## Problems

**1.** A spring with k = 400 N/m is stretched 0.12 m from equilibrium. Find (a) the spring force magnitude and (b) the elastic PE stored.

**2.** A spring stores 3.2 J of elastic PE when compressed 0.04 m. What is the spring constant?

**3.** A 0.30 kg mass is pressed against a horizontal spring (k = 1200 N/m) compressing it 0.05 m on a frictionless surface. The mass is released from rest. Find its speed when the spring returns to natural length.

**4.** Two springs both obey Hooke's Law. Spring A has k = 300 N/m and spring B has k = 600 N/m. Both are compressed by 0.10 m. (a) Which stores more energy? (b) How much more energy does it store than the other?

## Answers

1. (a) |F_s| = kx = 400 × 0.12 = **48 N**
   (b) U_s = ½kx² = ½(400)(0.12²) = ½(400)(0.0144) = **2.88 J**

2. U_s = ½kx² → 3.2 = ½k(0.04²) = ½k(0.0016) = 0.0008k → k = 3.2/0.0008 = **4 000 N/m**

3. U_s = ½(1200)(0.05²) = ½(1200)(0.0025) = 1.5 J; K_f = U_s → ½(0.30)v² = 1.5 → v² = 10 → **v = 3.2 m/s**

4. (a) U_A = ½(300)(0.01) = 1.5 J; U_B = ½(600)(0.01) = 3.0 J → **Spring B stores more energy**
   (b) ΔU = 3.0 − 1.5 = **1.5 J more**

## Mastery Signal

Chris can identify k, x, and U_s correctly in each problem without confusing Hooke's Law (force) with the PE equation (energy), and can convert between compressed state and released-state speed using energy.
