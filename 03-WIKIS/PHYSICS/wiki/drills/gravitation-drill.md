# Universal Gravitation Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Applying Newton's law of universal gravitation, calculating gravitational field, finding orbital speeds and periods using Kepler's third law, and working with gravitational potential energy.

## Prerequisites

[[../equations/newtons-law-of-gravitation]], [[../equations/gravitational-field]], [[../equations/keplers-third-law]], [[../equations/orbital-speed]], [[../equations/escape-speed]]

## Constants

G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
M_Earth = 5.97×10²⁴ kg, R_Earth = 6.37×10⁶ m
M_Moon = 7.35×10²² kg
M_Sun = 1.99×10³⁰ kg

## Problems

### Part A — Newton's Law of Gravitation

1. Two 10.0 kg bowling balls are placed 1.0 m apart. What is the gravitational force between them?

2. At what altitude above Earth's surface is g = 9.8/4 = 2.45 m/s²? (Hint: g ∝ 1/r², and at the surface r = R_Earth.)

3. The Moon is 3.84×10⁸ m from Earth's center. Find the gravitational force on the 7.35×10²² kg Moon from Earth.

### Part B — Orbital Motion

4. A satellite orbits Earth at an altitude of 400 km above the surface (r = R_Earth + 400 km). Find its orbital speed and period.

5. The Moon's orbital radius is 3.84×10⁸ m. Find its orbital period using Kepler's third law. (Compare to the known value of 27.3 days.)

6. Mars has an orbital radius of 1.52 AU (1 AU = 1.50×10¹¹ m). Earth's orbital period is 1.00 year. Find Mars's orbital period using T² ∝ r³.

### Part C — Escape Speed

7. Find the escape speed from Earth's surface.

8. Find the escape speed from the Moon's surface (M_Moon = 7.35×10²² kg, R_Moon = 1.74×10⁶ m).

9. A projectile is launched from Earth with speed 12 km/s. Will it escape Earth's gravity?

### Part D — Gravitational PE

10. A 500 kg satellite is at r = 2R_Earth from Earth's center. Find its gravitational PE using U = −GMm/r.

11. How much work is required to move the satellite in Problem 10 from r = 2R_Earth to r = 3R_Earth?

## Check Yourself

Problem 1: F = G(10)(10)/(1.0)² = 6.67×10⁻⁹ N (extremely small!)
Problem 4: v_c = √(GM/r) where r = 6.37×10⁶ + 4.0×10⁵ = 6.77×10⁶ m → v_c ≈ 7670 m/s; T = 2πr/v ≈ 5540 s ≈ 92 min
Problem 7: v_e = √(2GM/R) = √(2(6.674×10⁻¹¹)(5.97×10²⁴)/6.37×10⁶) ≈ 11,200 m/s ≈ 11.2 km/s

## Mastery Signal

Chris can compute orbital speed, orbital period, and escape speed from G, M, and r alone — without memorizing "g = 9.8 m/s²" for these problems.
