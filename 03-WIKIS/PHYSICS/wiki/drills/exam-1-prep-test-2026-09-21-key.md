# Practice Test A — Tutor Key

## 1 — 6 points

a. Meter (m), kilogram (kg), second (s). (1)

b. (72.0 km/h)(1000 m/km)(1 h/3600 s) = **20.0 m/s**. Factors (1), result (1).

c. at has units m/s, so it cannot be added to positions. Replace it with at²/2, which has units m. Diagnosis (1), repair (1). This check detects an invalid formula; matching dimensions alone would not prove a formula correct.

d. **7.7 m²**, two significant figures. (1)

## 2 — 8 points

a. Slopes: **+2 m/s²**, **0 m/s²**, **−3 m/s²**. (1 each)

b. Signed areas: 4 m + 8 m + 2 m = **14 m**. Areas (1), result (1).

c. Last segment crosses zero at t = 4 + 4/3 = 16/3 s. Its positive area is 8/3 m and its negative area has magnitude 2/3 m. Distance = 4 + 8 + 8/3 + 2/3 = **46/3 m ≈ 15.3 m**. Split at zero (1), total (1).

d. **t = 16/3 s ≈ 5.33 s**. (1)

Bridge: slope of v(t) is acceleration; signed area is displacement; area under |v(t)| is distance. Zero acceleration does not imply zero velocity.

## 3 — 10 points

a. First vector: Aₓ = 30.0 cos 35.0° = **24.6 m**; Aᵧ = 30.0 sin 35.0° = **17.2 m**. Sketch (1), components (2).

b. Net vector: **(4.57 m, 17.2 m)**. (1 each)

c. Magnitude = **17.8 m**; direction = **75.1° north of east**. Magnitude (1), angle (1), quadrant/direction (1).

d. Distance = **50.0 m** (1). Path length adds both legs; displacement joins the initial and final locations (1).

## 4 — 12 points

a. **v(t) = (6.00 m/s) − (6.00 m/s²)t + (1.50 m/s³)t²**; **a(t) = (−6.00 m/s²) + (3.00 m/s³)t**. Two points each for correct derivative and units.

b. v(t) = (1.50 m/s³)(t − 2 s)², so **t = 2 s is the only rest time**. Equation/factorization (2), in-interval root (1). It is a repeated root; the cart does not reverse direction there.

c. At t = 1 s, v = **+1.50 m/s**, a = **−3.00 m/s²**. It moves right and slows down. Values/signs (2), interpretation (1).

d. dx/dt is the instantaneous rate of change of position, including direction (1). Rest describes v at an instant; acceleration describes how velocity changes (1). In this particular polynomial both v and a happen to vanish at t = 2 s; that is not a general requirement. A vertically thrown ball at its apex is a counterexample.

## 5 — 10 points

a. Integrate dv/dt = (2.00 m/s³)t:

v(t) = (1.00 m/s³)t² + C₁. At t = 0 the cart moves left at 3.00 m/s, so C₁ = −3.00 m/s.

**v(t) = (1.00 m/s³)t² − (3.00 m/s)**.

Integrate dx/dt = v(t):

x(t) = (1/3 m/s³)t³ − (3.00 m/s)t + C₂. At t = 0 it is at x = 4.00 m, so C₂ = 4.00 m.

**x(t) = (1/3 m/s³)t³ − (3.00 m/s)t + (4.00 m)**.

Each integration (2), each initial condition/constant (1): 6 total. Accept equivalent definite-integral derivations with explicit limits and initial conditions.

b. x(3 s) = 4.00 m and x(0) = 4.00 m, so **Δx = 0 m**. Evaluation (1), endpoint subtraction (1).

c. It moves left until **t = √3 s**, then right (1); signed displacement cancels but distance does not (1). Optional verification: total distance = **4√3 m ≈ 6.93 m**. Do not require the optional distance for full credit.

Bridge: integration accumulates signed change; the constants supply the initial state that differentiation removed. Differentiate both answers to check them.

## 6 — 10 points

a. Constant acceleration, no supplied time: **v² = v₀² + 2aΔx**. Select and justify using available quantities (2), solve symbolically (1), evaluate with units (2).

**a = ((20.0 m/s)² − (8.00 m/s)²)/(2 × 42.0 m) = +4.00 m/s²**.

b. **t = (v − v₀)/a = 3.00 s**. Relationship (1), result/units (2).

c. Average velocity for constant acceleration is (8.00 + 20.0)/2 = 14.0 m/s; multiply by 3.00 s to recover **42.0 m**. Independent relationship (1), recovered displacement (1). Alternatively use v₀t + at²/2.

Diagnostic: distinguish equation selection from algebra. The relationship (v − v₀)/Δx has units s⁻¹ and cannot be acceleration. The no-time relationship comes from eliminating t or integrating v dv = a dx for constant a.

## 7 — 12 points

a. Sketch with ground origin and initial height (1). With aₓ = 0 and aᵧ = −g:

- **x(t) = (13.8564 m/s)t**.
- **y(t) = 12.0 m + (8.00 m/s)t − (4.90 m/s²)t²**.
- **vₓ(t) = 13.8564 m/s**.
- **vᵧ(t) = 8.00 m/s − (9.80 m/s²)t**.

Initial components and position functions (2), velocity functions (1). Unrounded intermediate components are intentional.

b. Set y = 0. Roots are approximately **2.58 s** and **−0.949 s**. Reject the negative root for motion after launch. Impact time **2.58 s**, horizontal distance **35.8 m**. Impact equation (1), physical root (1), x evaluation (2).

c. **vₓ = +13.9 m/s**, **vᵧ = −17.3 m/s**, speed **22.2 m/s**. (1 each)

d. The range shortcut assumes equal launch and landing heights, which this problem does not have. (1)

Check: vertical displacement recovered from the chosen time is −12.0 m; impact has negative vertical velocity and positive horizontal velocity. Do not round flight time before computing range and speed.

## 8 — 10 points

a. Weight mg downward, normal perpendicular out of ramp, kinetic friction up ramp. Choose downhill positive along ramp. Diagram/forces (2), axes (1).

b. No normal acceleration: **N = mg cos 25.0° = 44.4 N**. Friction **fₖ = μₖN = 8.88 N**, up ramp. N setup/result (2), friction (1).

c. **ma = mg sin 25.0° − μₖmg cos 25.0°**.

**a = g(sin 25.0° − 0.200 cos 25.0°) = 2.37 m/s² downhill**. Force equation (1), number (1), direction/units (1).

d. Weight is Earth's gravitational force; normal is the ramp's contact force perpendicular to its surface. (1)

Check: without friction a would be 4.14 m/s² downhill; adding uphill friction reduces it. N is not mg on this incline.

## 9 — 8 points

a. Table block: N up, m₁g down, tension toward pulley. Hanging block: m₂g down, tension up. Diagrams (2). Positive toward pulley for m₁ and down for m₂:

**T = m₁a**, **m₂g − T = m₂a**. (1 each)

b. **a = m₂g/(m₁ + m₂) = 3.92 m/s²**, hanging block downward; **T = m₁a = 11.8 N**. Solving coupled equations (1), acceleration (1), tension (1).

c. The hanging block accelerates downward, so its net force is downward: m₂g exceeds T. (1)

Check: a < g and T < m₂g = 19.6 N; the same acceleration magnitude follows from the taut, inextensible string.

## 10 — 8 points

a. Normal force is tilted inward by the bank angle from vertical. Vertical balance **N cos θ = mg**; horizontal inward equation **N sin θ = mv²/r**. Force diagram/setup (2). Divide:

**v = √(rg tan θ) = 11.9 m/s** (2).

**a = v²/r = g tan θ = 3.57 m/s², horizontally toward the curve's center** (2, one for magnitude and one for direction).

The inward horizontal component of the road's normal force supplies the centripetal acceleration. There is no additional force called “centripetal force.” Do not deduct for omitting an unasked force label once the force setup is correct. Record whether Chris volunteers that physical agent; this test alone is not sufficient to close the previously recorded answer-completion habit.

b. Velocity includes direction; its direction changes continuously even though its magnitude is constant. (2)

Check: zero bank angle leads to zero friction-free design speed; larger bank angle permits higher speed at the same radius. Doubling speed at fixed radius quadruples the required inward acceleration.

## 11 — 6 points

b. **5.00 m/s**, **36.9° east of north** (or 53.1° north of east). (1 each)

c. Northward component sets crossing time: **120 m/(4.00 m/s) = 30.0 s**. Drift **(3.00 m/s)(30.0 s) = 90.0 m east**. (1 each)
