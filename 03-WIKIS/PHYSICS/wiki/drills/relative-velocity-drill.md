# Relative Velocity Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Choosing a reference frame and using vector addition to relate the velocity of
one object as measured by different observers.

## Prerequisites

[[../concepts/relative-velocity]], [[../concepts/vector-components]]

## Instructions

For every problem:

1. Define what each subscript means.
2. Write the vector equation before inserting numbers.
3. Draw the velocity triangle or component axes.
4. Report magnitude, direction, and units.

Use the cancellation pattern
`v_PA = v_PB + v_BA`: the inside `B` labels cancel, leaving `P` relative to
`A`. Reversing a subscript reverses the vector.

## Problems

**Problem 1 - Walking train.** A train moves east at 20.0 m/s relative to the
ground. A passenger walks east at 1.50 m/s relative to the train. Find the
passenger's velocity relative to the ground.

**Problem 2 - Walking against the train.** The same passenger walks west at
1.50 m/s relative to the train. Find the passenger's velocity relative to the
ground.

**Problem 3 - Boat across a river.** A boat points due north and moves at
4.00 m/s relative to the water. The water flows east at 3.00 m/s relative to
the shore. Find the boat's speed and direction relative to the shore.

**Problem 4 - Aim upstream.** The same boat must arrive directly north of its
starting point. At what angle west of north must it point, and what is its
resulting northward speed relative to the shore?

**Problem 5 - Aircraft wind.** An aircraft flies at 200 km/h due east relative
to the air. Wind moves at 50.0 km/h due south relative to the ground. Find the
aircraft's ground speed and direction.

## Check Yourself

1. `v_PG = v_PT + v_TG = 1.50 + 20.0 = 21.5 m/s east`.
2. With east positive, `v_PG = -1.50 + 20.0 = 18.5 m/s east`.
3. `v_BS = v_BW + v_WS`; magnitude `5.00 m/s`, direction
   `tan^-1(3/4) = 36.9 degrees east of north`.
4. The west component must be 3.00 m/s. With boat speed 4.00 m/s,
   `sin(theta) = 3/4`, so `theta = 48.6 degrees west of north`; northward
   speed `sqrt(4.00^2 - 3.00^2) = 2.65 m/s`.
5. Magnitude `sqrt(200^2 + 50.0^2) = 206 km/h`; direction
   `tan^-1(50/200) = 14.0 degrees south of east`.

## Mastery Signal

Chris writes the correct subscript equation before using numbers, chooses signs
from a stated coordinate system, and solves a two-dimensional wind or current
problem without prompting.
