# Drill — Polar ↔ Cartesian Conversion

## Skill Being Practiced

Converting between (x, y) and (r, θ) in both directions, with correct quadrant checking.

## Prerequisites

[[../concepts/coordinate-systems]], [[../equations/polar-cartesian-conversion]], right-triangle trig ([[../appendix/math-geometry-trig]]).

## Instructions

Sketch the point before calculating, every time. Report r as positive, and label θ as degrees or radians.

## Problems

1. Convert (x, y) = (3.00 m, 4.00 m) to polar coordinates.
2. Convert (x, y) = (−5.00 m, −5.00 m) to polar coordinates. (Watch the quadrant.)
3. Convert (r, θ) = (10.0 m, 60.0°) to Cartesian coordinates.
4. Convert (x, y) = (2.00 m, −6.00 m) to polar coordinates.
5. Convert (r, θ) = (8.00 m, 200.0°) to Cartesian coordinates. (Both components should come out negative — sketch first to confirm.)

## Check Yourself

1. r = 5.00 m, θ = 53.1°
2. r = 7.07 m, θ = 225.0° (calculator gives 45.0° — add 180° since x and y are both negative, third quadrant)
3. x = 5.00 m, y = 8.66 m
4. r = 6.32 m, θ = −71.6° (equivalently 288.4°) — fourth quadrant, x positive so no correction needed
5. x = −7.52 m, y = −2.74 m

## Mastery Signal

Chris gets all five correct without prompting, sketches every problem before calculating, and can explain out loud why problems 2 and 4 need (or don't need) the 180° quadrant correction.
