# Stress-Strain and Elastic Modulus Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Applying stress σ = F/A, strain ε = ΔL/L₀, and Young's modulus Y = σ/ε to compute deformation of materials under load.

## Prerequisites

[[../equations/youngs-modulus]], [[../concepts/stress-and-strain]]

## Instructions

For each problem: (1) identify the modulus type (Young's for tension/compression), (2) compute stress, (3) compute strain, (4) find the deformation. Convert all units to SI (Pa, N, m) before calculating.

## Problems

**1.** A steel rod (Y = 2.0 × 10¹¹ Pa) has cross-section 4.0 cm² and length 2.0 m. A tensile force of 80 000 N is applied along its axis. Find: (a) stress, (b) strain, (c) elongation ΔL.

**2.** A copper wire (Y = 1.2 × 10¹¹ Pa) of diameter 2.0 mm and length 4.0 m hangs vertically. A 25 kg mass is attached to the bottom. Find the elongation.

**3.** An aluminum column (Y = 7.0 × 10¹⁰ Pa) of cross-section 10 cm² and length 1.5 m supports a 5000 N load. Find the compression ΔL.

**4.** A bone has Y = 1.5 × 10¹⁰ Pa and cross-section 3.0 cm². A force of 4500 N compresses it. What strain does the bone undergo? Is this within the typical elastic limit of 1% strain?

## Answers

**1.** 
- (a) σ = F/A = 80 000 / (4.0 × 10⁻⁴) = **2.0 × 10⁸ Pa** (200 MPa)
- (b) ε = σ/Y = (2.0 × 10⁸) / (2.0 × 10¹¹) = **1.0 × 10⁻³** (0.1%)
- (c) ΔL = ε × L₀ = 1.0 × 10⁻³ × 2.0 = **0.0020 m = 2.0 mm**

**2.** 
A = π(0.001)² = 3.14 × 10⁻⁶ m². F = mg = 25 × 9.80 = 245 N. σ = 245/(3.14×10⁻⁶) = 7.80×10⁷ Pa. ε = 7.80×10⁷/1.2×10¹¹ = 6.5×10⁻⁴. ΔL = 6.5×10⁻⁴ × 4.0 = **0.0026 m = 2.6 mm**

**3.**
A = 10 × 10⁻⁴ m² = 10⁻³ m². σ = 5000/10⁻³ = 5.0×10⁶ Pa. ε = 5.0×10⁶/7.0×10¹⁰ = 7.1×10⁻⁵. ΔL = 7.1×10⁻⁵ × 1.5 = **1.1 × 10⁻⁴ m = 0.11 mm**

**4.**
A = 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ m². σ = 4500/(3.0×10⁻⁴) = 1.5×10⁷ Pa. ε = 1.5×10⁷/1.5×10¹⁰ = **1.0 × 10⁻³ = 0.1%**. Yes, well within the elastic limit of 1%.

## Mastery Signal

Chris can correctly compute all three quantities (stress, strain, deformation) in under 2 minutes per problem and distinguish when to use Young's (tensile/compressive) vs. shear or bulk modulus.
