# Work Calculation Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Computing work done by a constant force using W = Fd cos θ, and recognizing when work is zero.

## Prerequisites

[[../concepts/work]], [[../equations/work-constant-force]]

## Instructions

For each problem: (1) draw a quick diagram labeling F, d, and θ, (2) state the sign of the work before calculating, (3) compute W, (4) check units.

## Problems

**1.** A person pulls a crate horizontally with a force of 50 N for 8.0 m. The rope is horizontal. How much work does the person do?

**2.** A mover pushes a box with 60 N along a ramp that makes 20° with horizontal. The mover's force is directed along the ramp. The box moves 5.0 m along the ramp. How much work does the mover do?

**3.** A 12 kg box is carried horizontally at constant speed for 6.0 m. How much work does gravity do on the box? How much work does the person carrying it do against gravity?

**4.** A horse pulls a barge with a rope making 30° with the direction of motion. The tension in the rope is 200 N and the barge moves 100 m. How much work does the rope tension do?

**5.** A dog pulls on a leash with 45 N at 25° above horizontal while walking 3.0 m forward. Calculate (a) the work done by the dog on the collar, (b) the work done by gravity on the dog during the same walk.

## Answers

1. W = 50 × 8.0 × cos 0° = **400 J**
2. W = 60 × 5.0 × cos 0° = **300 J** (force and displacement both along the ramp → θ = 0°)
3. W_gravity = 0 J (gravity is vertical, displacement is horizontal → θ = 90°). Work done against gravity = 0 J — no vertical component of displacement.
4. W = 200 × 100 × cos 30° = 20 000 × 0.866 = **17 320 J ≈ 17 300 J**
5. (a) W = 45 × 3.0 × cos 25° = 135 × 0.906 = **122 J**; (b) W_gravity = 0 (horizontal displacement, so θ = 90° for gravity).

## Mastery Signal

Chris can correctly identify θ in each situation without confusion and calculate work in under 90 seconds per problem.
