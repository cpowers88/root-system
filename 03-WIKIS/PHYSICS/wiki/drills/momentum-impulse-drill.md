# Momentum and Impulse Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Calculating momentum, applying the impulse-momentum theorem, and reading a F-t graph for impulse.

## Prerequisites

[[../concepts/linear-momentum]], [[../concepts/impulse]], [[../equations/impulse-momentum-theorem]]

## Instructions

For each problem, draw a brief diagram, define your positive direction, write the relevant equation, substitute values with units, and check units in the final answer.

## Problems

### Part A — Momentum

1. A 0.145 kg baseball moves at 40.0 m/s. What is its momentum?

2. A 1500 kg car moves at 25.0 m/s east, and a 0.200 kg ball moves at 300 m/s east. Which has more momentum?

3. A 70.0 kg sprinter starts from rest and reaches 10.0 m/s. What is the change in momentum?

### Part B — Impulse-Momentum Theorem

4. A 0.145 kg baseball (initially at rest) is hit by a bat and leaves at 45.0 m/s. What impulse did the bat deliver?

5. A 1200 kg car going 25 m/s brakes to a stop in 4.0 s. What is the average braking force?

6. A soccer ball of mass 0.43 kg is kicked with an average force of 1200 N over a 0.010 s contact time. If the ball was at rest before the kick, what is its final speed?

### Part C — F-t Graphs

7. A force acts on a 2.0 kg object. The F-t graph shows a triangular pulse: force rises linearly from 0 N at t = 0 to 100 N at t = 0.5 s, then drops back to 0. What is the impulse? What is the final speed if the object started at rest?

8. A constant 50 N force acts for 3.0 s, then a constant 100 N force in the opposite direction acts for 1.5 s. What is the net impulse?

### Part D — Challenge

9. A 0.060 kg tennis ball traveling at 28 m/s hits a wall perpendicularly and bounces back at 22 m/s. (a) What is the change in momentum? (b) If the contact time is 5.0 ms, what was the average force the wall exerted?

## Check Yourself

Problem 1: p = (0.145)(40.0) = 5.80 kg·m/s
Problem 5: F = Δp/Δt = (1200)(25)/4.0 = 7500 N
Problem 6: vf = Ft/m = (1200)(0.010)/0.43 = 27.9 m/s
Problem 9a: Δp = m(vf − vi) = 0.060(−22 − 28) = −3.0 kg·m/s (if "toward wall" is positive)

## Mastery Signal

Chris can calculate impulse from both F·Δt and the area under a F-t graph, and use the impulse-momentum theorem to find unknown forces or final speeds — in under 3 minutes per problem.
