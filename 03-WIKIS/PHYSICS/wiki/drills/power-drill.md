# Power Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Calculating power, converting between forms of the power equation, and connecting power to the work-energy theorem.

## Prerequisites

Stage 8 — Conservation of Energy; [[../equations/work-energy-theorem]], [[../equations/power]]

## Instructions

For each problem, identify what is given, write the relevant equation, substitute with units, and sanity-check the answer. Express answers in watts (W) or kilowatts (kW) as appropriate.

## Problems

### Part A — Basic Power

1. A crane lifts a 500 kg load 20.0 m in 40.0 s. What is the average power of the crane?

2. A motor does 12,000 J of work in 3.0 minutes. What is its power output in watts? In kilowatts?

3. A runner exerts an average force of 400 N and maintains a speed of 5.0 m/s. What is her power output?

### Part B — Power and Speed (P = Fv)

4. A car engine produces 150 hp (1 hp = 746 W). What is the maximum thrust force available at a constant speed of 30 m/s?

5. A cyclist generates 250 W of power and rides at a constant speed (net force = 0). If air resistance and friction provide a total retarding force of 40 N, what is the cyclist's speed?

### Part C — Power and Energy

6. A 60 W lightbulb runs for 8 hours. How much energy does it use in joules? In kilowatt-hours?

7. A pump delivers 3.0 kW of power and fills a tank in 20.0 minutes. How much work (energy) does the pump do in that time?

### Part D — Gravitational Power

8. A 1200 kg elevator rises 30.0 m in 18.0 s. What minimum power must the motor provide? (Ignore cable mass and friction.)

9. A waterfall drops 60.0 m and has a flow rate of 500 kg/s. What is the available power? (How much of this can actually be captured is a separate question — this is the theoretical maximum.)

## Check Yourself

1. W = mgh = (500)(9.8)(20) = 98,000 J; P = W/t = 98,000/40 = 2450 W = 2.45 kW
2. P = 12,000 J / 180 s = 66.7 W = 0.0667 kW
3. P = Fv = (400)(5.0) = 2000 W = 2.0 kW
4. P = 150 hp × 746 W/hp = 111,900 W. F = P/v = 111,900/30 = 3730 N
5. P = Fv → v = P/F = 250/40 = 6.25 m/s
6. E = Pt = (60 W)(8 h × 3600 s/h) = 1,728,000 J = 1.728 MJ. In kWh: E = (60 W)(8 h) = 480 Wh = 0.48 kWh
7. W = Pt = (3000 W)(20.0 × 60 s) = 3,600,000 J = 3.6 MJ
8. P = mgh/t = (1200)(9.8)(30.0)/18.0 = 352,800/18.0 = 19,600 W ≈ 19.6 kW
9. P = (dm/dt)gh = (500 kg/s)(9.8)(60.0) = 294,000 W = 294 kW

## Mastery Signal

Chris can move between P = W/t and P = Fv without confusion, and convert between watts, kilowatts, and horsepower.
