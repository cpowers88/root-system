# SHM Energy Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Using energy conservation in simple harmonic motion to find speed, amplitude, or position; understanding how energy distributes between kinetic and potential during oscillation.

## Prerequisites

[[../concepts/shm-energy]], [[../equations/shm-energy]], [[../equations/shm-position]]

## Instructions

For each problem, draw an energy bar chart (KE + PE at the stated position), write the energy equation, and solve. Always check that KE ≥ 0 (speed must be real).

## Energy Equations

```
E_total = ½kA² = ½mv_max²

At any position x:
½mv² + ½kx² = ½kA²
v = √[(k/m)(A² − x²)] = ω√(A² − x²)
```

## Problems

### Part A — Speed at Given Position

1. A spring-mass system: k = 40 N/m, m = 0.80 kg, A = 0.30 m. Find the speed at x = 0.20 m.

2. Same system as Problem 1. Find the speed at x = 0 (equilibrium). This should give v_max.

3. A 0.50 kg mass on a spring (k = 100 N/m) has A = 0.12 m. Find the speed when PE = KE (i.e., when ½kx² = ½mv²).

### Part B — Amplitude from Initial Conditions

4. A 0.30 kg mass is attached to a spring (k = 50 N/m). At t = 0, x = 0.05 m and v = 0.80 m/s. Find the amplitude A.

5. A mass on a spring (k = 200 N/m, m = 2.0 kg) is at x = 0.10 m with speed 0.50 m/s. Find A.

### Part C — Total Energy

6. For the system in Problem 1, calculate the total energy E_total.

7. If the amplitude of that system is doubled to 0.60 m (by stretching it farther before release), what happens to the total energy? What happens to the maximum speed?

### Part D — Energy Distribution

8. At what position x (as a fraction of A) does the oscillator have equal KE and PE?

9. A 1.0 kg mass on a spring (k = 25 N/m, A = 0.40 m) is at x = 0.30 m. Calculate both KE and PE at this position and confirm they sum to E_total.

### Part E — Challenge

10. A spring-mass system (m = 0.25 kg, k = 100 N/m) is released from rest at x = 0.20 m. Find: (a) total energy, (b) maximum speed, (c) speed at x = 0.10 m, (d) position where speed equals half of maximum.

## Check Yourself

Problem 1: E = ½(40)(0.30)² = 1.80 J; v at x = 0.20: ½(0.80)v² = 1.80 − ½(40)(0.04) = 1.80 − 0.80 = 1.00 J → v = √(2.5) = 1.58 m/s
Problem 8: Equal KE and PE → ½kx² = ½(½kA²) → x = A/√2 ≈ 0.707A

## Mastery Signal

Chris can find the speed at any position in SHM using energy conservation, find amplitude from initial conditions, and identify where kinetic and potential energies are equal — without using trig functions.
