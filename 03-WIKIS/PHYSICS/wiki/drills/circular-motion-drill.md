# Circular Motion Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Applying a_c = v²/r, F_c = mv²/r, and T = 2πr/v to find centripetal acceleration, force, speed, or period in uniform circular motion problems.

## Prerequisites

[[../concepts/uniform-circular-motion]], [[../equations/centripetal-acceleration]], [[../problem-types/circular-motion]]

## Instructions

For each problem: (1) identify the radius r; (2) identify what provides the centripetal force; (3) apply F_c = mv²/r; (4) check units. Draw a diagram showing the circular path, the velocity direction, and the centripetal acceleration direction.

---

## Problems

**Problem 1 — Basic centripetal acceleration**
A car rounds a flat curve of radius 45.0 m at 15.0 m/s. What is the centripetal acceleration?

**Problem 2 — Centripetal force from friction**
The car in Problem 1 has a mass of 1200 kg. What minimum friction force is required to keep it on the circular path? What is the minimum coefficient of static friction needed?

**Problem 3 — Period and speed**
A satellite orbits Earth at an altitude where its orbital radius is 6.80 × 10⁶ m and its orbital speed is 7650 m/s. Find the orbital period.

**Problem 4 — Finding speed from centripetal acceleration**
A ball on a 0.80 m string is swung in a horizontal circle. If the string can exert a maximum tension of 25.0 N and the ball's mass is 0.300 kg, what is the maximum speed of the ball?

---

## Solutions

**Problem 1:**
```
a_c = v²/r = (15.0)²/45.0 = 225/45.0 = 5.00 m/s²
Direction: toward center of the curve.
```

**Problem 2:**
```
F_c = ma_c = 1200 × 5.00 = 6000 N
This friction must be at least 6000 N (static friction provides centripetal force on flat road).
μs_min: f = μs N = μs mg → μs = a_c/g = 5.00/9.80 = 0.510
```

**Problem 3:**
```
T = 2πr/v = 2π(6.80×10⁶)/7650
  = (2π × 6.80×10⁶)/7650
  = 4.272×10⁷/7650
  = 5585 s  ≈  93.1 minutes
```

**Problem 4:**
```
Maximum tension provides centripetal force:
T = mv²/r
v² = Tr/m = (25.0)(0.80)/(0.300) = 20.0/0.300 = 66.7 m²/s²
v = √66.7 = 8.16 m/s
```

## Mastery Signal

Chris can identify what force provides centripetal acceleration in each setup (friction, tension, gravity, normal) and correctly calculate speed, force, or acceleration without confusing r with diameter or mixing up which formula to use.
