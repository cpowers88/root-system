# Drill — Tangential and Radial Acceleration

## Skill Being Practiced

Splitting total acceleration on a curved path into radial (centripetal) and tangential components, and combining them correctly.

## Prerequisites

[[../concepts/tangential-and-radial-acceleration]], [[../concepts/uniform-circular-motion]], [[../equations/tangential-and-radial-acceleration]]

## Instructions

For each problem: sketch the path, draw a_r and a_t as perpendicular arrows, compute each, then combine with the Pythagorean theorem for the total.

## Problems

**Problem 1 — Basic split**
A car rounds a curve of radius 60.0 m at an instantaneous speed of 12.0 m/s, while speeding up at a rate of 2.00 m/s². Find a_r, a_t, and the total acceleration magnitude.

**Problem 2 — Slowing down**
A roller coaster car at the bottom of a loop (radius 10.0 m) has a speed of 15.0 m/s and is decelerating at 3.00 m/s² at that instant. Find the total acceleration magnitude and describe the direction of a_t relative to v.

**Problem 3 — Recognize the special case**
A satellite moves in a circular orbit at constant speed 7000 m/s, radius 7.0×10⁶ m. Find a_t and explain why it has that value without calculating a_r first.

## Check Yourself

1. a_r = v²/r = 144/60.0 = 2.40 m/s². a_t = 2.00 m/s² (given). a = √(2.40² + 2.00²) = √(5.76+4.00) = √9.76 ≈ 3.12 m/s².
2. a_r = v²/r = 225/10.0 = 22.5 m/s². a_t = 3.00 m/s² (magnitude; direction opposite v since decelerating). a = √(22.5² + 3.00²) = √(506.25+9.00) = √515.25 ≈ 22.7 m/s².
3. a_t = 0, because "constant speed" means dv/dt = 0 by definition — no need to compute anything else to know this.

## Mastery Signal

Chris identifies immediately whether a_t is zero (constant speed) or nonzero (speed changing) from the problem wording, computes both components correctly, and combines them with √(a_r²+a_t²) rather than simple addition.
