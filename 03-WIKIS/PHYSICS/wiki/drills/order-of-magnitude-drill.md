# Order-of-Magnitude Estimation Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Breaking a vague real-world question into a chain of reasonable assumptions to produce a power-of-ten estimate.

## Prerequisites

[[../concepts/order-of-magnitude-estimation]]

## Instructions

For each prompt, state your assumptions explicitly, then estimate to the nearest power of ten.

## Problems

1. Estimate the number of times your heart beats in your lifetime.
2. Estimate the number of grains of sand on a typical beach.
3. Estimate the number of breaths you've taken so far in your life.
4. Estimate the volume of water in an Olympic swimming pool, in cubic meters.
5. Estimate the number of cars in a mid-size city.

## Check Yourself

There's no single "correct" number — check that your chain of assumptions is reasonable and that the units work out to the right final quantity (e.g., problem 1 should end in "beats," not "beats per minute").

## Mastery Signal

Chris produces a full order-of-magnitude estimate, with stated assumptions, in under 5 minutes without searching for a real answer online.
