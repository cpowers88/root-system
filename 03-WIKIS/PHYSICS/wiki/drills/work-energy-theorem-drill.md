# Work-Energy Theorem Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Applying W_net = ΔK to find final speed, stopping distance, or work done by a specific force.

## Prerequisites

[[../equations/work-energy-theorem]], [[../equations/kinetic-energy]], [[../concepts/work]]

## Instructions

For each problem: (1) identify the system, (2) list all forces and compute each force's work, (3) compute W_net, (4) apply W_net = ΔK, (5) solve for the unknown, (6) check units.

## Problems

**1.** A 3.0 kg object is initially at rest. A net horizontal force of 18 N acts on it for 5.0 m on a frictionless surface. Find the final speed.

**2.** A 1500 kg car traveling at 25 m/s brakes to a stop over 60 m. What is the magnitude of the average braking (friction) force?

**3.** A 0.50 kg ball is thrown downward from a height. It starts at 2.0 m/s and reaches 6.0 m/s just before hitting the floor. Ignoring air resistance, through what vertical distance did it fall? (Use g = 9.80 m/s².)

**4.** A horizontal push of 40 N moves a 6.0 kg box 4.0 m on a rough floor. Friction is 15 N opposing motion. The box starts from rest. Find the final speed.

**5.** A 0.25 kg hockey puck slides on nearly frictionless ice at 8.0 m/s. A player hits it and it reaches 22 m/s. How much work did the stick do?

**6.** A 2.0 kg particle moves along the x-axis. At x = 0 it has v = 4.0 m/s. A net force F_x = 3x N acts on it (varies with position). Find its speed at x = 3.0 m using W = ∫F dx.

## Answers

1. W_net = 18 × 5.0 = 90 J; K_i = 0; ½(3.0)v_f² = 90 → v_f = **7.7 m/s**

2. K_i = ½(1500)(25²) = 468 750 J; K_f = 0; W_net = −468 750 J = −F × 60 → F = **7 810 N ≈ 7 800 N**

3. W_gravity = mg × h = 0.50 × 9.80 × h; ΔK = ½(0.50)(36 − 4) = 8.0 J; 4.9h = 8.0 → h = **1.6 m**

4. W_push = 40 × 4.0 = 160 J; W_friction = −15 × 4.0 = −60 J; W_net = 100 J; ½(6.0)v_f² = 100 → v_f = **5.8 m/s**

5. W_net = ΔK = ½(0.25)(22² − 8²) = ½(0.25)(484 − 64) = ½(0.25)(420) = **52.5 J**

6. W = ∫₀³ 3x dx = 3[x²/2]₀³ = 3(9/2) = 13.5 J; K_i = ½(2.0)(4.0²) = 16 J; K_f = 16 + 13.5 = 29.5 J; ½(2.0)v_f² = 29.5 → v_f = **5.4 m/s**

## Mastery Signal

Chris can set up and solve all six problems independently, correctly summing work from multiple forces and handling the integral in Problem 6 using the power rule.
