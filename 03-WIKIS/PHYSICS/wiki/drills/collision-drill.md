# Collision Drill

## Skill Being Practiced

Classifying collisions, applying momentum conservation, solving 1D and 2D collision problems, and checking whether kinetic energy is conserved.

## Prerequisites

[[../concepts/collision-types]], [[../concepts/conservation-of-momentum]], [[../equations/collision-equations]], [[../problem-types/perfectly-inelastic-collision]], [[../problem-types/elastic-collision]]

## Instructions

For every problem: (1) Draw a before-and-after diagram with labeled masses and velocities. (2) Define positive direction. (3) Identify collision type. (4) Write the correct equation(s). (5) Solve with units. (6) For inelastic collisions, calculate the kinetic energy lost as a check.

## Problems

### Part A — Perfectly Inelastic Collisions (1D)

1. A 2.0 kg block moving at 6.0 m/s to the right collides with a 3.0 kg block at rest. They stick together. What is their final velocity?

2. A 10,000 kg train car moving at 5.0 m/s collides with and couples to a 7,000 kg car at rest. Find the final speed of the combined cars.

3. Two clay balls: 0.50 kg at 4.0 m/s east and 0.30 kg at 2.0 m/s west. They collide and stick. Find the final velocity (magnitude and direction).

### Part B — Elastic Collisions (1D, one at rest)

4. A 1.0 kg ball moving at 3.0 m/s strikes a 2.0 kg ball at rest elastically. Find both final velocities.

5. A 4.0 kg ball moving at 5.0 m/s strikes a 1.0 kg ball at rest elastically. Find both final velocities. What does the lighter ball do?

6. Two identical 0.50 kg balls. Ball 1 moves at 8.0 m/s; Ball 2 is at rest. They collide elastically. What happens?

### Part C — Elastic Collisions (1D, both moving)

7. Ball A (3.0 kg, 2.0 m/s east) collides elastically with Ball B (1.0 kg, −4.0 m/s west). Use momentum conservation and kinetic energy conservation simultaneously.

### Part D — 2D Collisions

8. A 0.50 kg puck moving at 6.0 m/s in the +x direction collides with a 0.50 kg puck at rest. After the collision, puck 1 moves at 30° above the +x axis at 4.0 m/s. Find puck 2's speed and direction.

9. A 2000 kg truck moving east at 15 m/s collides at an intersection with a 1500 kg car moving north at 20 m/s. They lock together (perfectly inelastic). Find the magnitude and direction of the final velocity.

### Part E — Energy Check

10. For problem 1: Calculate the kinetic energy before and after. How much kinetic energy was lost? Where did it go?

## Check Yourself

Problem 1: vf = (2.0)(6.0)/(2.0 + 3.0) = 2.4 m/s →
Problem 4: v₁f = [(1−2)/(1+2)](3.0) = −1.0 m/s; v₂f = [2(1)/(1+2)](3.0) = 2.0 m/s
Problem 6: Identical masses → Ball 1 stops completely, Ball 2 moves at 8.0 m/s. (Classic Newton's Cradle result.)
Problem 9: px = 2000(15) = 30,000 kg·m/s east; py = 1500(20) = 30,000 kg·m/s north; vf = 30,000√2 / 3500 = 12.1 m/s at 45° NE

## Mastery Signal

Chris can identify the collision type from the problem statement, write the correct conservation equations without prompting, and solve for all unknowns — including 2D cases — in under 5 minutes per problem.
