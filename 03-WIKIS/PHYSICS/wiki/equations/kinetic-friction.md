# Equation — Kinetic Friction

## Equation

$$f_k = \mu_k n$$

## Plain-English Meaning

The kinetic (sliding) friction force equals the kinetic coefficient of friction times the normal force. It acts exactly opposite to the direction of motion. It is a fixed value — it does not vary with speed (within the scope of this course).

## Variables

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| f_k | kinetic friction force | N |
| μ_k | kinetic coefficient of friction | dimensionless (no units) |
| n | normal force (surface pushing perpendicular to itself on the object) | N |

## When to Use

- When the object is already sliding (in motion) on a surface.
- μ_k is given or can be looked up for the material pair.
- Kinetic friction always opposes the direction of motion — pick the correct sign in your ΣF equation.

## When NOT to Use

- When the object is stationary — use f_s ≤ μ_s n (static friction) instead.
- When the surface is frictionless (μ_k = 0, so f_k = 0 anyway, but the problem should say "smooth" or "frictionless").

## Assumptions

- The contact area and speed don't affect the friction (approximately true for most rigid surfaces in intro physics).
- μ_k < μ_s always — once an object starts moving, less friction holds it than was needed to start it.

## Calculus Origin

None in this stage. Friction is treated as a constant force here. In Ch 6 (Stage 6), resistive forces that vary with velocity appear (air drag).

## Example

A 10 kg crate slides across a floor with μ_k = 0.30.
n = mg = 10 × 9.80 = 98 N.
f_k = 0.30 × 98 = 29.4 N, directed opposite to motion.

## Common Mistake

Using the weight (mg) instead of the normal force (n) in the formula. On a flat horizontal surface with no other vertical forces, n = mg — but on an incline, n = mg cosθ, which is less than mg. Always find n first from ΣF_⊥ = 0.
