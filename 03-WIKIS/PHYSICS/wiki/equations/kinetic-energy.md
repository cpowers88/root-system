# Kinetic Energy

## Equation

```text
K = ½mv²
```

## Meaning in Plain English

Kinetic energy is the energy an object has because it is moving. It depends on both mass and the *square* of speed — doubling the speed quadruples the kinetic energy.

## Variables

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| K | kinetic energy | J (joule = kg·m²/s²) |
| m | mass of the object | kg |
| v | speed (magnitude of velocity) | m/s |

## Units Check

[K] = kg × (m/s)² = kg·m²/s² = J ✓

## Sign Convention

K is always ≥ 0. Mass is positive; v² is always positive. Kinetic energy is never negative.

## When to Use It

- Any time you know speed and need energy (or know energy and need speed).
- As the "before" or "after" energy in the work-energy theorem W_net = ΔK.
- As part of total mechanical energy in Stage 8 (conservation of energy).

## When Not to Use It

- When the object is rotating but not translating — use rotational KE K_rot = ½Iω² (Stage 10).
- For a rolling object, both translational K = ½mv² and rotational K = ½Iω² contribute.

## Required Assumptions

Object treated as a particle (no internal rotation or vibration). Uses instantaneous speed.

## Calculus Origin

K = ½mv² comes from integrating Newton's second law (work-energy theorem derivation):

```text
W = ∫F dx = ∫ma dx = ∫m(dv/dt) dx = ∫m(dv/dx)(dx/dt) dx = ∫mv dv = ½mv²
```

## Example Problem Type

"A 1200 kg car moves at 28 m/s. What is its kinetic energy?"
→ K = ½(1200)(28²) = ½(1200)(784) = 470 400 J ≈ 4.7 × 10⁵ J.

## Common Mistake

Using v instead of v²: K ≠ mv/2. The ½v² is NOT the same as ½ × v. Always square the speed first.
