# Equation — Weight

## Equation

$$w = mg$$

or in vector form (pointing downward):

$$\vec{w} = -mg\hat{j}$$

## Plain-English Meaning

Weight is the gravitational force that Earth (or another planet) exerts on an object. It equals mass times the local gravitational field strength. On Earth's surface, g ≈ 9.80 m/s² downward.

## Variables

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| w | weight (gravitational force) | N |
| m | mass | kg |
| g | gravitational acceleration at Earth's surface | 9.80 m/s² (downward) |

## When to Use

- Whenever you need the gravitational force on an object near Earth's surface.
- In FBDs: this is the "weight arrow" pointing straight down.
- Also the starting point for normal force calculations on inclines: n = mg cosθ.

## When NOT to Use

- Far from Earth's surface: g changes with altitude (Ch 13, Stage 13). For orbit problems, use Newton's law of universal gravitation instead.
- For objects in free fall or space, be careful: weight still exists (gravity still pulls), but the object is also accelerating, so it appears "weightless."

## Assumptions

- Near Earth's surface where g is approximately constant at 9.80 m/s².
- "g" is the magnitude; the direction is always straight toward Earth's center (downward for our problems).

## Calculus Origin

None. This is a direct proportionality. The deeper explanation — why g = GM_Earth/R_Earth² — comes from Ch 13 (Stage 13).

## Example

A 70 kg person on Earth: w = 70 × 9.80 = 686 N downward.
Same person on the Moon (g_Moon ≈ 1.62 m/s²): w_Moon = 70 × 1.62 = 113 N. Mass is unchanged at 70 kg.

## Common Mistake

Using mass (70 kg) instead of weight (686 N) in Newton's Second Law problems. When the equation calls for a force, use newtons — always convert using w = mg before plugging in.
