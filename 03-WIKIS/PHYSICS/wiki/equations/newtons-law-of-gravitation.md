# Equation — Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

## Equation

```
F_g = G × m₁ × m₂ / r²

Direction: attractive — along the line connecting the two centers, toward each other.
```

## Plain-English Meaning

Every object with mass attracts every other object with mass. The force grows with more mass and shrinks with the square of distance (inverse-square law). Doubling the distance between objects reduces the gravitational force to one-fourth.

## Variables

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| F_g | gravitational force between the two objects | N |
| G | universal gravitational constant = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ | N·m²/kg² |
| m₁, m₂ | masses of the two objects | kg |
| r | distance between the centers of mass | m |

## Important: r is center-to-center distance

If an object is 200 km above Earth's surface, r = R_Earth + 200 km = 6,571 km. The 200 km alone is wrong.

## Connection to g = 9.8 m/s²

Near Earth's surface: F_g = m × g_surface, where g_surface = GM_E/R_E² ≈ 9.8 m/s². At any altitude: g = GM_E/(R_E + h)² — a variable, not a fixed constant.

## When to Use

- Finding the gravitational force between any two masses (planet-moon, Earth-satellite, two billiard balls)
- Orbital mechanics (Newton's 2nd law + F_g provides centripetal force)
- Gravitational field strength at any distance from a massive object

## Common Mistake

Confusing r (center-to-center distance) with h (height above the surface). Also: forgetting to square r — F_g ∝ 1/r², not 1/r.
