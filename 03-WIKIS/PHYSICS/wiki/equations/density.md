# Density

## Equation

```text
ρ = m / V
```

## Meaning in Plain English

Density tells you how tightly mass is packed into a given amount of space. A small, heavy object (like a lead ball) has high density; a large, light object (like a styrofoam block of the same mass) has low density.

## Variables

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| ρ | density | kg/m³ |
| m | mass | kg |
| V | volume | m³ |

## Units Check

[ρ] = M/L³. Confirm: kg ÷ m³ = kg/m³. ✓ Matches.

## When to Use It

Comparing materials by how "heavy for their size" they are; finding the mass of a uniform object when you know its volume (or vice versa).

## When Not to Use It

When the object is hollow, made of mixed materials, or has air pockets — plain ρ = m/V then gives an average that may not be physically meaningful without extra care (e.g., a hollow sphere needs the mass and volume of just the shell material).

## Required Assumptions

The substance is uniform (homogeneous) throughout its volume.

## Calculus Origin

None — density is a ratio, not a rate, so no derivative or integral is involved.

## Example Problem Type

Given the mass and volume of a uniform block, find its density and identify the material; or given density and one of mass/volume, solve for the other.

## Common Mistake

Confusing density (kg/m³, a property of the material) with mass (kg, a property of a specific object) or weight (a force, not introduced until Ch. 5).
