# Equation — Young's Modulus (Tensile/Compressive Elasticity)

## Equation

```
Y = (F/A) / (ΔL/L₀)

Rearranged to find deformation:
ΔL = (F × L₀) / (A × Y)
```

## Plain-English Meaning

Young's modulus Y measures how stiff a material is when pulled or compressed along one axis. A large Y means the material barely stretches under large forces (steel). A small Y means it stretches a lot under small forces (rubber).

- F/A = stress (force per unit area, like pressure but applied along a direction)
- ΔL/L₀ = tensile strain (how much it stretched, as a fraction of original length — dimensionless)

## Variables

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Y | Young's modulus | Pa = N/m² |
| F | applied force (along the axis) | N |
| A | cross-sectional area (perpendicular to force) | m² |
| L₀ | original length | m |
| ΔL | change in length (positive = stretch, negative = compression) | m |

## Typical Values

| Material | Y (GPa) |
|---|---|
| Steel | ~200 |
| Aluminum | ~70 |
| Bone | ~18 |
| Rubber | ~0.01 |

## When to Use

When a problem asks how much a rod, wire, bone, or beam changes length under a tensile (stretching) or compressive (squishing) force applied along its length.

## Common Mistake

Using Young's modulus for a shear (side-sliding) deformation — shear modulus S applies there. Young's modulus only applies to forces along the long axis of the object.
