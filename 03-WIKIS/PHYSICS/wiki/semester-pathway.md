## What the real syllabus changed — read this before using the tables

Five things moved. Two of them change *what capability is worth building*, which
matters more than the date corrections.

### 1. Scope shrank. Universal Gravitation is off the course.

**The printed schedule never reaches Chapter 13.** Week 4's "Gravitational Force and
Free-Body Diagram (5.5, 5.7)" is §5.5 *The Gravitational Force and Weight* — mg in a
free-body diagram — **not** universal gravitation, orbits, or the inverse-square law.

**Active-path scope is now Chapters 1–12, 15, and 16.1–16.3.** Against the old plan:
**Ch 13 out, Ch 17 out, Ch 16 trimmed** to its first three sections. The previous
"Chapters 1–13 and 15–17" target was inherited from the neighbour capture and
overstated the course by roughly two chapters.

[[stages/stage-13-universal-gravitation]] and [[stages/stage-17-superposition]] move to
**durable reference** — same standing as Stage 14 fluids and Stage 18 relativity. The
Aug 19 prep day aimed at gravitational potential is **off the exam path**; it stays
real capability for later ISYE and Physics II work, so it is redirected, not deleted.
See § Phase 1.

### 2. The equation sheet is supplied. Formula recall is not what is being tested.

> *"The equation sheet will be provided for all the exams."* — Evaluation and Grading Policy

Exams are closed-book **with an equation sheet available**. The syllabus says so twice
(the Requirements section words it as bringing one page of the equation sheet; either
way, the formulas are in the room).

**This is the largest teaching consequence in the document, and it points the same
direction Chris's profile already does.** Cue-dependent recall of arbitrary formulas is
his known weak channel (`CHRIS_CORE.md` § 3) — and the course has removed that burden
from the graded surface. Therefore:

| Low value now | High value now |
|---|---|
| Drilling formula memorisation | **Recognising which situation is which**, cold |
| Reciting equations from blank paper | **Choosing the right model from a worded problem** |
| Flashcards as a primary tool | **Setup, symbolic derivation, and execution under a clock** |

This does **not** relax [[current-position]]'s derivation reps. Deriving is how the
connection to the physics gets built — this hub's identified gap. What changes is that
derivation is now training for *selection and setup*, not insurance against a blank
formula sheet. The exams reward a student who classifies fast.

### 3. AI is explicitly permitted as a tutor. It is prohibited in submitted work.

Section 54's policy is now stated, and it is **not** blanket prohibition:

- **Permitted** — reviewing explanations of course concepts; practising problem-solving
  technique with AI *"as a tutoring resource to guide you through the problem-solving
  process, rather than to generate final answers"*; exploring examples and seeking
  clarification on difficult topics.
- **Prohibited** — AI-generated content submitted as part of any graded assignment,
  quiz, lab, project, or exam; copying or paraphrasing AI text into submitted work;
  using AI to complete problems or write explanations submitted as his own.
- Unacknowledged AI-assisted submission is treated as another person's work under the
  KSU Code of Academic Integrity.

**This is exactly the engine this hub already runs**, and the "most restrictive until
verified" holding position retires for PHYS. **WebAssign homework is graded, so AI must
never produce a WebAssign answer** — teach the method, Chris solves it himself. That
shape does not change.

### 4. Three quarters of the grade is four closed-book sittings.

| Component | Weight | Notes |
|---|---:|---|
| Unit Exams (4) | **45%** | Closed book. **Lowest of the four is dropped** → three count, 15% each |
| Final Exam | **30%** | Closed book, **comprehensive**, mandatory, never rescheduled |
| Homework (WebAssign) | 10% | Weekly. Lowest dropped |
| Recitation Worksheet | 10% | Lowest dropped |
| Quizzes | 5% | Unannounced in-class + WebAssign/D2L reading quizzes. Lowest dropped |

**Exams + final = 75%.** Scale is standard (90/80/70/60) and the instructor rounds up at
≥ .1 — an 89.1 is an A, an 89.0 is a B.

**There is no attendance component.** The neighbour section's 7.5% attendance + 7.5%
recitation does not exist here. Attendance is *expected* in prose and enforced
indirectly: the 10% recitation worksheet is produced in the Friday breakout, and
in-class quizzes are unannounced. Missing Friday costs worksheet points, not attendance
points.

**A missed unit exam becomes the dropped one** — there are no make-ups. That is a real
buffer worth naming: one exam can be lost to a family collision without a grade
consequence, and exactly one.

### 5. Pre-class reading is graded, so the one-week-ahead rule is now load-bearing.

> *"To prepare for each class, students are required to read or watch the assigned
> materials in advance."*

Reading quizzes run through WebAssign and D2L, and in-class quizzes are unannounced.
The lead is no longer only a strategic advantage — **arriving unread is directly
scorable.**

### The one-week-ahead rule, and when to break it

Break the lead and work current-week material if any of these is true:

1. **An exam is inside seven days** — switch to retrieval. The sweep weeks above are this
   rule pre-applied to the four real dates.
2. **A cold check on the current week fails.** The lead is worthless if the foundation
   moved.
3. **WebAssign is due and unfinished.** Graded work outranks the lead — and see the
   extension rule below, which is the version of this that actually bites.

Otherwise hold the lead. Losing it once is recoverable; the recovery is one consolidation
week, taken deliberately, not by drifting.
