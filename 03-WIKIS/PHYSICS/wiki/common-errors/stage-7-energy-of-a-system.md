# Common Errors — Stage 7: Energy of a System

1. **Forgetting the cosine angle in work.** W = Fd cos θ — not just Fd. If F and d are not parallel, you must include the angle. A force pulling upward at 30° while an object moves horizontally requires cos 30°, not cos 0°.

2. **Using the wrong angle for θ.** θ is the angle *between* the force vector and the displacement vector, not between the force and the horizontal, or between the rope and the surface. Draw both vectors tail-to-tail and measure the angle between them.

3. **Thinking work is a vector.** Work is a scalar — it has sign (positive or negative) but no direction. Positive work adds energy to the object; negative work removes it.

4. **Confusing W_net with W by one force.** The work-energy theorem W_net = ΔK uses the *total* (net) work done by *all* forces, not just one. Sum all the individual works first.

5. **Wrong sign on spring force.** F_s = −kx, not +kx. The minus sign means the spring always pushes/pulls back toward equilibrium. Dropping the minus sign reverses the direction of the restoring force.

6. **Forgetting to square x in Us = ½kx².** The spring PE grows as the square of displacement. Compressing a spring twice as far stores four times the energy, not twice.

7. **Using the work-energy theorem with net force instead of net work.** The theorem says W_net = ΔK. You must compute the work done by each force (F·d·cos θ for each) and add them — not plug in "net force × displacement" unless you have verified that all forces act over the same displacement at the same angle.

8. **Choosing a bad reference point for U_g and then forgetting it.** U_g = mgy only works consistently if y is measured from the same reference level throughout the problem. Setting y = 0 at the ground is fine; so is y = 0 at the starting height — but switching mid-problem is a fatal error.

9. **Treating kinetic energy as signed.** K = ½mv² is always ≥ 0. Speed is always positive; v² is always positive. If your calculation gives a negative K, you made an error upstream.

10. **Claiming gravity does no work on a horizontal surface.** On a horizontal surface gravity is perpendicular to displacement, so it does zero work — correct. But on an incline or vertical drop, gravity does work equal to mg times the *vertical* displacement, not the distance along the incline.
