# Common Errors — Stage 2: Motion in One Dimension (Ch 2)

1. **Confusing average and instantaneous velocity.** Average velocity = Δx/Δt (slope of a chord on the x-t graph). Instantaneous velocity = dx/dt (slope of the tangent at one point). They are equal only for constant velocity.

2. **Assuming zero velocity means zero acceleration.** At a turning point, v = 0 but a ≠ 0. A ball thrown upward stops momentarily at the top, but g is still pulling it down. This is the most common conceptual trap in Ch 2.

3. **Using constant-acceleration formulas when acceleration is not constant.** The Big Four (v = v₀ + at; x = x₀ + v₀t + ½at²; v² = v₀² + 2aΔx; x = x₀ + ½(v+v₀)t) apply ONLY when a is constant throughout the motion. If the problem says "speed doubles every second" or gives a graph with a changing slope, these equations fail.

4. **Forgetting to choose a positive direction and sign everything consistently.** Gravity is −9.8 m/s² if you call upward positive. If you forget the sign of a₀ on g, the velocity and position equations give wrong answers. Write out your sign convention at the top of every problem.

5. **Conflating displacement with distance.** Displacement = final position minus initial position (can be negative). Distance = total path length (always positive). The equations of kinematics use displacement, not distance.

6. **Reading x-t graph slopes as positions instead of velocities.** The y-axis of an x-t graph is position, not velocity. Velocity is the SLOPE of the x-t graph. Students often state "velocity = 5 m" by reading the y-axis instead of computing the slope.

7. **Forgetting the initial conditions (v₀ and x₀).** When integrating a(t) to get v(t) and x(t), the integration constants are the initial values. Dropping them changes the problem completely.

8. **Using the kinematic equations out of their time domain.** If a ball hits the floor at t = 2 s, the equations are only valid for 0 ≤ t ≤ 2 s. Plugging in t = 3 s gives a meaningless answer where the ball is below the floor.
