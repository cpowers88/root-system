# Common Errors — Stage 1: Physics and Measurement

1. **Confusing mass and weight.** Mass (kg) is an inherent property of an object; weight is a force due to gravity, not introduced until Ch. 5. Don't say "kilograms of weight."

2. **Flipping a conversion factor.** Multiplying by 1 in./2.54 cm instead of 2.54 cm/1 in. leaves the wrong unit in place. Always check which unit cancels.

3. **Thinking dimensional analysis can find numerical constants.** It only checks the *shape* of an equation (which powers of which dimensions), not dimensionless numbers like ½ or π.

4. **Applying the wrong significant-figure rule.** Multiplication/division uses the fewest-sig-figs rule; addition/subtraction uses the fewest-decimal-places rule. Mixing these up is the single most common Ch. 1 mistake.

5. **Rounding too early.** Rounding intermediate results in a multi-step calculation (instead of only the final answer) causes small errors to compound.

6. **Treating order-of-magnitude estimates as if they need precision.** The entire point of a Fermi estimate is that it's allowed to be rough — spending too long perfecting one input defeats the purpose.

7. **Treating ambiguous trailing zeros as significant.** A value like "1500 g" is ambiguous; use scientific notation (1.5 × 10³ g vs. 1.500 × 10³ g) to remove the ambiguity.
