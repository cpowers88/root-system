# Common Errors — Stage 3: Vectors (Ch 3)

1. **Adding magnitudes instead of components.** If A⃗ has magnitude 3 and B⃗ has magnitude 4, the resultant is NOT 7 unless they point in exactly the same direction. You must always add Ax+Bx and Ay+By separately, then find the new magnitude.

2. **Using the wrong trig function for the wrong component.** The rule is: Ax = A cos θ and Ay = A sin θ when θ is measured from the +x axis. If θ is given from the +y axis (or from vertical), the sin and cos swap. Always draw the triangle first.

3. **Forgetting the quadrant when using tan⁻¹.** The calculator always returns an angle between −90° and +90°. If the vector is in the second or third quadrant (Ax < 0), you must add 180° to the calculator output. A sketch prevents this every time.

4. **Treating vector subtraction differently from vector addition.** A⃗ − B⃗ = A⃗ + (−B⃗). To subtract, flip all of B's components signs, then add. Don't try to subtract magnitudes.

5. **Confusing the dot product with multiplication.** The dot product A⃗·B⃗ = AB cos θ is NOT just A × B. It depends on the angle between the vectors. Two perpendicular vectors (like î and ĵ) have dot product zero even though neither vector is zero.

6. **Forgetting that î, ĵ, k̂ are dimensionless.** They carry no units. The units come from the magnitude that multiplies them: "5.0 m/s î" means 5.0 m/s in the x-direction; î itself is just a direction marker.

7. **Using degrees vs. radians incorrectly.** When computing sin/cos on a calculator, check whether it is in degree mode or radian mode. Most problems in this chapter give angles in degrees — make sure the calculator agrees.

8. **Skipping the diagram.** Every vector problem should start with a labeled sketch showing the coordinate axes, the vector, its angle, and the two components as legs of a right triangle. Students who skip the sketch consistently make sign errors.
