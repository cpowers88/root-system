# Common Errors — Stage 18: Special Relativity (Ch 38)

1. **Confusing proper time and proper length — and who measures them.** Proper time Δt₀ is measured by the clock that travels WITH the event (the moving clock). Proper length L₀ is measured in the frame where the object is AT REST. The moving observer always measures the shorter time and the shorter length — but "moving" is relative to the observer in question.

2. **Using γ less than 1.** γ = 1/√(1−v²/c²) ≥ 1 ALWAYS. γ = 1 when v = 0, and γ → ∞ as v → c. If your calculation gives γ < 1, you made an arithmetic error. Time dilation gives Δt = γΔt₀ ≥ Δt₀ (dilated time is always longer). Length contraction gives L = L₀/γ ≤ L₀ (contracted length is always shorter).

3. **Assuming relativistic effects are only relevant near v = c.** γ starts growing significantly above v ≈ 0.1c. GPS satellites at v ≈ 4 km/s need relativistic corrections (though tiny). Particle accelerators need them prominently. "Negligible" depends on the precision required.

4. **Using K = ½mv² for relativistic kinetic energy.** The classical formula fails at high speeds. Relativistic KE: K = (γ−1)mc². At low speeds, this reduces to ½mv² (Taylor expansion of γ), which is why classical mechanics works fine at everyday speeds.

5. **Forgetting the rest energy in E = γmc².** Total energy E = KE + rest energy = (γ−1)mc² + mc² = γmc². Rest energy E₀ = mc² is always present, even for a stationary object. A proton at rest has mc² ≈ 938 MeV stored in its rest mass.

6. **Applying Newtonian momentum p = mv in relativistic equations.** Relativistic momentum is p = γmv. At v = 0.8c, γ ≈ 1.67, so p = 1.67mv — significantly more than mv. Using p = mv underestimates momentum for fast particles.

7. **Thinking time dilation means moving clocks are broken.** Moving clocks run slow because time itself passes more slowly in the moving frame — not because the clock is malfunctioning. All physical processes (heartbeats, chemical reactions, radioactive decay) slow down by the same factor γ in a moving frame.

8. **Assuming simultaneity is absolute.** Two events simultaneous in one frame are generally NOT simultaneous in another frame. This is one of the deepest conceptual shifts from Newtonian physics. Students often unconsciously assume a universal "now" — special relativity eliminates it.
