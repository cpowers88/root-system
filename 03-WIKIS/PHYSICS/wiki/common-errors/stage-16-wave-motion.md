# Common Errors — Stage 16: Wave Motion (Ch 16)

1. **Confusing wave speed v with particle speed (transverse velocity).** The wave speed v = fλ = ω/k is how fast the wave pattern moves through the medium. The transverse particle speed (dy/dt) varies with position and time — it is NOT the same as v. Maximum transverse particle speed is v_y,max = Aω, which can be faster or slower than v.

2. **Thinking the medium moves with the wave.** In a transverse wave, the medium (string, water surface) oscillates perpendicular to the propagation direction — it does NOT travel with the wave. Water waves don't push water forward; sound waves don't blow air forward (significantly).

3. **Confusing wavelength λ and period T.** λ is a spatial quantity (meters per cycle). T is a temporal quantity (seconds per cycle). They're related through v = λ/T = λf. Don't substitute T where λ is needed.

4. **Applying the wave speed formula v = √(T/μ) to the wrong tension.** T here is tension in the string (N), not the period. In problems where a string is under tension and vibrating, check that you're using tension (force) not period (time) — they use the same symbol T.

5. **Getting the Doppler formula wrong when both source and observer move.** The full formula is f_obs = f_s(v ± v_o)/(v ∓ v_s). The sign rule: numerator top (+) when observer moves toward source; denominator bottom (−) when source moves toward observer. Mixing these up produces a frequency that is too high or too low.

6. **Doubling distance and thinking intensity quadruples (instead of quarters).** Intensity follows an inverse-square law: I ∝ 1/r². Doubling r reduces I by a factor of 4. Students sometimes square the factor in the wrong direction.

7. **Forgetting the reference intensity I₀ when computing decibels.** β = 10 log₁₀(I/I₀) where I₀ = 10⁻¹² W/m² (threshold of human hearing). Forgetting I₀ gives a decibel value without meaning. Also: decibels are NOT linearly additive — two 60 dB sources together give about 63 dB, not 120 dB.
