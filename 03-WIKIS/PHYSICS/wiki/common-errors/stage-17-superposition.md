# Common Errors — Stage 17: Superposition and Standing Waves (Ch 17)

1. **Confusing nodes and antinodes.** A node is a point of ZERO displacement at all times — the two waves always cancel there. An antinode is a point of MAXIMUM displacement — the two waves always reinforce there. Students sometimes reverse these, especially when drawing diagrams.

2. **Using the wrong standing wave condition for closed vs. open pipes.** String fixed at both ends = open pipe at both ends: L = nλ/2 (n = 1,2,3,…) — all harmonics. Pipe closed at one end: L = nλ/4 (n = 1,3,5,…) — odd harmonics only. A closed end is a displacement node (pressure antinode); an open end is a displacement antinode (pressure node). Applying the open-pipe formula to a closed pipe gives wrong frequencies.

3. **Thinking standing waves travel.** Standing waves don't propagate — the pattern of nodes and antinodes stays fixed in space. The energy sloshes back and forth but the wave doesn't move. Students confuse standing waves with traveling waves that "stand still."

4. **Misidentifying the fundamental frequency (n = 1) for closed pipes.** The fundamental is the LOWEST frequency, which corresponds to n = 1 and gives the longest wavelength that fits the boundary conditions. For a closed pipe: λ₁ = 4L, f₁ = v/(4L). Students sometimes use v/(2L) (the open-pipe formula) for a closed pipe.

5. **Adding beat frequencies incorrectly.** Beat frequency = |f₁ − f₂|, always a positive number — it is the difference, not the sum. Two sources at 440 Hz and 444 Hz produce 4 beats per second, not 884.

6. **Thinking constructive interference produces double the amplitude.** Yes — exactly. But this does NOT mean double the intensity. Intensity ∝ A², so doubling amplitude quadruples intensity. Destructive interference does not eliminate energy — the energy redistributes to points of constructive interference.

7. **Treating resonance as always dangerous.** Resonance simply means a system is driven at its natural frequency, producing large amplitudes. Whether this is useful (musical instruments, MRI scanners) or damaging (Tacoma Narrows Bridge) depends on the damping and the structure — it's not inherently destructive.
