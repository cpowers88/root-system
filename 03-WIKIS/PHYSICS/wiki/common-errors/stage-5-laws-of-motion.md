# Common Errors — Stage 5: The Laws of Motion

1. **Drawing forces the object exerts on other things.** A FBD shows only forces ON the chosen object. The reaction forces belong on the other object's FBD. This is the most common FBD mistake.

2. **Treating weight and normal force as a Newton's Third Law pair.** They are not. Weight (Earth pulling object down) pairs with the object pulling Earth up. Normal force (surface pushing object up) pairs with object pushing surface down. Weight and normal force happen to be equal in magnitude when the object rests on a horizontal surface with no vertical acceleration — but they are not an action-reaction pair.

3. **Forgetting to decompose weight on an incline.** Weight always points straight down (−y). On an inclined plane the components are mg sinθ (down the slope) and mg cosθ (perpendicular to slope). Using mg instead of mg cosθ for the normal force is a systematic error that propagates to friction.

4. **Setting static friction equal to μ_s n as an equation instead of an inequality.** Static friction adjusts itself to prevent slipping — it ranges from 0 to μ_s n. It only equals μ_s n at the moment of impending motion. Using f_s = μ_s n when the object is simply at rest (not on the verge of sliding) will give the wrong answer.

5. **Confusing mass with weight.** "A 10 kg force" is not physically meaningful. Force is measured in newtons. Mass (10 kg) becomes weight only after multiplying by g: w = mg = 10 × 9.80 = 98 N.

6. **Applying Newton's 2nd Law to the whole system but using the wrong mass.** When two blocks are connected and moving together, ΣF = ma works with the total mass. But tension in the rope between them must be found by drawing a FBD for one block alone with its own mass.

7. **Forgetting that tension pulls in both directions along the rope.** A rope connects two objects; the tension pulls the left object right and the right object left. Both magnitudes are equal if the rope is massless.

8. **Sign errors in the Atwood machine.** Students often set up the equation for one mass correctly and then flip the sign for the other without being consistent. Fix: define one direction (say, "m₁ goes up" is positive) and stick to it for both masses.

9. **Assuming the normal force always equals mg.** This is only true on a flat horizontal surface with no other vertical forces and no vertical acceleration. On an incline, n = mg cosθ. If a vertical force is applied (e.g., someone pushes down on the block), n increases above mg.

10. **Ignoring friction when the problem says "smooth" or "frictionless."** "Smooth" is code for zero friction. Students sometimes add friction anyway out of habit. Always re-read the problem statement.
