# Common Errors — Stage 14: Fluid Mechanics (Ch 14)

1. **Using gauge pressure when absolute pressure is needed (or vice versa).** Gauge pressure = absolute pressure minus atmospheric pressure. P_gauge = P_abs − P_atm. Bernoulli's equation uses absolute pressure throughout — do not mix gauge and absolute pressure in the same equation.

2. **Confusing buoyant force with net force.** Archimedes' principle gives the buoyant force F_B = ρ_fluid × V_submerged × g — upward. The net force on the object is F_B − mg. If F_B > mg, it accelerates upward; if F_B < mg, it sinks. Students sometimes equate buoyancy directly with weight and skip the net force.

3. **Using the object's density instead of the fluid's density in the buoyancy formula.** F_B = ρ_FLUID × V_submerged × g. The density of the fluid is what matters for buoyancy, not the density of the object. The object's density determines whether it sinks or floats, but not the magnitude of F_B.

4. **Applying Bernoulli's equation across a pump, fan, or viscous section.** Bernoulli's equation assumes ideal (non-viscous, incompressible, steady, streamline) flow. It cannot be applied across devices that add or remove energy from the flow.

5. **Misidentifying A and v in the continuity equation.** A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ applies to the cross-sectional area of the pipe (perpendicular to flow), not the length. In a tapered pipe, the smaller area is where the speed is higher — students sometimes mix these up intuitively (thinking slower in the narrow part because it seems more crowded).

6. **Assuming pressure is lowest where speed is lowest.** Bernoulli says: where speed is high, pressure is low (and vice versa). This is counterintuitive — faster flow has lower pressure. The fast-moving air over an airplane wing has LOWER pressure than the slow-moving air below.

7. **Forgetting the ρgy term in Bernoulli's equation when height changes.** P + ½ρv² + ρgy = constant. If a pipe goes up or down, the height term matters. Many students drop ρgy when heights differ, getting a wrong pressure or speed.
