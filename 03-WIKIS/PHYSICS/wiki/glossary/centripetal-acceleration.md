# Centripetal Acceleration

## Plain-English Definition

The acceleration experienced by an object moving in a circle. It always points toward the center of the circle, even as the direction of motion rotates around.

## Physics Meaning

a_c = v²/r. It arises because the velocity vector constantly changes direction (even though its magnitude, the speed, stays constant). It is a real acceleration — there must be a real net inward force causing it.

## Units / Symbols If Applicable

Symbol: a_c. Unit: m/s². Direction: always toward the center (centripetal = "center-seeking").

## Example

A car making a 30 m radius turn at 15 m/s has centripetal acceleration a_c = (15)²/30 = 7.5 m/s² directed toward the center of the curve.

## Common Confusion

Mixing up centripetal (inward) acceleration with the fictitious centrifugal ("outward") sensation. In an inertial reference frame, centrifugal force doesn't exist. The centripetal acceleration is inward; what you feel thrown outward in a turning car is your inertia resisting the change in direction.

## Flashcard

**Q:** What is centripetal acceleration, what direction does it point, and what is its formula?
**A:** The acceleration of an object in circular motion; points toward the center of the circle; a_c = v²/r.
