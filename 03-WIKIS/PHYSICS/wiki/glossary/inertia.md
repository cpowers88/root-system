# Glossary — Inertia

**Definition:** Inertia is the tendency of an object to resist changes to its state of motion. An object at rest resists being set in motion; an object in motion resists being stopped or redirected.

**Physical anchor:** You feel "thrown" forward when a car brakes suddenly — your body's inertia resists the sudden deceleration. A hockey puck on ice keeps moving because nothing stops it (very low friction).

**Measured by:** Mass (kg). More massive objects have greater inertia — they're harder to accelerate with the same force.

**Law:** Newton's First Law states inertia directly — an object keeps its current velocity unless a net force acts on it.

**Stage introduced:** [[../stages/stage-5-laws-of-motion]]

**Do not confuse with:** Momentum (p = mv), which is mass × velocity. Inertia is just the resistance to change, not the "quantity of motion" already possessed.
