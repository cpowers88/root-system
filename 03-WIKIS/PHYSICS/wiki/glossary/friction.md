# Glossary — Friction

**Definition:** A contact force that opposes relative motion (or the tendency of relative motion) between surfaces. Always acts parallel to the surface, opposite to motion or tendency of motion.

**Two types:**
- **Static friction (f_s):** Prevents sliding. Adjusts from 0 to a maximum of μ_s × n.
- **Kinetic friction (f_k):** Opposes sliding once motion begins. Fixed at μ_k × n.

**Key relationship:** μ_s > μ_k always — harder to start sliding than to maintain sliding.

**Physical anchor:** It's harder to push a fridge to get it moving than to keep pushing it once it's moving. That difference IS the difference between static and kinetic friction.

**Coefficients:** μ (mu) is dimensionless, a property of the pair of surfaces in contact, not of either object alone. Rubber on concrete: μ_s ≈ 1.0. Ice on ice: μ_s ≈ 0.10.
