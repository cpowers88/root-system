# Conservative Force

**Definition:** A force for which the work done between two points is independent of the path taken. Equivalently, the work done over any closed loop is zero. Conservative forces have associated potential energy functions.

**Examples:** Gravity (near Earth), spring force, electric force.

**Contrast:** Nonconservative forces (friction, air drag) do path-dependent work and have NO potential energy.

**Unit:** Forces are in N, but the "conservative" property is about work (J) being path-independent.

**Flash fact:** Gravity is conservative — it doesn't matter if you carry a box up a straight staircase or a winding ramp. Gravity does the same work either way (depends only on Δy).
