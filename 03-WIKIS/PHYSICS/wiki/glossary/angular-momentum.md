# Angular Momentum

## Plain-English Definition

How much "spinning tendency" an object or system has, taking into account both how fast it spins and how its mass is spread around the rotation axis.

## Physics Meaning

For a rigid body: L = Iω — moment of inertia times angular velocity.
For a particle: L = mvr sin θ — depends on mass, speed, distance from axis, and direction of travel.
L is a vector pointing along the rotation axis (right-hand rule).

## Units / Symbols

L; SI unit kg·m²/s. (Same as J·s — joule-seconds — but the angular momentum name is preferred.)

## Example

A figure skater rotating at 2.0 rad/s with arms extended (I = 4.5 kg·m²):
L = (4.5)(2.0) = 9.0 kg·m²/s.

When she pulls her arms in to I = 1.5 kg·m²:
ω_f = 9.0/1.5 = 6.0 rad/s. She spins three times faster.

## Common Confusion

Confusing L (angular momentum, kg·m²/s) with ω (angular velocity, rad/s). They are related by L = Iω — the moment of inertia I is the proportionality factor between them. Two objects can have the same ω but very different L if their I values differ.

## Flashcard

**Q:** What is angular momentum for a rigid body, and what are its units?
**A:** L = Iω; units kg·m²/s.
