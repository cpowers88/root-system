## Strategic Thesis

AI is inserted at the task layer; humans stay at the judgment,
relationship, accountability, and improvement layers. The operating loop:

> Human sets intent → AI performs first-pass work → human reviews
> exceptions → human updates rules/SOPs/prompts/data → workflow improves →
> production capacity increases.

Full model: [[progressive-operating-thesis]] and
[[human-agent-operating-model]] — the thesis lives in the pages now, not

## Business Design Rule

Design around business throughput, not "cool AI tools." Every
recommendation answers:

1. What human task is being reduced, accelerated, or upgraded?
2. What business bottleneck is being removed?
3. What quality-control gate protects the client?
4. What human role becomes more valuable after AI is inserted?
5. How does this produce measurable revenue, margin, speed, capacity, or
   retention?

If a workflow doesn't connect to revenue, cost reduction, risk reduction,
speed, quality, or management visibility — flag it low priority.

## Raw Intake Protocol

When Chris drops a source into `raw/`:

**Step 1 — Classify** into one or more of: market research · offer design ·
audit method · sales system · pricing model · fulfillment system · tool
stack · human-agent operating model · role redesign / job creation ·
risk / quality control · case study material · parked / not useful.

**Step 2 — Prefer updating over creating.** The wiki already has a full
page set. Check whether the source strengthens, corrects, or extends an
existing page first. New page only when the idea is reusable, fits nowhere,
and supports the North Star.

**Step 3 — Extract with a use test.** For each useful idea: core idea,
practical use, which page it updates (or why it needs a new one), what's
missing from Chris to finish it. Discard anything that fails the Business
Design Rule bar.

**Step 4 — File and tag.** New pages follow the Page Template and get
exactly one phase tag. Updated pages keep their tag unless the timing
genuinely changed.

**Step 5 — Update navigation.** Update
`wiki/ai-integration-company/index.md` and `wiki/log.md`; print the changed
file paths.

**Step 6 — Close with a gap report:**

```text
## What Changed
## What Was Added
## What Was Updated
## What Was Parked
## Missing Data Needed From Chris
## Recommended Next Action
```

Hard rules: don't summarize a raw file for its own sake; don't duplicate
full source content into a page.

## Phase Tags (graph convention)

Every wiki page carries exactly one phase tag as the FIRST tag in its
frontmatter:

- `phase-1` — Foundation (weeks 0–4): start-here, first-30-days, thesis +
  operating model, skill-roadmap, tool-stack, most-profitable-pathways
- `phase-2` — First clients (months 1–3): first-90-days, market-map,
  sales-system, audit method, offer ladder, pricing, financial-model,
  sales/audit templates
- `phase-3` — Deliver & convert (months 3–12): one-year-plan, fulfillment,
  gates, role redesign, agent-manager design, workflow/CRM/document
  pathways, retainer-model, case studies, delivery templates
- `phase-4` — Expand (years 1–3): three-year-plan, dashboards, internal
  assistants
- `phase-5` — Scale (years 3–10): ten-year-scale-plan
- `phase-all` — Always-on reference: index, README,
  risks-and-failure-modes, what-not-to-do

If a page's execution timing changes, move its tag. Isolate a phase in the
single `.ROOT` graph with `path:"03-WIKIS/BUSINESS" tag:#phase-1` — the
filtered view is the progression map; the graph colors this wiki as one block.
