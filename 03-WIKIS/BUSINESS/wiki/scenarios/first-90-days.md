# First 90 Days

> Proof quarter: first paid audits, first implementation projects, first retainer, first case studies.

## Timeline Gate — Read This First
Same clock as [[first-30-days|First 30 Days]]: this quarter begins only after
CASTLE/NOW opens authorized client acquisition. `CURRENT_STRATEGY.md` owns the live
milestones. Nothing here overrides fixed school commitments or the October 5–November
11 high-load protection.

## Purpose
Define the 90-day targets and the month-2/month-3 agenda that follows [[first-30-days|First 30 Days]], so the quarter ends with a functioning micro-business — paying clients, documented results, and a repeatable weekly rhythm.

## Key Idea
The 90-day goal is not income (that's a byproduct); it is **completing the full loop at least twice**: outreach → audit sold → audit delivered → project sold from findings → project delivered with measured results → retainer offered → case study written. Once the loop has run twice, you have a business; everything after is repetition and refinement.

## Why It Matters
Ninety days is long enough to prove the model with real clients and short enough to prevent perfectionist drift. Every asset the [[one-year-plan|One-Year Plan]] needs — case studies, referral sources, delivery playbooks, pricing data — is created by running this loop, not by planning.

## Targets (Day 90 Scorecard)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Paid audits delivered | 3–5 |
| Implementation projects sold from audits | 2–3 |
| Retainer clients | 1+ |
| Case studies written | 2+ |
| Total revenue for the quarter | $10,000–$25,000 |
| Weekly sales rhythm | Running without exception for 8+ weeks |

Miss the revenue number but hit the loop counts → fine, raise prices. Hit revenue but skip case studies and retainers → not fine; you're building a freelance job, not a company.

## Month 2 — Sell and Deliver the First Audits
- Outreach continues at full weekly minimums ([[sales-system|Sales System]]) — delivery is never an excuse
- Close and deliver audits 1–2. Expect 20–30 hours each; capture every reusable artifact (process-map patterns, interview phrasings, report sections) into templates
- Present each report live, ending on the Phase 1 proposal ([[smb-ai-audit-method|SMB AI Audit Method]], Step 5)
- Sell and start implementation project #1 — a quick win from your own audit findings ([[service-offer-ladder|Service Offer Ladder]] Rung 2)
- Keep skill-building inside real work: whatever project #1 needs is this month's curriculum ([[skill-roadmap|Skill Roadmap]])

### Month 2 pitfalls
Underpricing out of nervousness (hold the [[pricing-models|rate card]]); letting the first project sprawl (hold the scope doc — [[fulfillment-system|Fulfillment System]]); pausing outreach the moment delivery starts (the classic feast/famine seed).

## Month 3 — Complete the Loop and Systematize
- Deliver project #1 through full handoff: documentation, training, **measured before/after, retainer offer** ([[retainer-model|Retainer Model]])
- Write case study #1 within a week of handoff ([[05-BUSINESS/03-Case Studies/CASE_STUDY_TEMPLATE|Case Study Template]]); arm outreach with it immediately — proof-backed messaging noticeably outperforms
- Deliver audits 3–5 and sell projects 2–3 from their findings
- Ask every satisfied client for 2 specific referrals; activate at least 2 referral partners
- Systematize what repeated: audit templates v2, proposal template, scope doc, handoff checklist — the delivery playbook becomes real
- Review pricing with actual hours data; raise where the data says so

## The Weekly Rhythm (Locked In By Day 90)
- **Sales block (non-negotiable, ~1 day/wk):** outreach touches, partner contact, pipeline review
- **Delivery blocks (~3 days/wk):** audits and builds, worked to the [[fulfillment-system|fulfillment lifecycle]]
- **Systems block (~half day/wk):** templates, playbook notes, case studies, bookkeeping
- **Learning block (~half day/wk):** whatever the next sold offer requires

## Practical Actions
- Put the Day-90 scorecard somewhere visible; review it every Friday against actuals.
- After each audit and project, spend 30 minutes harvesting reusables before starting the next thing — this habit is worth six figures over two years.
- Schedule the retainer conversation into every project handoff agenda so it can't be forgotten or chickened out of.

## Intermediate Version
Same loop, tightened: audits delivered with the full six-step method including the [[human-agent-operating-model|task inventory]] and redesign sections, project #1 shipped with [[quality-control-and-risk-gates|gates]] and a [[human-role-redesign|role card]], and the retainer pitched as "we run your calibration loop" rather than generic maintenance. The loop counts stay the target; the deliverable quality is what rises.

## Beginner Version
This page is the beginner version. If conversions lag: audits not selling → sharpen the pain-specific outreach ([[market-map|Market Map]]); audits selling but projects not → your findings aren't quantified in dollars or the roadmap isn't payback-ranked; projects selling but delivery drowning → tighten scope and slow the sales throttle one notch (never to zero).

## Advanced Version
Reused as the **new-vertical entry playbook**: months 2–3 mirror this structure whenever the firm opens a new vertical or region — first audits at founding pricing, first case studies, then scale. See [[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]].

## Revenue Connection
$10K–$25K in the quarter, but the compounding assets matter more: 2+ case studies (cuts future CAC), 1+ retainer (first recurring dollar), pricing data (raises margins), and templates (cuts delivery hours 30%+ on the next round). This quarter is where lifetime-value economics start.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
The quarter's projects are where the [[progressive-operating-thesis|thesis]] first gets delivered for money: every build ships with its gate design and a named human reviewer, and every handoff explains what the staff's job becomes. Do that on the first two projects and "we redesign work, not just automate it" stops being positioning and starts being your track record.

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **Outreach pauses during delivery** — the feast/famine seed, planted exactly now.
- **Underpricing out of nervousness** — the rate card exists so the quote isn't a mood.
- **Loop left incomplete** — revenue without case studies and a retainer is a freelance job wearing a business costume; the scorecard's non-revenue rows are the ones that compound.
