# Skill Roadmap

> What to learn, in what order, and to what depth — mapped to the services each skill unlocks.

## Purpose
Sequence the skills required to run this company, so learning time goes to what unlocks revenue next instead of what's interesting this week.

## Key Idea
Skills are learned **just ahead of the offer that needs them**, and every skill is learned by building something real — your own systems first, then a client's. The stack has four layers, and the layers compound in this order:

1. **Diagnostic skills** (see the waste) → unlocks the [[smb-ai-audit-method|audit]]
2. **Build skills** (fix the waste) → unlocks the [[most-profitable-pathways|pathways]]
3. **Commercial skills** (sell and price the fix) → unlocks margin
4. **Operator skills** (run the firm) → unlocks scale

An engineering background gives a head start on layer 2 — which is exactly why the real risk is over-investing there and starving layers 1 and 3. The market pays for diagnosis and outcomes; the build is table stakes.

### Third-Party Validation of This Layer Order (added 2026-07-12, WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025)
The WEF's Core Skills 2025 ranking (1,000+ employers, 22 industries, 55 economies) independently ranks systems thinking (42%), resource management/operations (41%), and quality control (35%) as named, employer-cited core skills — corroborating that Layers 1 and 2 below aren't invented categories. Notably, **AI & big data ranks only 11th in current core-skill importance (45%)** despite being separately named the #1 fastest-*growing* skill — third-party confirmation of this roadmap's "diagnosis over AI-tool-fetish" ordering: the tool skill is rising fast but isn't yet what employers rank most essential, process/systems literacy still leads. Separately, the same report finds skill instability easing (39% of workers' skills expected to change by 2030, down from 44% in 2023 and 57% in 2020) while reskilling participation rises (50% of workers vs. 41% in 2023) — continuous learning is trending from novelty toward baseline expectation, which is worth a line in a proposal when pitching training as a standard delivery component, not an add-on.

## Layer 1 — Diagnostic Skills (Months 0–3, then forever)
- **Process mapping:** trigger → steps → tools → handoffs → outputs; practice on any business you can access
- **Waste quantification:** time studies, loaded-cost math, revenue-leak estimation ([[smb-ai-audit-method|audit method, Step 3]])
- **Interviewing:** getting frontline staff to describe what *actually* happens (never matches the official process)
- **Business fluency:** reading a P&L, understanding cash flow, margin, and CAC well enough to talk to owners as a peer
- **Construction domain fluency (parallel credibility prep — free, doable before the timeline gate):** Procore's free training library at learn.procore.com covers the domain layer the construction credibility wedge needs: *Construction 101 / Construction Bootcamp* (roles, lifecycle, blueprints, estimating, scheduling), the *Data in Construction* series (data collection/analysis on jobsites, ML & AI in construction, emerging tech), an *AI in Construction* program, and per-tool training (Estimating, etc.). Procore is the dominant construction-management platform — speaking its vocabulary builds credibility with $2M–$15M contractors, and knowing what data their platform already captures feeds the [[smb-ai-audit-method|audit]]'s task inventory and the [[data-and-dashboard-pathway|dashboard pathway]]. This remains legitimate pre-gate prep work under [[market-map|Market Map]]'s real-estate-first / construction-parallel entry-wedge decision.

## Layer 2 — Build Skills (Months 0–9, deepening for years)
Priority order:
1. **One integration platform to expert level** (Make or n8n — see [[tool-stack|Tool Stack]]): triggers, iterators, error handlers, webhooks, rate limits
2. **APIs and data plumbing:** REST, auth (OAuth/API keys), JSON, webhooks; enough scripting (Python or JS) to fill gaps no-code can't reach
3. **LLM engineering:** prompt design for extraction/classification/drafting, structured outputs, retrieval (RAG) fundamentals, evaluation and testing of AI outputs, cost/latency management — treat model output as an unreliable component to engineer around
4. **CRM platforms:** one mainstream CRM deeply ([[crm-and-sales-ops-pathway|CRM pathway]])
5. **Data & BI:** SQL basics, data modeling, one BI tool ([[data-and-dashboard-pathway|dashboard pathway]])
6. **Reliability engineering & AI quality control:** logging, monitoring, alerting, graceful failure — plus the gate craft: validation layers, confidence routing, sampling audits, acceptance tests ([[quality-control-and-risk-gates|Quality Control & Risk Gates]]). The discipline that separates you from hobbyist automators, and a directly sellable skill: gate architecture is why your $12K build beats a $2K freelancer zap
7. **Agentic delivery:** driving Claude Code (or equivalent) as your build multiplier — per-client configurations, reviewing and testing generated code, knowing what to delegate to the agent vs. keep by hand. This skill compresses every other layer-2 skill's delivery hours; see [[claude-code-leverage]]

## Layer 3 — Commercial Skills (Months 1–12)
- **Consultative selling:** discovery questioning, mirroring, closing the audit ([[sales-system|Sales System]]) — rehearsed out loud, not just read about
- **Proposal writing & value pricing:** ROI framing, scope definition, saying no ([[pricing-models|Pricing Models]])
- **Expectation management:** weekly updates, scope-change conversations, delivering bad news early
- **Proof-building:** writing case studies that sell ([[05-BUSINESS/03-Case Studies/CASE_STUDY_TEMPLATE|Case Study Template]])
- **Role design & training:** writing [[human-role-redesign|role cards]], running staff training, coaching client employees into [[agent-manager-job-design|operator/agent-manager roles]] — the skill that makes adoption stick, and a paid line item in every implementation

## Layer 4 — Operator Skills (Year 1+)
- Delegation and playbook-writing (turning your delivery into trainable process — [[fulfillment-system|Fulfillment System]])
- Hiring and reviewing technical work you didn't do
- Financial management: margin per engagement, utilization, cash forecasting
- Basic contracts and risk: MSAs, SOWs, liability limits, data-handling terms

## Learning Method (How, Not Just What)
- **Build > courses.** One rule: for every hour of tutorial, two hours building. Certifications don't sell audits; demos and case studies do.
- **Your business is the lab:** automate your own lead tracking, invoicing, reporting, and monitoring first — every internal build is practice plus a sales demo.
- **Timebox layer 2:** any skill not needed for a sold or next-quarter offer goes on the "later" list. The [[what-not-to-do|What NOT To Do]] page covers tool-collecting as a failure mode.

## Practical Actions
- Self-assess against all four layers this week; pick the single weakest skill that blocks your *next* revenue step and schedule it.
- Establish the weekly split: ~10 hours build-skill practice, ~5 hours diagnostic/commercial practice during months 0–3.
- Keep a "skills unlocked → offers unlocked" log; it keeps learning honest.

## Beginner Version
Ninety-day core: process mapping + waste math, one automation platform to working fluency, LLM extraction/classification basics, and the audit sales conversation rehearsed. That's enough to earn money; everything else is learned inside paid work.

## Intermediate Version
Layers 1–3 are earning: audits templated, two build stacks fluent (platform + code), gate architecture standard on every delivery, and the commercial skills producing 50%+ proposal close rates. The learning budget shifts toward what the *next quarter's* offers need — usually dashboards/BI or assistant evaluation — and toward writing playbooks, the first layer-4 skill.

## Advanced Version
You stop being the most skilled builder on purpose. Your learning shifts to layer 4 plus staying sharp enough to review architecture and hard problems. The team learns layers 1–2 from your playbooks; you hire for what's below your bar and train for what isn't documented yet.

## Revenue Connection
Every layer maps to money: layer 1 sells audits, layer 2 delivers projects, layer 3 sets margins, layer 4 removes you as the bottleneck. The roadmap's sequencing *is* the revenue sequencing of the [[first-90-days|First 90 Days]] and [[one-year-plan|One-Year Plan]].

## Human-Agent Management Connection
Two skill clusters exist *because* of the [[progressive-operating-thesis|thesis]]: AI quality control (layer 2 #6 — engineering the gates humans work behind) and role design & training (layer 3 — moving client staff into the [[agent-manager-job-design|new roles]]). Both tie straight to revenue: gates justify premium pricing, and trained roles are what keep systems adopted and retainers renewed. Example of the pattern every skill should follow: *learning CRM cleanup matters because messy CRM data destroys follow-up, reporting, and automation reliability — and it sells as a revenue-recovery service.*

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **Layer-2 overinvestment** — the engineering-background trap: six months of building skills with zero diagnostic or sales practice produces an unemployed expert.
- **Course consumption as progress** — tutorial hours don't sell audits; the 1:2 tutorial-to-building rule is the guard.
- **Learning ahead of revenue** — any skill not needed for a sold or next-quarter offer goes on the "later" list.
