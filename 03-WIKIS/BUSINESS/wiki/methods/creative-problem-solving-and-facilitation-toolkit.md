# Creative Problem Solving and Facilitation Toolkit

> A selective field method for moving a named, evidence-backed problem from
> diagnosis to alternatives, choice, implementation, and learning. Use the
> technique catalog only when a real decision is stuck; brainstorming is not a
> substitute for observation, strategy, or customer evidence.

## Purpose

Give an audit or client workshop enough structure to produce a decision and a
next action. The method preserves the strongest operating idea in James M.
Higgins's *101 Creative Problem Solving Techniques* without reproducing all 101
exercises or treating its 1994 anecdotes as current evidence.

## The Creative Problem-Solving Loop

1. **Scan the environment:** collect observations, artifacts, complaints,
   exceptions, trend changes, and constraints.
2. **Recognize the gap or opportunity:** state what is happening, what should be
   happening, and why the difference matters.
3. **Define and reframe the problem:** separate symptoms from causes; write the
   decision or outcome in more than one useful form.
4. **Surface assumptions:** list what the group is treating as fixed, then test
   which assumptions are facts, constraints, habits, or guesses.
5. **Generate alternatives:** deliberately separate idea production from
   evaluation so premature criticism does not collapse the option set.
6. **Choose:** compare alternatives against explicit criteria established from
   the problem, constraints, risk, and intended outcome.
7. **Implement:** turn the chosen direction into steps, owners, dates, resources,
   review gates, and a smallest safe test.
8. **Control and learn:** define the result signal, review exceptions, and reopen
   the problem when evidence invalidates the diagnosis or assumptions.

Do not enter Step 5 with only a vague topic such as "use AI" or "improve sales."
The minimum entry condition is a named problem or opportunity, a decision owner,
relevant evidence, and a tangible output expected from the session.

## Technique Chooser

Use the smallest technique that addresses the blockage. The labels below are
retrieval pointers into the source, not a mandatory sequence.

| Need | Useful techniques | Required output |
|---|---|---|
| Understand current state or causes | complaints, checklists, fishbone diagram, why-why, pattern review | evidence-linked cause map |
| Reframe the problem | rewrite the objective, squeeze/stretch, reversal, assumption reversal | 2-4 candidate problem statements |
| Generate individually | analogy, attribute listing, fresh eye, mind mapping, what-if | unfiltered option list |
| Reduce anchoring or status pressure | brainwriting, Crawford slips, nominal group technique | independent ideas before discussion |
| Explore combinations systematically | morphological analysis | option combinations across key dimensions |
| Explore uncertain futures | scenario writing, storyboarding | alternatives robust to more than one plausible future |
| Screen alternatives | screening matrix | scored comparison against explicit criteria |
| Read group preference | dot voting | preference signal, followed by evidence and risk review |
| Plan execution | how-how diagram, force-field analysis | steps plus forces helping and blocking implementation |

### Selection rules

- Prefer silent individual generation before open discussion when hierarchy,
  confidence, or anchoring could suppress information.
- Use a fishbone or why-why chain to organize causal hypotheses, then verify them;
  the diagram itself does not prove causation.
- Use assumption reversal to expose the design space, not to pretend legal,
  safety, contractual, or physical constraints do not exist.
- Use dot voting only to expose preference. Popularity is not validation.
- Use a screening matrix only after criteria and their meaning are explicit.
  Scores should make judgment inspectable, not create false precision.
- End every generative technique with convergence, an owner, and a test. An idea
  inventory without a decision is unfinished work.

## Bounded Workshop Protocol

### Before the session

Write a one-page brief:

- **Question:** What specific decision or stuck outcome must move?
- **Evidence:** What was observed, measured, or supplied by the client?
- **Decision owner:** Who can accept the outcome and authorize the next step?
- **Participants:** Who performs, receives, manages, or reviews the work?
- **Constraints:** What is truly fixed for this session?
- **Output:** What artifact and decision should exist by the end?
- **Stop condition:** What missing evidence would make ideation premature?

### During the session

1. Recontract the question, output, timebox, and decision rights.
2. Present the current-state evidence in descriptive language.
3. Confirm or revise the problem statement; distinguish fact from assumption.
4. Choose one cause/reframe technique only if the diagnosis is still weak.
5. Generate silently before sharing; defer evaluation for the timebox.
6. Cluster duplicates and clarify alternatives without advocating for one.
7. Apply explicit criteria: outcome impact, evidence, feasibility, risk, review
   burden, reversibility, cost, and time to learning.
8. Record the decision, rejected alternatives, and unresolved assumptions.
9. Build the smallest safe test with owner, date, artifact, metric, review gate,
   and next decision.

### After the session

Ship a short decision record, not a transcript:

- problem and evidence;
- assumptions tested and still open;
- alternatives considered;
- choice and rationale;
- action owner, date, and test;
- result signal and review date.

## Where It Fits in the Business

This is an optional workshop layer inside
[[consulting-methodology|Consulting Methodology]], not a new offer by default.
Use it after contracting and discovery when a client has accepted the diagnosis
but needs help generating or selecting a response.

Inside the [[smb-ai-audit-method|SMB AI Audit Method]], it can support the move
from verified findings to a prioritized implementation roadmap. It must not be
used to invent pain, manufacture ROI, or replace workflow observation.

Inside [[strategic-diagnosis-and-coherent-action|Strategic Diagnosis and Coherent
Action]], it expands alternatives only after the challenge has a causal
diagnosis. The final choice still needs a guiding policy and coherent actions.

Inside [[product-discovery-and-operating-model|Product Discovery and Operating
Model]], it can generate prototypes or test designs after customer evidence
exists. Workshop enthusiasm does not satisfy the productization gate.

## Revenue Connection

The method can make an audit findings session or implementation-design workshop
more valuable when stakeholders are genuinely stuck. A paid workshop is viable
only when it owns a named decision and ships a usable decision record, option
matrix, or test plan. "Brainstorming" alone is not a defensible deliverable.

## Human-Agent Boundary

AI can organize observations, produce alternate framings, expand combinations,
draft matrices, identify missing criteria, and challenge internal consistency.
Humans own the problem definition, confidential context, constraints, criteria,
tradeoffs, authorization, and consequences. Never let an AI-generated option set
erase frontline evidence or create the appearance of stakeholder agreement.

## Risks / Failure Modes

- **Technique theater:** choosing an entertaining exercise without a decision to
  make or output to ship.
- **Premature ideation:** generating solutions before observing and defining the
  actual workflow problem.
- **Groupthink:** allowing the highest-status or fastest speaker to anchor the
  room before independent input exists.
- **Consensus as evidence:** mistaking votes, excitement, or agreement for market,
  operational, or causal validation.
- **False precision:** scoring guesses in a matrix and treating the total as fact.
- **No implementation bridge:** ending with ideas but no owner, test, metric, or
  review date.
- **Historical overreach:** treating the book's 1994 company stories as proof of
  current technology, markets, or effectiveness.
