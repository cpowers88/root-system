# Opportunity Landscape Run 1 — Sector Screen

## Decision

Which broad economic sectors deserve deeper workflow-level comparison before
Chris commits scarce learning and proof time to a market?

This is a screen, not a market selection. Sector scale does not establish an
accessible buyer, a valuable workflow, or willingness to pay.

## Method and evidence boundary

The screen compares four signals:

1. economic surface or employment base;
2. recurrence of operational workflows and measurable failures;
3. growth, pressure, or change that may create a buying trigger; and
4. the difference between absolute upside and Chris's path to credible proof.

Primary-source anchors were checked on 2026-09-06. The workflow and buyer
statements below are **inferences for Run 2 testing**, not verified demand.
Current AI-use data is a readiness/competition clue only; low adoption is not
proof of demand and high adoption is not proof of saturation.

## Preliminary two-view frontier

These lists determine Run 2 research order. They do not activate an offer,
project, outreach, or specialized learning plan.

### Absolute-ceiling candidates

1. Healthcare operations — largest spend and strongest employment-growth signal.
2. Manufacturing and industrial systems — large asset, inventory, quality, and
   planning surface with plausible software/data defensibility.
3. Freight/logistics and wholesale supply networks — enormous recurring flow
   surface spanning multiple buyer types.
4. Finance and insurance operations — valuable recurring decisions and data,
   moderated by regulation and high incumbent adoption.
5. Energy/utilities and information infrastructure — high-value assets and
   scalable systems, but concentrated buyers and strong technical barriers.

Confidence is **low to medium** because no row yet names a narrow buyer,
workflow baseline, willingness-to-pay evidence, or defensible vehicle.

### Probability-adjusted candidates for Chris

1. Construction and field operations — strongest credibility, operating
   judgment, failure recognition, and language fit.
2. Real-estate/property operations — easiest safe observation route, but buying
   authority and scalable value remain weakly evidenced.
3. Wholesale/distribution and industrial supply-chain operations — close match
   to Chris's planning, material-flow, bottleneck, and crew-allocation strengths.
4. Professional-service operations — reachable information workflows and fast
   prototypes, offset by competition and lower incumbent credibility.
5. Freight/logistics operations — strong constraint-and-flow fit, but no warm
   access has been established.

Confidence is **medium for Chris's fit pattern and low for commercial access**.
Construction remains the benchmark, not the declared winner.

## Three decision-relevant findings

1. **Supply chain is not one candidate.** Manufacturing, freight, wholesale,
   construction procurement, retail replenishment, and healthcare supplies form
   a connected workflow field. Run 2 should compare procurement, inventory,
   scheduling, exception management, and asset reliability across sectors.
2. **The highest ceiling and the best entry route separate sharply.** Healthcare,
   finance, utilities, and information systems have attractive scale but also
   the strongest trust, regulation, technical, or sales-cycle barriers.
3. **Chris's durable advantage is cross-sector operating diagnosis.** His fit is
   strongest where materials, people, dates, evidence, and money must stay
   synchronized. The next screen should rank those workflows before choosing an
   industry or a technology.

## Disconfirmation and next test

This screen should change if Run 2 finds that a high-ranked sector lacks a
reachable process owner, recurring measurable loss, usable evidence, or a
vehicle with acceptable adoption and sales friction. A familiar sector also
drops if its valuable failures are already well served or controlled by buyers
Chris cannot reach.

**Next exact action:** compare 15–20 opportunity cells across procurement and
inventory, capacity and scheduling, field operations, revenue and billing,
claims and compliance, document/data flow, asset reliability, and forecasting.
Return no more than three workflow families that survive both economic and
entry-friction tests.
