# Internal AI Assistants Pathway

> Company knowledge assistants and staff copilots — a strong expansion offer for retained clients, and a trap as a first sale.

## Purpose
Define the internal AI assistant service line, and — just as important — define when *not* to sell it. This is the most hype-contaminated pathway and needs the tightest discipline.

## Key Idea
An internal assistant is only as good as the knowledge and data behind it. For a client whose processes you've already mapped ([[smb-ai-audit-method|audit]]), whose documents you've structured ([[document-automation-pathway|document automation]]), and whose systems you've integrated ([[workflow-automation-pathway|workflow automation]]), an assistant is a natural, high-value capstone. For a cold client with chaotic data, it's an unscoppable science project. **Sell it as the roof, never the foundation.**

This is explicitly *not* the "low-value chatbot gimmick" the [[what-not-to-do|What NOT To Do]] page warns about — the difference is grounding in real company data, scoped tasks, and measured usage.

## Why It Matters
- **High perceived value:** owners ask for "our own AI" unprompted; being able to deliver it credibly differentiates you.
- **Deepens the retainer:** an assistant needs continuous knowledge updates, quality monitoring, and extension — recurring work by nature. See [[retainer-model|Retainer Model]].
- **Compounds prior work:** it monetizes the data structuring and integration you already did, at high margin.

## What Gets Sold (Typical Builds)
- **Company knowledge assistant:** answers staff questions from SOPs, policies, product docs, and past project records — with citations to source documents
- **Sales/service copilot:** drafts replies to customer emails from account history and templates; human sends
- **Onboarding assistant:** walks new hires through role-specific processes and answers "how do we..." questions
- **Operations Q&A over structured data:** "what's the status of job 4471?" answered from the systems you integrated (read-only first)
- **Meeting & comms leverage:** call transcription → summaries, action items, CRM updates for review

## Delivery Notes (What Makes This Pathway Different)
- **Prerequisites are the scope:** before quoting, verify the knowledge exists in usable form. If SOPs live in someone's head, the project is "document your operations" first (a legitimate paid engagement) — not an assistant.
- **Ground everything; cite everything.** Retrieval over the client's own content with visible sources. An assistant that confidently invents policy is worse than no assistant.
- **Scope by task, not by magic:** "answers HR-policy and product-spec questions" is deliverable; "an AI that knows everything about the company" is not.
- **Measure usage weekly.** An unused assistant is a cancelled retainer in waiting; adoption work (champions, seeded FAQs, team training) is part of delivery.

## Pricing
- Scoped knowledge assistant (one domain, e.g., SOPs + policies): **$8,000–$20,000** build
- Multi-source assistant with system integrations: **$20,000–$50,000**
- Mandatory ongoing component: **$500–$2,500/mo** for knowledge maintenance, monitoring, and model updates — never sell an assistant without it. See [[pricing-models|Pricing Models]].

## Practical Actions
- Build an assistant over this wiki + your own SOPs as your first project; you'll hit every real problem (chunking, stale docs, hallucination, evaluation) at zero client risk.
- Create a pre-sale readiness checklist: documented SOPs? clean document storage? a named internal owner? No on two or more → sell the prerequisite work instead.
- Define 15–25 test questions with correct answers per assistant *before* building; that's your acceptance test.

## Beginner Version
Don't sell this to clients yet. Build one for yourself, and optionally one scoped FAQ-style assistant for an existing happy client at a modest price, clearly framed as v1. Master retrieval quality and evaluation first — see [[skill-roadmap|Skill Roadmap]].

## Intermediate Version
Scoped knowledge assistants for retained clients whose data you've already structured: one domain, grounded retrieval with citations, a 15–25-question acceptance test, and — non-negotiable — a trained client-side [[agent-manager-job-design|knowledge-base maintainer]] who owns content freshness. Usage reviewed monthly inside the retainer; an unused assistant gets fixed or retired, never ignored.

## Advanced Version
Assistants with tool use (read data, draft records for approval, kick off the automations you built), role-based access, evaluation suites run on every knowledge update, and usage analytics reported quarterly. Positioned as the capstone tier of the [[service-offer-ladder|Service Offer Ladder]].

## Revenue Connection
Lower project volume, high ticket, and the highest retainer attach rate of any pathway (near 100%, because unmaintained assistants visibly decay). Its real function in the P&L: expanding revenue per existing client without new customer-acquisition cost.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
Assistants have the strongest agent-manager conversion of any pathway: they *require* a [[agent-manager-job-design|knowledge-base maintainer]] (stale content is the #1 failure), benefit from an [[agent-manager-job-design|AI quality auditor]] running the acceptance tests after every update, and their whole value model is category-2 work (staff searching/asking) done by AI with humans applying the answers. The prerequisite conversation — "your SOPs live in someone's head" — is itself a [[human-role-redesign|role-redesign]] sale.

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **Hallucinated policy** — an assistant that confidently invents an answer is worse than none; grounding + citations + acceptance tests, always.
- **Unscoppable "AI that knows everything" expectations** — scope by task and domain in writing before quoting.
- **Decay after launch** — knowledge rots in months without a named maintainer; never sell an assistant without the maintenance component.
