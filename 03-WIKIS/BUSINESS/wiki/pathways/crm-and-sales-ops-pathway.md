# CRM & Sales Ops Pathway

> Fixing how SMBs capture, follow up, and close revenue — the pathway with the easiest ROI conversation in the business.

## Purpose
Define the CRM and sales/admin operations service line: what gets fixed, how it's delivered, and why "recovered revenue" is a stronger pitch than "saved hours."

## Key Idea
Most SMBs lose 10–30% of their potential revenue to **process failure, not market failure**: leads that never get a callback, quotes that go out days late, no follow-up after the first "let me think about it," and a CRM that's either absent or abandoned. This pathway engineers the revenue pipeline — capture, speed-to-lead, follow-up, pipeline visibility, and the admin work wrapped around sales — so fewer dollars leak out.

## Why It Matters
- **Revenue recovered beats cost saved.** Owners hesitate at "save $15K in admin time" but move fast on "you're losing ~$80K/yr in leads nobody calls back."
- **Failures are visible and measurable:** lead response time, follow-up rate, and quote turnaround are easy baselines for before/after [[05-BUSINESS/03-Case Studies/CASE_STUDY_TEMPLATE|case studies]].
- **CRM is sticky.** Once you've configured and integrated their CRM, you're structurally embedded — a strong [[retainer-model|retainer]] position.

## What Gets Sold (Typical Builds)
- **CRM rescue or implementation:** select/configure a right-sized CRM (HubSpot, Pipedrive, or the vertical tool), migrate data, design the pipeline stages, get the team actually using it
- **Speed-to-lead system:** every inbound (form, call, email, chat) logged instantly, auto-acknowledged, routed, with escalation if untouched in N minutes
- **Follow-up sequences:** automated multi-touch follow-up for quotes and stale deals, AI-drafted where personalization matters, human-approved
- **Quote/proposal ops:** templated generation, e-sign, expiry reminders (overlaps [[workflow-automation-pathway|Workflow Automation]])
- **Sales admin automation:** call logging, meeting-note capture and summarization into CRM, activity reports
- **Pipeline reporting:** conversion by stage, lead-source ROI, rep activity — the bridge into the [[data-and-dashboard-pathway|Data & Dashboard Pathway]]

## Delivery Notes (What Makes This Pathway Different)
This is the most **people-heavy** pathway: the system fails if staff don't use it. So:
- Get the owner to mandate the process, not just approve the tool
- Design for the laziest user — fewer required fields, automation fills what it can
- Train live, provide 2-minute screen recordings, and check adoption weekly for the first month
- Report adoption metrics to the owner; unused seats are your problem before they become your churn

## Pricing
- Speed-to-lead / follow-up quick win: **$3,000–$7,000**
- Full CRM implementation + sales ops build: **$8,000–$25,000**
- Ongoing sales ops management inside retainer tiers: **$1,000–$4,000/mo**
- Anchor to recovered revenue: if slow follow-up is costing ~$80K/yr, a $15K fix is obviously cheap. See [[pricing-models|Pricing Models]].

## Practical Actions
- Learn one mainstream CRM deeply (HubSpot free tier is the practical default) including its API and automation hooks — see [[tool-stack|Tool Stack]].
- Build a demo speed-to-lead system for a fictional trades company; use it in sales calls.
- In every [[smb-ai-audit-method|audit]], measure the client's actual lead response time by submitting a test inquiry. The result usually sells the project by itself.

## Beginner Version
Sell the speed-to-lead quick win: capture → instant acknowledgment → CRM record → owner alert → 3-touch follow-up sequence. One or two tools, 2–3 weeks, unmistakable results. Avoid full CRM migrations until you've done several of these.

## Intermediate Version
Full CRM implementations and follow-up systems with AI-drafted, human-approved outreach as the standard pattern: the AI writes every follow-up, a named person approves sends, and adoption metrics go to the owner weekly. You've done enough of these that the vertical's pipeline stages, fields, and automations come from your template, not from scratch.

## Advanced Version
Full revenue-ops engagements: CRM as the hub integrated with phone, email, quoting, accounting, and marketing; lead-source attribution; AI-assisted lead scoring and drafted outreach; sales ops managed continuously under a senior retainer tier ([[service-offer-ladder|Service Offer Ladder]] Rung 5).

## Revenue Connection
Mid-size project tickets with the highest close rate of any pathway (the ROI story sells itself), plus the strongest retainer stickiness. Clients whose revenue pipeline you run do not leave over price.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
Customer-facing text is the most sensitive AI-first work there is, so this pathway leans hardest on the human-review category of the [[human-agent-operating-model|operating model]]: AI drafts every follow-up and logs every call, humans approve anything a customer sees ([[quality-control-and-risk-gates|approval gates]]). The salesperson's job shifts from typing follow-ups to judging them — more selling hours, better consistency, which is the adoption pitch that wins the team over ([[human-role-redesign|Human Role Redesign]]).

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **Adoption failure** — the pathway's #1 risk: the system works, the reps don't use it. Owner mandate, laziest-user design, and weekly adoption reporting are the countermeasures.
- **AI-sent messages without review** — one hallucinated price or tone-deaf reply to a grieving customer costs the account; customer-facing sends stay human-approved until accuracy history says otherwise.
- **Full CRM migration as a first project** — data migrations are where beginners drown; sell the speed-to-lead quick win first.
