# First Workflow Observation Field Plan

> A field-ready protocol for practicing the Advisor-Builder observation method,
> producing a concise workflow report, and testing the real-estate and
> construction access wedges without disguising research as a sales pitch.

## Decision

Use a two-step sequence rather than choosing one vertical from theory:

1. **Run the first session on a narrow local real-estate workflow.** Access is
   immediate, the workflow can be observed in person, and mistakes in the
   interview method are inexpensive. Treat this as method practice and an
   access test, not proof that a brokerage or business owner will pay.
2. **Run the next qualified session with a small Boston-area construction
   owner or operations lead.** This is the stronger commercial-discovery test
   because Chris has domain credibility and may reach the buyer, but it should
   use a method already rehearsed once.
3. **Hold the medium construction company as the validation target.** Pursue it
   after the first two sessions unless access unexpectedly opens sooner. Its
   richer systems, staff handoffs, and buying center can produce stronger
   evidence, but the scheduling cost and relationship stakes make it a poor
   place to learn the basic interview rhythm.

This sequence tests both live strategy assumptions: real estate as the fastest
warm-access wedge (S-02) and construction as the stronger credibility and pain
access wedge (S-03).

## What This Session Is

The first session is a **workflow observation proof**, not the full paid
[[smb-ai-audit-method|SMB AI Audit]]. It should examine one real workflow, show
how it actually runs, identify observed friction, and return a short report.
Do not promise a complete operational diagnosis, savings total, automation, or
implementation plan from one conversation.

The session succeeds if it produces:

- one specific workflow observed from trigger to outcome;
- one recent event reconstructed with evidence;
- actual steps, tools, handoffs, waits, exceptions, and rework;
- at least one defensible time, volume, delay, or error estimate;
- clear separation of facts, estimates, interpretations, and unknowns;
- one useful recommendation or next measurement;
- one advancement commitment, even if the commitment is only a second
  observation or an introduction.

## Candidate Comparison

Score each factor from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong). The scores below are provisional
until access and role details are confirmed.

| Candidate | Fast access | Live observation | Buyer evidence | Quantifiable workflow | Repeatability signal | Scheduling ease | Best use |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Local 1099 real-estate role | 5 | 5 | 2 | 4 | 3 | 5 | First rehearsal and S-02 access test |
| Small Boston construction business | 3 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | First serious commercial-discovery test |
| Medium Boston construction company | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 1 | Later validation of complexity and buying center |

The real-estate session has two evidence limitations. A spouse will usually be
more candid but may also be more helpful and encouraging than a normal prospect.
A 1099 agent can prove personal workflow pain and access, but cannot by herself
prove that the brokerage, team lead, or business owner will buy a solution.
Record those limitations rather than discounting the session.

## Choose One Workflow, Not One Industry

Do not begin with "How could AI help your business?" Begin by choosing one
workflow that has happened recently and can be shown.

Use this selection gate before scheduling the full session. A workflow qualifies
when at least four of these are true:

- it occurred in the last 30 days;
- it occurs at least weekly or carries a material consequence when it occurs;
- the participant performs or directly manages it;
- its artifacts can be shown without exposing prohibited client data;
- it crosses at least two tools, people, or handoffs;
- it contains waiting, retyping, searching, chasing, error correction, or
  owner review;
- the participant can estimate volume and time;
- improving it would change revenue, capacity, cash timing, risk, or customer
  experience.

### Real-estate workflow candidates

Ask the participant which one produced the most recent costly or frustrating
event. Likely candidates to test, not assume, are:

- lead receipt -> first response -> qualification -> appointment;
- repeated lead follow-up across calls, texts, email, and CRM;
- showing or appointment coordination and status updates;
- offer or transaction-document coordination;
- inspection, lender, attorney, or closing follow-up;
- weekly pipeline review and deciding who needs action next.

Prefer lead follow-up if it can be shown safely: it is frequent, tied to revenue,
and exposes response time, channel switching, CRM quality, and dropped follow-up.
If the brokerage controls most of the process, explicitly map the boundary
between what the agent controls and what the brokerage controls.

### Construction workflow candidates

Prefer a workflow with an owner-visible economic consequence:

- inbound lead -> site visit -> estimate -> quote follow-up;
- bid invitation -> takeoff -> subcontractor pricing -> bid submission;
- field condition -> change-order documentation -> approval -> billing;
- daily log or timecard -> office entry -> payroll/job costing;
- material request -> purchase -> delivery -> field confirmation;
- completed work -> invoice package -> invoice sent -> cash received.

For a first construction session, **lead-to-estimate** or **change order-to-bill**
usually gives the clearest link between administrative friction and revenue.
The participant still chooses based on a recent event; do not force a textbook
workflow onto the company.

## Privacy and Permission Boundary

Before the session:

- explain that the exercise maps the process, not employee performance;
- ask permission before recording audio, viewing screens, or retaining artifacts;
- use redacted, synthetic, or already-closed examples where possible;
- do not copy customer names, contact details, contract terms, access tokens,
  brokerage data, or company-confidential documents into `.ROOT`;
- keep any authorized client-specific notes in the separately approved client
  workspace; store only sanitized patterns and method improvements here;
- stop screen observation when unrelated private information appears.

For the family session, apply the same consent standard as an external interview.
The relationship does not replace permission.

## Preparation: 20-30 Minutes

### 1. Write the learning card

Complete this before the call:

```text
Participant role:
Candidate segment:
Workflow to inspect:
Trigger:
Desired outcome:

Assumption A:
Assumption B:
Assumption C:

What evidence would strengthen each assumption:
What evidence would weaken each assumption:
Kill question:
Requested advancement:
```

Good assumptions are falsifiable. Example: "Lead follow-up requires repeated
manual checking across three channels and causes qualified leads to wait more
than one business day." A weak assumption is: "AI would help real estate."

The **kill question** should make it possible to learn that no material problem
exists. Example: "Show me the last five leads from arrival through disposition;
were any response or follow-up steps late, missing, or manually reconstructed?"

### 2. Request one recent example

Send this message when scheduling:

> I am practicing a workflow-observation method, not pitching software. Could I
> spend 45-60 minutes watching one recurring process you personally handle? I
> want to map the steps, tools, handoffs, and waiting, then give you a short
> written workflow report. Please choose a recent completed example that you can
> show safely. We will redact private customer information, and you can stop the
> observation at any point.

For a business owner, add:

> If the report is useful, I may ask for a second observation or an introduction
> to the person who owns the process. There is no software proposal attached to
> this session.

### 3. Prepare the evidence sheet

Create one row per step:

| # | Actor | Action | Tool/artifact | Active time | Wait time | Handoff | Exception/rework | Evidence type |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|---|

Use evidence labels consistently:

- **O:** directly observed;
- **R:** supported by a record, timestamp, or artifact;
- **S:** participant estimate;
- **I:** Chris's inference;
- **U:** unknown or not yet verified.

Never present `S`, `I`, or `U` as an observed fact.

## Session Agenda: 45-60 Minutes

### Minute 0-5: contract the session

Use this opening:

> I am mapping one process, not evaluating you or trying to sell an AI tool.
> I will ask about the last real example and may interrupt vague answers to get
> the actual step. I will separate what I observe from estimates and send you a
> short report to correct. Is it okay to proceed, and is there anything I should
> not view or record?

Confirm:

- the participant's role in the workflow;
- the start and end boundary;
- recording and artifact permissions;
- the scheduled stop time;
- what a useful report would help the participant decide.

### Minute 5-12: reconstruct the last event

Ask these in order:

1. "When did this most recently happen?"
2. "What started it?"
3. "What did you receive first, and where did it appear?"
4. "What did you do next?"
5. "What counted as complete?"
6. "What was unusual or difficult about this example?"

If the answer becomes generic, say: "Let us stay with that specific one. What
was on the screen or in your hand at that moment?"

### Minute 12-35: watch the workflow

Ask the participant to replay or demonstrate the safe example. Use neutral
prompts:

- "Show me where it enters."
- "What do you look for before moving it forward?"
- "Where does that information come from?"
- "Do you type or copy that anywhere else?"
- "Who receives it next, and how do they know it is ready?"
- "What happens while you wait?"
- "How do you notice that something was missed?"
- "Show me the exception that takes the longest."
- "What judgment would you never want automated?"

Capture every tool switch, manual search, duplicate entry, interruption, queue,
approval, correction, and unofficial workaround. Do not recommend fixes during
the demonstration. A premature solution changes what the participant shows.

### Minute 35-45: quantify carefully

For each meaningful friction point ask:

- "How often did this occur in the last week or month?"
- "How long did this instance take, excluding waiting?"
- "How long did it wait before the next action?"
- "Who else spends time on it?"
- "What happens when it is late or wrong?"
- "Is there a timestamp, report, calendar, invoice, or sample that could verify
  that estimate?"
- "Have you tried to fix this before? What did you spend or change?"

Use ranges when exact data does not exist. Do not invent a labor rate, conversion
rate, or revenue loss. Mark it unknown and state what record would resolve it.

### Minute 45-52: test the diagnosis

Replay the workflow in plain language:

> I heard that the process starts with [trigger], crosses [tools/people], and
> usually finishes when [outcome]. The main friction appears to be [friction],
> supported by [evidence]. The consequence may be [time/delay/risk], but [unknown]
> still needs verification. What did I misunderstand?

Then ask the disconfirming questions:

- "When does this process work perfectly?"
- "Is this annoying but economically harmless?"
- "Could the apparent waste be a necessary control?"
- "What would make changing it a bad idea?"
- "Who would disagree with this diagnosis?"

### Minute 52-60: secure advancement

Ask for one action matched to what remains unknown:

- permission to observe a second instance;
- a non-sensitive volume or timestamp export;
- an introduction to the upstream/downstream role;
- a conversation with the owner or decision-maker;
- a dated review of the draft report;
- for a qualified business prospect, permission to scope a paid audit.

Use a direct close:

> The remaining question is [unknown]. The best next proof is [specific action].
> Are you willing to do that by [date]?

Praise without a dated action is not advancement.

## Quantification Rules

Use conservative arithmetic and show every assumption.

### Labor capacity

```text
annual hours = minutes per instance / 60 x instances per month x 12 x people
annual labor capacity = annual hours x loaded hourly cost
```

Call this **capacity consumed**, not cash savings, unless the company would
actually reduce overtime, contractor expense, or headcount. Recovered employee
time may create more selling, production, or customer capacity without reducing
payroll.

### Delay

Report delay separately from labor:

```text
median wait = typical elapsed time between ready and next action
annual delayed cases = cases per period x periods
```

Do not convert delay into revenue unless records establish a credible link to
lost conversion, penalties, missed billing, or delayed cash.

### Errors and rework

```text
annual rework hours = error rate x annual volume x rework time per error
```

If the participant remembers only severe failures, label the estimate as recall
evidence and request a record-based sample.

### Revenue and cash

Use separate categories:

- **revenue leakage:** business that likely would have been won or retained;
- **cash-timing delay:** money earned but invoiced or collected later;
- **cost:** labor, fees, rework, penalties, or duplicate software;
- **risk exposure:** material but not honestly monetizable from current evidence.

Never add cash-timing delay to annual cost as if the revenue disappeared.

## The Workflow Report: 2-4 Pages

Write and send the report within 24 hours while the evidence is fresh.

### 1. Scope and evidence

- participant role and workflow boundary;
- session date and duration;
- evidence reviewed: observation, artifacts, records, and estimates;
- explicit exclusions and privacy limits.

### 2. Current-state map

Use one line per step:

```text
trigger -> receive -> inspect -> re-enter -> wait -> approve -> send -> outcome
```

Annotate actors, tools, active time, waiting, handoffs, and exceptions. The map
must be recognizable to the participant without explanation.

### 3. Findings

Limit the first report to the three strongest findings. For each include:

```text
Finding:
Observed condition:
Evidence and confidence:
Operational consequence:
Conservative magnitude:
Unknowns:
Recommended next action:
Human judgment that must remain:
```

Do not recommend AI by default. The correct first response may be eliminating a
step, clarifying ownership, using an existing feature, improving data capture,
or collecting better measurements.

### 4. Priority table

| Finding | Impact | Confidence | Ease | Risk | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rank high-impact, high-confidence, low-risk changes first. A large speculative
benefit ranks below a smaller observed leak.

### 5. Next proof

End with exactly one recommended next step, its owner, due date, and the evidence
it should produce. Do not attach a broad implementation roadmap to a one-workflow
observation.

## Report Quality Gate

Do not send until every answer is yes:

- [ ] The report describes one bounded workflow rather than the whole business.
- [ ] Every material claim is labeled observed, recorded, estimated, inferred,
  or unknown.
- [ ] The participant could recognize the map as their actual work.
- [ ] Calculations show units, volume, period, people, and assumptions.
- [ ] Capacity, cost, revenue leakage, cash timing, and risk are not conflated.
- [ ] Necessary controls and human judgment are preserved.
- [ ] Recommendations solve diagnosed friction rather than display AI capability.
- [ ] Private identifiers and prohibited artifacts are absent from `.ROOT`.
- [ ] The participant has a correction window before any lesson is reused.
- [ ] The report ends with one specific next proof.

## Post-Session Evidence Review

Within 10 minutes of the session, complete this ledger:

| Evidence question | Result |
|---|---|
| Last concrete event captured? | Yes / No |
| Workflow observed live? | Yes / No / Partial |
| Current workaround documented? | Yes / No |
| Frequency verified? | Record / Estimate / Unknown |
| Consequence quantified? | Verified / Range / Unknown |
| Decision owner identified? | Yes / No |
| Prior attempt or spend? | Yes / No / None |
| Advancement secured? | Action + date / Stall |
| Strategy assumption informed? | S-01 / S-02 / S-03 / None |

Then classify the outcome:

- **Advance:** observed recurring pain, credible consequence, and a real next
  commitment. Continue within the same workflow or segment.
- **Measure:** pain appears real but the magnitude is unverified. Request the
  smallest record or second observation needed.
- **Park:** inconvenience exists but has little consequence, low frequency, or
  no process owner willing to act.
- **Reject:** the presumed problem does not exist, is already solved, or depends
  on access that cannot be granted.

One session does not establish a vertical. Review after three to five comparable
workflow conversations. Repeatable evidence means the same workflow, consequence,
buyer, and advancement pattern recurs across independent participants.

## Recommended Execution Order

### Session 1: local real-estate rehearsal

- Select one workflow from a real event in the prior 30 days.
- Favor lead follow-up if it qualifies under the selection gate.
- Run the full 45-60 minute protocol and deliver the 2-4 page report.
- Treat feedback as method calibration and S-02 access evidence.
- Ask for an introduction to one independent agent, team lead, broker, attorney,
  investor, or developer who experiences a comparable workflow.

### Session 2: small construction commercial discovery

- Request the owner or operations lead plus access to the person performing the
  selected workflow.
- Choose lead-to-estimate or change order-to-bill based on the last concrete pain.
- Observe an artifact trail or safe screen replay, not only the owner's account.
- Ask whether the result merits a broader paid operational audit.
- Treat buyer access, live observation, repeated waste, and willingness to
  continue as evidence for S-01 and S-03.

### Session 3: medium construction validation

- Approach only with the improved protocol and a sanitized sample report.
- Identify initiator, evaluator, gatekeeper, and decision-maker before assuming
  the interviewee can authorize anything.
- Use the session to test whether the small-company pattern survives more tools,
  roles, controls, and handoffs.
- Do not offer free enterprise-wide analysis. Bound the observation to one
  workflow and use it to qualify a paid audit.

## Stop Conditions

Stop or rescope if:

- the participant cannot show a recent example;
- the workflow is governed entirely by a third party and the participant cannot
  influence or measure it;
- private data cannot be safely separated from observation;
- the conversation remains hypothetical after two attempts to anchor it;
- the participant wants an implementation design before granting workflow
  access;
- Chris begins solving during observation instead of collecting evidence.
