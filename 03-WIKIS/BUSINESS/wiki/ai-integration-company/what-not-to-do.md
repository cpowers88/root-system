# What NOT To Do

> The explicit exclusion list: business models, behaviors, and temptations that look like progress and aren't.

## Purpose
Name the traps in advance. Every item here either burns time without building the [[north-star-alignment|North Star]] asset, or actively damages the positioning and economics the rest of this wiki constructs. When tempted, read the entry; the temptation usually dies on contact with the reasoning.

## Key Idea
The opportunity cost of this business is enormous: every hour spent on a dead-end "AI opportunity" is an hour not spent on the compounding loop (audit → build → retainer → proof). The exclusion list isn't moralizing — it's **portfolio discipline**. Most items below can make *someone* money; none of them build a firm worth tens of millions on the diagnose–build–maintain model.

## Excluded Business Models

### ❌ Get-rich-quick AI side hustles
"Make $10K/mo with AI in 30 days" schemes. They optimize for extraction from beginners, not value to businesses. Zero transferable assets: no clients, no proof, no skills the [[most-profitable-pathways|pathways]] need.

### ❌ Faceless content spam
AI-generated YouTube channels, blog farms, social accounts. Race-to-the-bottom economics, platform-dependent, and reputationally toxic for someone selling *reliability* to business owners.

### ❌ Dropshipping and e-commerce arbitrage
A different business entirely — consumer traffic and margin games, not B2B systems engineering. Builds nothing toward the North Star.

### ❌ Prompt-pack / template / course selling
Selling "500 ChatGPT prompts" or beginner courses before having real results. Sell outcomes to businesses, not shortcuts to aspirants. (A course built on 50 real case studies in year 5 is a different conversation.)

### ❌ Low-value chatbot gimmicks
Website chat widgets sold at $99/mo, "AI receptionists" with no process integration, novelty bots. They churn, they embarrass, and they position you as a trinket vendor. The [[internal-ai-assistants-pathway|Internal Assistants pathway]] defines the line: grounded in real company data, scoped to real tasks, measured — or not sold at all.

### ❌ Venture-scale fantasy
Raising money to build "the AI platform for X" before ever serving a paying SMB. This wiki's model is self-funding by design; software emerges *from* delivery IP in year 4+ if the data supports it ([[ten-year-scale-plan|Ten-Year Scale Plan]], Route B) — not from a pitch deck in month 2.

## Excluded Positioning & Sales Behaviors

### ❌ Selling "AI" instead of outcomes
The moment the pitch is about technology, you're competing with every hype merchant. The pitch is quantified waste and recovered dollars ([[sales-system|Sales System]]).

### ❌ Free audits and spec work
Free diagnosis attracts non-buyers and reprices your expertise at zero. Discounted-for-proof with the first 2–3 clients, fine; free, never ([[pricing-models|Pricing Models]]).

### ❌ Hourly billing for delivery
Caps income, punishes skill, invites micromanagement. Fixed-fee, value-anchored, always.

### ❌ Serving everyone
"We help businesses use AI" closes nothing. Vertical focus is a compounding asset ([[market-map|Market Map]]); generality is a permanent restart.

### ❌ Overpromising AI capabilities
"100% accurate," "fully autonomous," "replaces your staff" — each phrase creates a disappointed client and a reference you can't use. Under-promise, instrument, and report honestly ([[fulfillment-system|Fulfillment System]]).

## Excluded Operating Behaviors

### ❌ Tool-collecting and cert-collecting
A new platform each month, credentials nobody asks for. Clients buy outcomes and proof; the [[tool-stack|Tool Stack]] evaluation gate exists precisely to stop this.

### ❌ Building products before doing services
Inverting the sequence — "I'll just build the SaaS" — skips the part where services teach you what's worth productizing and pay you to learn it.

### ❌ Skipping the audit to close faster
Unscoped projects are where margins, timelines, and reputations die ([[risks-and-failure-modes|Risks & Failure Modes]] #5). The audit isn't sales friction; it's the risk-control system.

### ❌ Automating without process mapping
"Just automate what we do" sounds efficient and builds faster chaos. No workflow gets automated before its current state is mapped and its redesigned future state is agreed — that's the [[smb-ai-audit-method|audit method]] and [[human-agent-operating-model|task classification]] doing their jobs.

### ❌ Replacing staff without redesigning roles
Selling headcount cuts is both bad ethics and bad engineering: the veteran's undocumented exception-handling is what keeps the workflow alive, and it leaves with her. The offer is capacity and [[human-role-redesign|role redesign]] — AI takes the typing, people take the judgment. If a client only wants bodies removed, that's a fit problem.

### ❌ Selling tools instead of outcomes
"You need HubSpot and Make" is a purchase order, not an engagement. Clients buy fewer leaks, faster follow-up, and visible operations; tools are implementation detail ([[north-star-alignment|North Star]]).

### ❌ Letting clients turn projects into unlimited support
Every delivered system attracts "quick questions" forever. Unbounded goodwill support is an unpriced retainer — the answer is the real [[retainer-model|retainer]], offered at handoff, with support defined inside a tier.

### ❌ Taking hostage positions
Running client systems in your accounts, hoarding credentials, obfuscating builds so clients "need" you. Retention comes from delivered value ([[retainer-model|Retainer Model]]); hostage-taking destroys the referral engine this business runs on — and it's wrong.

### ❌ Hiring to look like a company
Office, brand agency, headcount before margin. Every hire follows demonstrated margin and a written playbook ([[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]]).

### ❌ Chasing enterprise deals early
Six-month procurement cycles, security reviews, and committee politics will consume a young firm's entire pipeline capacity for one maybe. Grow into up-market via the [[retainer-model|Partner-tier retainer]], on the strength of a case-study moat, in year 4+.

## The Litmus Test
When a new opportunity appears, run it through the [[north-star-alignment|alignment filter]] plus one question:

> **"Does this make the core loop — audit → build → retainer → proof — stronger, or does it compete with it for my hours?"**

Strengthens it → evaluate properly (one new offer per quarter max). Competes with it → the answer is no, regardless of how lucrative it looks this week.

## Practical Actions
- Re-read this page whenever an "opportunity" produces excitement disproportionate to its fit — that feeling is the tell.
- Keep a "graveyard list" of everything declined and why; reviewing it quarterly proves the discipline is paying (or surfaces a rule worth changing deliberately, which is allowed — drift is not).
- Give a trusted peer permission to ask, "which item on your not-to-do list is this?"

## Intermediate Version
The mid-stage temptations change shape: the big unscoped project from a warm client, the "strategic partnership" that's really free consulting, the adjacent offer that would fork your delivery stack, and the client who wants staff cut with no redesign. Same litmus test, bigger dollar signs attached to the wrong answers.

## Beginner Version
The beginner's three: **no free work, no selling "AI," no tool-collecting.** Those three rules alone put you ahead of 90% of people entering this market.

## Advanced Version
At scale, the list governs corporate development: no acquisitions outside the model, no products without services-proven demand, no clients that fail the filter no matter the logo. The discipline that protected a solo founder's hours now protects a firm's balance sheet.

## Revenue Connection
Every exclusion redirects hours into the loop that compounds. The difference between year-3 outcomes of otherwise identical founders is rarely talent — it's how much of their two thousand annual working hours went into the loop versus the graveyard list.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
Several exclusions are the [[progressive-operating-thesis|thesis]] stated negatively: no automation without process mapping (the loop starts with mapped work), no staff replacement without [[human-role-redesign|role redesign]] (humans move up, not out), no autonomy promises without [[quality-control-and-risk-gates|gates]]. The exclusion list is what keeps the "redesign of work" positioning honest.

## Risks / Failure Modes
The page *is* the failure-mode list — but it has one of its own: **the list only works when read at the moment of temptation.** Excitement disproportionate to strategic fit is the tell; that's the moment to open this page, not after the quarter is spent.
