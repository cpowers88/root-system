# Sales System

> How to find SMB owners, earn the conversation, and close audits — without paid ads, hype, or pretending to be bigger than you are.

## Purpose
Define the repeatable system for generating and closing clients: channels, messaging, the sales conversation itself, and the pipeline discipline that keeps revenue predictable.

## Key Idea
You are not selling AI; you are selling **a diagnosis of a pain the owner already feels**, delivered by someone who evidently understands how their business runs. The entire sales system is engineered to earn one thing: a booked [[smb-ai-audit-method|audit]]. Everything after that — projects, retainers — is sold by delivered work, not by salesmanship. This makes the sales problem small and concrete: *book 2–4 qualified audit conversations per week.*

The positioning, in one sentence:

> **"I help SMB owners find where time, leads, money, and information are leaking, then redesign the workflow so AI handles repeatable admin while staff manage exceptions and customer judgment."**

The offer should sound like operational engineering, never like an AI pitch.

## What You're Selling (Angles That Land)
Faster lead response · less owner dependency · fewer missed tasks · cleaner CRM · better follow-up · faster reporting · reduced admin load · better staff consistency · visible operations · **more capacity without more headcount**. Every one of these is a felt pain with a dollar figure behind it.

**Never say:** "I can add AI to your business" · "I build chatbots" · "AI will replace your admin staff" · "this will be passive income." Each of those either commoditizes you, scares the staff who must adopt the system, or promises what you can't deliver ([[what-not-to-do|What NOT To Do]]).

## Why It Matters
Delivery skill without a sales system is a hobby. Most technical founders fail here — not because selling is hard, but because they do it sporadically, apologetically, and without a script. A system (fixed channels, fixed cadence, fixed conversation structure) removes the daily decision of *whether* to sell. See [[risks-and-failure-modes|Risks & Failure Modes]] — inconsistent pipeline is failure mode #1.
- **Consulting is a "credence" purchase** (Ch.12, *Principles of Marketing*, intake 2026-07-12): unlike a product, a client can't fully judge the quality of an audit or build even after buying it — they have to trust the seller going in. This is *why* [[case-study-template|proof content]] and warm referrals carry outsized weight in the channel list below, more than for a business selling something inspectable before purchase.

## Who You're Actually Selling To (added 2026-07-12, *Principles of Marketing* Ch.7, pp.330-333)
The Sales Conversation below assumes a single owner-buyer. That holds for the smaller end of [[market-map|Market Map]]'s range, but toward the upper end (up to 200 employees) a real "buying center" often exists — up to ~4 distinct roles that rarely show up on an org chart:
- **Initiator** — the person who first raises "we have a problem here" (often not the owner — an office manager or ops lead).
- **Influencer/evaluator** — sets the criteria the audit gets judged against.
- **Gatekeeper** — controls Chris's access to the owner (often the same office manager).
- **Decider / purchaser** — signs and pays; usually still the owner at this size, but not always.

C-level buyers specifically screen vendors for: ability to marshal resources on the client's behalf, demonstrated understanding of the buyer's actual business goals (not just the technology), responsiveness, and willingness to be held accountable. Practical read: in a discovery call at a larger prospect, ask directly "who else should be in this conversation?" before assuming the person on the phone is the sole decision-maker — missing the gatekeeper or influencer is a common reason a well-run discovery call still doesn't close.

## Channels (In Priority Order)

### 1. Warm network & referrals (best close rate, start here)
Everyone you know who owns, runs, or works inside an SMB. The ask is soft: "I diagnose where businesses lose money to manual work — who do you know drowning in admin?" After every delivered project: a direct, specific referral request. Target: referrals become 50%+ of new business by year 2.

### 2. Local & vertical communities
Chambers of commerce, trade associations for your [[market-map|target verticals]], BNI-style groups, local business events. You're often the only person in the room who can do this work. Offer a 20-minute "where service businesses leak money to manual work" talk — it books audits.

### 3. Strategic partners
Bookkeepers/accountants, MSPs (IT shops), business brokers, marketing agencies, fractional CFOs — they see operational pain daily and can't fix it. Referral fee (10–15% of first project) or reciprocal referrals. Five active partners can fill a pipeline by themselves.

### 4. Direct outreach (cold email / LinkedIn / calls)
Works only when specific: name their process, not your technology. *"Most HVAC companies take 2+ days to get quotes out; the ones responding in 2 hours win the job. I run a short paid audit that shows exactly where your quote process loses jobs — worth a 15-minute call?"* Volume: 10–15 highly researched contacts/day beats 200 spray-and-pray.

### 5. Proof content (supporting, not primary)
Case studies ([[case-study-template|template]]), before/after posts, short teardown videos of common broken workflows. Purpose: making the other four channels convert better, not going viral.

## The Sales Conversation (Audit Close)
1. **Discovery (15–20 min):** how work flows through the business, where things pile up, what's most annoying, what it's costing them (get *their* estimate first).
2. **Mirror:** replay their pains in their own words, sharper than they said them. This is where credibility is won.
3. **Bridge:** "These are exactly the leaks my audit quantifies. In two weeks you get every problem priced in dollars and a prioritized fix-list — whether or not you ever hire me to fix them."
4. **Close:** price, timeline, what you need from them, start date. Then stop talking.
5. **Objections:** "Need to think" → "What would you need to see to feel confident?" / "Expensive" → compare to one month of the admin cost they just described / "We tried a tool before" → "Tools fail without diagnosis; that's why I audit first."

Use the full [[negotiation-toolkit|Negotiation Toolkit]] for this conversation — Mirroring and Calibrated Questions for discovery, the Accusation Audit before a skeptical first meeting, and the "That's Right" test to check whether a "Yes" at the close is real commitment or a polite dodge. For fee/retainer pushback specifically, the toolkit's Ackerman Model and "don't compromise" discipline hold price better than splitting the difference.

## Pipeline Discipline
- Track every prospect in a CRM (yes, use what you sell): stages Lead → Conversation → Audit proposed → Audit sold → Project → Retainer.
- **Non-negotiable weekly minimums** (beginner): 10 outreach touches, 2 partner/community interactions, 1 piece of proof shared. Sales activity happens on scheduled days *especially* when delivery is busy — the deadly cycle is deliver → empty pipeline → panic.
- Review weekly: conversations booked, audits proposed, close rate. Fix the *first* broken number, not the last.

## Practical Actions
- Write your one-sentence positioning: "I help [vertical] businesses find and fix the manual work costing them $50–200K/yr — starting with a 2-week paid audit."
- List 50 warm contacts and 5 potential referral partners; contact 5/day using the ready-made [[outreach-scripts|outreach scripts]].
- Rehearse the [[audit-sales-call-script|audit sales call script]] out loud before the first real conversation.
- Track weekly activity on the [[weekly-scorecard|weekly scorecard]].

## Beginner Version
Channels 1–2 only. Goal: **first 3 audits within 60 days**, sourced from people who already trust you or rooms you show up in. No website funnel needed — a one-page description of the audit and a calendar link suffice. See [[first-30-days|First 30 Days]].

## Intermediate Version
Channels 1–4 all active with tracked source attribution, 2–3 referral partners producing, the vertical talk booked quarterly, and case studies armed in every channel. The pipeline runs at 2–4 qualified conversations per week without heroics, and the weekly minimums survive delivery crunches because they're scheduled, not aspirational.

## Advanced Version
All five channels instrumented with source-attribution, a partner program with tracked referral economics, outbound run to a documented playbook (possibly by a part-time SDR), and your own dashboard showing pipeline coverage vs. next-quarter target — you run the sales system you sell. See [[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]].

## Revenue Connection
The math: 10 audit conversations/month × 40% close = 4 audits × $2,500 = $10K/mo *before* the 50–70% of audits that convert to $5K–$25K projects and then retainers. Every metric in the [[one-year-plan|One-Year Plan]] traces back to conversations booked per week.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
The [[progressive-operating-thesis|thesis]] is a sales weapon: "your people move up, they don't move out" disarms the staff-replacement fear that quietly kills SMB deals, and the [[quality-control-and-risk-gates|gate architecture]] answers the risk objection the cautious buyer won't voice. Sell the redesigned operation — AI producing, their team managing — and you're the only vendor in the conversation not selling a tool.

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **Feast/famine pipeline** — the #1 business killer; weekly minimums are non-negotiable, hardest-enforced when busiest.
- **Selling AI novelty** — the moment the pitch is technology, you're competing with every hype merchant on price.
- **Sporadic, apologetic selling** — a system (fixed channels, cadence, script) removes the daily decision of whether to sell; see [[risks-and-failure-modes|Risks & Failure Modes]] #15.
