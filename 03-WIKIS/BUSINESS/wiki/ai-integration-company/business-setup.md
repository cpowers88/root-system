# Business Setup

> The legal, financial, and administrative foundation: what you actually need before client #1, before client #3, and before hire #1 — and what can wait.

## Purpose
List the real-world setup steps (entity, banking, insurance, contracts, taxes) tiered by when they're genuinely needed, so none of them becomes either a procrastination excuse ("I can't sell until my LLC is perfect") or a landmine ("I signed a $25K deal on a handshake").

## Key Idea
Setup is **tiered, not front-loaded**. A minimal tier before the first client, a protection tier before real money, an employer tier before the first hire. Doing tier 3 in week 1 is procrastination; skipping tier 2 at $10K/month is gambling. (Nothing here is legal or tax advice — confirm specifics for your state with a professional; the point of this page is knowing what to ask them.)

## Tier 1 — Before the First Paid Client (Week 1–2, ~$500–$1,000)
- [ ] **LLC filed** in your home state (online filing; most states $50–$500). Single-member is fine to start
- [ ] **EIN** from the IRS (free, 10 minutes, irs.gov)
- [ ] **Business bank account** — every business dollar in/out flows through it from day one; commingling is the classic first-year mess
- [ ] **Business password manager** with per-client vaults (this is also a delivery requirement — [[tool-stack|Tool Stack]])
- [ ] **Basic service agreement** for audits: scope, price, payment terms, IP ownership, confidentiality, limitation of liability. A reviewed template is fine at this stage; have a lawyer glance at it (~1 hour of fees) rather than draft from scratch
- [ ] **Invoicing + bookkeeping**: QuickBooks/Xero from the first invoice, not from tax season

## Tier 2 — Before Real Money (~Client #3 or First $10K Project)
- [ ] **Master Service Agreement + Statement of Work structure**: MSA holds the standing terms (liability cap, data handling, warranty disclaimers, payment terms, termination); each project gets a short SOW referencing it (use the [[scope-of-work-template|scope-of-work template]])
- [ ] **Professional liability (E&O) insurance** — you are automating people's revenue and financial flows; get quoted (typically $500–$1,500/yr at this size)
- [ ] **Cyber liability insurance** — mandatory before the first [[document-automation-pathway|document automation]] or data-heavy client
- [ ] **Data-handling one-pager**: where client data lives, which AI providers process it, retention policy, breach response. Written *before* a client asks — being ready is a sales asset
- [ ] **Accountant engaged** (not just software): quarterly estimated taxes, S-corp election analysis once profit clears ~$50–80K/yr (can save five figures in self-employment tax — ask, don't guess)
- [ ] **Retainer agreement template**: auto-renewal, 30-day termination, annual escalator, response-time commitments ([[retainer-model|Retainer Model]])

## Tier 3 — Before Hire #1 (Per the [[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]])
- [ ] Contractor vs. employee decision made with the accountant (misclassification is an expensive mistake)
- [ ] Contractor agreements with IP assignment (everything built for clients must be owned by the company, not the builder)
- [ ] Payroll service if employee; workers' comp where required
- [ ] Employee/contractor access controls: per-person credentials, offboarding checklist (day-one revocation)
- [ ] Simple employee handbook or engagement standards doc — your [[fulfillment-system|fulfillment playbook]] does most of this work already

## Standing Financial Hygiene (From Day One)
- **Pay yourself deliberately**: owner draws on a schedule, not "whatever's in the account"
- **Tax reserve**: move 25–30% of every dollar of profit to a separate tax sub-account the day payment lands
- **Cash buffer target**: 3 months of business + personal fixed costs before scaling spend ([[financial-model|Financial Model]])
- **Monthly close**: 30 minutes reconciling books every month beats 30 hours every April

## What NOT To Set Up Yet
Consistent with [[what-not-to-do|What NOT To Do]]:
- ❌ Trademark filings, multi-state registrations, Delaware C-corp structures — venture cosplay for a services firm
- ❌ Office space, branded merch, expensive website — a one-pager and a calendar link sell audits
- ❌ Complex holding-company structures — relevant at [[ten-year-scale-plan|ten-year-plan]] scale, noise now

## Practical Actions
- Do all of Tier 1 this week; it's one focused afternoon plus filing wait times.
- Put Tier 2 triggers in your task system now ("On signing client #3 → MSA + insurance quotes") so they fire automatically.
- Ask one accountant and one small-business lawyer for a fixed-fee "setup review" — a few hundred dollars for confidence that compounds.

## Beginner Version
Tier 1 only, done fast, then back to outreach. The entity doesn't make money; conversations do.

## Advanced Version
Tiers 1–3 complete plus: annual insurance review as data exposure grows, contract terms tightened from actual incident/negotiation history, diligence-ready document room (contracts, financials, client agreements organized) maintained continuously — which the [[ten-year-scale-plan|Ten-Year Scale Plan]] eventually cashes in.

## Revenue Connection
Setup doesn't generate revenue — it *protects* it: the MSA's liability cap protects the balance sheet, insurance protects against the one bad event, clean books enable the pricing and margin decisions in [[financial-model|Financial Model]], and all of it compounds into the "clean company" premium at exit.
