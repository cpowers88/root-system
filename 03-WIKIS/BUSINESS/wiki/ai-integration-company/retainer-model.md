# Retainer Model

> The recurring-revenue engine: how maintenance, optimization, and ongoing engineering turn projects into a valuable company.

## Purpose
Define the retainer offer in detail — tiers, deliverables, economics, and conversion mechanics — because recurring revenue is what separates a freelancer's income from a firm's enterprise value.

## Key Idea
Every system you build **degrades without attention**: APIs change, software updates break integrations, staff turn over, volumes grow, models improve. That reality is not a liability — it is the business model. The retainer sells *accountability for working systems*: monitoring, maintenance, support, continuous optimization, and a standing engineering relationship. Projects are how you earn the right to sell it; the retainer is what you actually build the company on.

## Why It Matters
- **Predictable revenue** funds hiring, smooths the feast/famine cycle, and lets you decline bad projects.
- **Enterprise value:** service firms sell for roughly 0.5–1× revenue on project income but meaningful multiples on contracted recurring revenue. The [[ten-year-scale-plan|Ten-Year Scale Plan]] is arithmetically impossible without this page.
- **Compounding client value:** retained clients buy more projects at zero acquisition cost, because you're already inside seeing the next problem.

## Retainer Tiers

### Tier 1 — Maintain ($500–$1,500/mo)
Monitoring and failure alerts on all built automations, fixes for anything that breaks, platform/API updates, email support, monthly health report. *Position: insurance on the systems.*

### Tier 2 — Optimize ($1,500–$4,000/mo)
Everything in Maintain, plus a monthly block of enhancement hours (new automation steps, workflow tweaks, small builds), prompt/SOP/rule updates from the exception log ([[quality-control-and-risk-gates|calibration loop]]), priority response, quarterly mini-audit of one process area, dashboard upkeep, staff support. *Position: your systems keep getting better.*

### Tier 3 — Partner ($4,000–$8,000/mo+)
Everything in Optimize, plus roadmap ownership, quarterly business review with the owner, new-system builds up to a defined size included, vendor/software selection support, staff training. Effectively a fractional automation-engineering department — Rung 5 of the [[service-offer-ladder|Service Offer Ladder]].

### Tier 4 — Strategic Operations Partner ($5,000–$15,000/mo, larger SMBs)
The advanced-operations tier for bigger clients (upper [[market-map|sweet spot]] and beyond): everything in Partner, plus you effectively staff their [[agent-manager-job-design|agent-manager and process-designer roles]] across departments — continuous workflow redesign, quarterly re-audits, new pathway rollouts, and org-level [[human-role-redesign|role redesign]] as they grow. Only for clients with 12+ months of history, multiple systems under management, and executive sponsorship. This is where the [[ten-year-scale-plan|up-market creep]] happens.

## Conversion Mechanics
- **Offer at handoff, every time.** The close of every project includes: "Here's what keeps this running. Who's responsible for that — your team, or ours?" Target: 60%+ of implementation clients take a retainer.
- **Bake the first 30–60 days of support into the project price**, then convert to paid retainer — the client experiences the value before paying for it.
- **Never sell "hours."** Sell outcomes and responsibilities (uptime, response time, quarterly improvements). Hour-based retainers get audited and nickel-and-dimed.
- **Annual escalation clause** (3–5%) and 30-day-out terms in writing. See [[pricing-models|Pricing Models]].

## Delivering Retainers Profitably
- **Monitoring must be automated.** Centralized failure alerts across all client automations ([[tool-stack|Tool Stack]]); checking things by hand destroys the margin.
- **Track effort per client monthly.** A Tier 1 client consuming Tier 3 attention gets a documented conversation and a re-tier, not silent margin erosion.
- **The monthly report is the retention tool.** One page: what was monitored, what was fixed before they noticed, what was improved, hours/dollars saved to date. Invisible work churns; reported work renews.
- Target gross margin: **70%+** at Tier 1–2 (mostly automated), 50–60% at Tier 3 (real senior time).

## Practical Actions
- Write the three tier descriptions as a one-pager now, even before you have retainer clients — it disciplines every project handoff.
- Build the monitoring/alerting backbone before client #3; retrofitting it across ten clients is far more painful.
- Create the monthly report template; automate its data population early.

## Beginner Version
One tier: $500–$1,500/mo — monitoring, fixes, small tweaks, monthly summary email. Offer it to every project client at handoff. Three retainer clients ≈ your first reliable $2K–$4K/mo baseline while you keep selling projects.

## Intermediate Version
Tiers 1–2 published and running across 5–10 clients with automated monitoring, monthly reports that explicitly show the [[quality-control-and-risk-gates|calibration loop]] working (exceptions down, straight-through rates up, rules updated), and quarterly re-classification of borderline tasks as models improve — visible proof the retainer is engineering, not insurance theater.

## Advanced Version
Four published tiers, contracted terms, automated monitoring and reporting, a delivery team owning retainer work separate from project builds, QBRs run for Tier 2–3, and net-revenue-retention tracked as a core KPI (target >100% — expansion outpacing churn).

## Revenue Connection
The math that matters: 25 clients averaging $2,500/mo = **$750K/yr recurring at 70% margin before any project revenue** — and that base is what an acquirer values at a real multiple. Every other page in this wiki ultimately feeds this one.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
The retainer is where you *are* the human-agent management layer for hire: monitoring is the operator work, prompt/SOP updating is the agent-manager work, sampling is the auditor work, and quarterly re-audits are the process-designer work ([[agent-manager-job-design|Agent Manager Job Design]]). The [[progressive-operating-thesis|operating loop]] requires continuous rule updating by design — which is why retainers in this model aren't an upsell; they're structurally necessary, and clients can see why.

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **Underpriced tiers** — a Tier 1 price with Tier 3 consumption, absorbed silently, is the classic margin bleed; track hours per client monthly and re-tier with data.
- **Invisible work** — retainers churn when the client can't see what they're paying for; the monthly report is the retention tool, never skipped.
- **Hour-based framing** — sell responsibilities and outcomes; sold hours get audited and nickel-and-dimed.
