# Three-Year Plan

> Years 2–3: from a solo practice to a firm — team, productized delivery, $1M+ revenue, and the owner out of the delivery critical path.

## Purpose
Define the transformation from "Chris with clients" to "a company with a method," including the hiring sequence, productization work, and the metrics that mark the transition.

**Date anchor:** Under the current vehicle, Years 2–3 would land roughly 2028–2029.
Treat that as a scenario, not North Star doctrine; reconcile it to
`CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`, CASTLE outcomes, graduation reality, and school capacity before
using it.

## Key Idea
The three-year project is **replacing yourself in delivery without losing the quality bar** — converting personal skill into organizational capability. The mechanism is the playbook-first hire: nothing is delegated until it's documented, and everything documented gets delegated. By month 36 the owner's job is architecture, key relationships, sales leadership, and the P&L — not building automations at midnight.

## Why It Matters
A practice that depends on the founder's hands caps out around $200–$300K/yr and is worth almost nothing to anyone else. A firm with team-delivered playbooks, a retainer base, and vertical brand can pass $1M with healthy margins — and is the only vehicle that can reach the [[ten-year-scale-plan|Ten-Year Scale Plan]]. Everything hard about years 2–3 (hiring, letting go of builds, process discipline) is the price of that conversion.

## End-of-Year-3 Scorecard

## Year 2 — Build the Team and Productize

### Hiring sequence (each hire funded by demonstrated margin, each onboarded via playbooks)
1. **Delivery engineer #1** (~month 13–16): takes 60%+ of build work within a quarter, using the [[fulfillment-system|fulfillment playbooks]]; their first 30 days mirror [[first-30-days|First 30 Days]] weeks 1–2
2. **Ops/admin** (part-time → full): scheduling, invoicing, client comms hygiene, retainer report assembly
3. **Delivery engineer #2 or audit consultant** (~month 18–24): whichever is the binding constraint — build capacity or diagnostic capacity

### Productization
- Each pathway becomes a **named, fixed-scope, fixed-price product** with a delivery checklist, margin target, and owner — the [[service-offer-ladder|Service Offer Ladder]] fully specified
- Vertical packages launched where case-study density justifies ("the HVAC ops system") — presold by proof, delivered from templates
- The audit evolves to a two-tier product: standard ($3.5K–$5K, consultant-delivered) and enterprise-lite ($7.5K+, founder-led) — [[smb-ai-audit-method|SMB AI Audit Method]] advanced version

### Year-2 marks
$300K–$600K revenue; founder out of routine builds by month 18; QA review step added so nothing ships unreviewed; first client fully served with zero founder delivery hours.

## Year 3 — Build the Machine
- **Sales leverage:** founder still closes, but pipeline generation is systematized — partner program formalized with tracked economics, outbound run by a part-time SDR/VA to a documented playbook, the vertical talk circuit and case-study library doing inbound work ([[sales-system|Sales System]] advanced version)
- **Delivery leadership:** senior engineer or delivery lead owns quality and capacity planning; founder reviews architecture and exceptions only
- **Client portfolio management:** tiered account coverage; QBRs on all Tier 2–3 retainers; expansion revenue (upsells into existing clients) targeted at 30%+ of project revenue
- **Financial discipline:** monthly P&L review, margin per product line, utilization tracking, 3-month cash buffer, owner compensation normalized (not just "what's left")
- **Second/third vertical entered** using the [[first-90-days|First 90 Days]] new-vertical playbook — only after vertical #1 is clearly dominant

## Failure Modes Specific to This Phase
- **Hiring ahead of playbooks** — the hire flails, quality drops, "I'll just do it myself" regression follows. Document first.
- **Founder as permanent bottleneck** — every decision routed through you means the team is expensive hands, not capability. Push decisions into the playbooks.
- **Growth outrunning the retainer base** — chasing project revenue while recurring share falls rebuilds the treadmill at a bigger scale. Watch the recurring-share metric quarterly.
- **Premature office/overhead/brand spending** — none of it closes SMB deals; margin and proof do.
See [[risks-and-failure-modes|Risks & Failure Modes]] for the full catalog.

## Practical Actions
- Maintain the "hire #1" task list from [[one-year-plan|year 1 Q2]]; convert it into a job description the month revenue supports it.
- Set the delegation rule now and keep it: *documented → delegated → reviewed → released.*
- Add the three phase-transition metrics to the monthly review: recurring share, founder delivery hours, revenue per team member.

## Intermediate Version
The minimum-viable firm: one delivery contractor plus a VA, $400–600K revenue, 50% recurring, founder still closing but no longer building routine work. Internally, the team operates as [[agent-manager-job-design|agent managers]] over agentic delivery tooling ([[claude-code-leverage]]) — one engineer supervising AI-assisted builds across multiple clients is the margin engine that funds hire #2.

## Beginner Version
Not applicable at this horizon — but the minimum-viable version of year 2–3 is: one delivery contractor + one VA, $400K revenue, 50% recurring. That's still a real firm, just a smaller one, and it keeps every option in the [[ten-year-scale-plan|ten-year plan]] open.

## Advanced Version
The upper scorecard plus optionality: by month 36 the firm can credibly choose between (a) continued organic scale, (b) geographic/vertical expansion, (c) productizing IP into software, or (d) early acquisition interest. The [[ten-year-scale-plan|Ten-Year Scale Plan]] evaluates these branches.

## Revenue Connection
The economic event of years 2–3 is **margin transfer**: revenue stops being bought with founder hours and starts being produced by system + team. Revenue per founder-hour rises 5–10×, recurring revenue crosses half the P&L, and the business acquires the two properties acquirers pay multiples for: it grows without the founder, and it keeps its clients.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
Years 2–3 apply the [[progressive-operating-thesis|thesis]] to your own firm: delivery engineers work as agent managers over AI-assisted builds, playbooks are your knowledge base, QA review is your sampling audit, and the productized vertical packages bundle [[human-role-redesign|role redesign]] and training as standard components. The firm sells the operating model it runs on — which is also the most honest sales demo in the portfolio.

## Risks / Failure Modes
The phase-specific catalog is in the body above (hiring ahead of playbooks, founder bottleneck, growth outrunning the retainer base, premature overhead). The meta-risk: **treating delegation as loss of quality control instead of building the [[quality-control-and-risk-gates|gate system]] that maintains it** — the same review-gate engineering you sell clients is how your own team ships safely.
