# Customer Discovery and Evidence

> A conversation method for learning what a prospect actually does, pays for,
> avoids, and will commit to without manufacturing validation.

## Purpose

Protect the Advisor-Builder from false-positive conversations. This method sits
before and inside the [[sales-system|Sales System]] and
[[smb-ai-audit-method|SMB AI Audit Method]]: learn from past behavior, observe the
real workflow, and distinguish polite interest from evidence.

## Key Idea

Do not ask whether an idea, audit, automation, or product sounds good. People
will protect the relationship, speculate about a future they do not control, and
offer feature ideas without paying. Ask about their actual work and the last
time the problem occurred.

Three rules govern the conversation:

1. Ask about the person's life and workflow, not your proposed solution.
2. Ask about specific past events, not opinions or hypothetical futures.
3. Talk less; listen for facts, existing workarounds, cost, and commitment.

## Evidence Ladder

| Signal | What it means | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Compliment or enthusiasm | Social signal, not demand | Ignore and return to facts |
| Feature idea | A clue about a goal or constraint | Ask why it matters and how they handle it now |
| Generic claim | Unanchored belief | Ask for the last concrete example |
| Existing workaround | The problem is real enough to trigger effort | Document cost, frequency, and owner |
| Prior spend or failed attempt | Strong pain and buying-process evidence | Ask what was bought, who approved it, and why it failed |
| Time commitment | Willingness to continue learning | Secure the next meeting, staff access, or observation |
| Reputation commitment | Internal introduction or referral | Treat as stronger than praise |
| Money or contract | Commercial evidence | Record against the relevant strategy assumption |

## Conversation Method

### Prepare the learning goal

- Name the three most important assumptions behind the conversation.
- Include one question whose answer could kill the opportunity.
- Decide what observable evidence would change the next action.
- Keep the conversation about the customer's problem until the evidence is
  clear; do not pitch to relieve uncertainty.

### Ask for actual events

- "Walk me through the last time that happened."
- "Show me how you handled it."
- "What did you try before?"
- "What did that cost in time, money, delay, or customer frustration?"
- "Who else is affected, and who owns the decision to change it?"
- "What happens if nothing changes for six months?"

### Recover from weak data

- **Compliment:** "What are you doing about this today?"
- **Hypothetical:** "When did this last happen?"
- **Feature request:** "What would that let you do that you cannot do now?"
- **Vague pain:** "What is the operational consequence?"
- **Premature pitch:** acknowledge it, stop explaining, and return to the current
  workflow.

### End with advancement

A useful conversation changes the state of the work. Ask for one next proof:
an introduction, a live process observation, sample non-sensitive data, the
decision-maker's participation, a paid diagnostic, or a dated follow-up. "Keep
me posted" and "sounds interesting" are stalls until paired with action.

## Running the Process

- Segment conversations. Mixed feedback from unlike customers creates false
  averages; ten conversations in one narrow workflow beat fifty broad opinions.
- Keep learning notes separate from sales forecasts. Record facts, quotes,
  workarounds, volumes, commitments, and open assumptions.
- Review after every three to five conversations. If the same costly workflow
  and buying trigger recur, deepen the segment; if not, change the assumption,
  not the questions until they produce praise.
- Share notes immediately with anyone helping evaluate the opportunity so one
  charismatic conversation does not become strategy by accident.

## Practical Actions

- Add the evidence ladder to the [[templates/audit-interview-guide|Audit
  Interview Guide]].
- For the first three warm-network workflow conversations, record one specific
  past event, one current workaround, one quantified consequence, and one
  advancement commitment.
- Treat conversation outcomes as evidence for CURRENT_STRATEGY assumptions
  S-01 through S-03, not as external Watchtower signals.

## Beginner Version

Use five questions: last occurrence, current workaround, frequency, cost, and
next proof. Do not explain an AI solution during the learning portion.

## Intermediate Version

Run a repeatable discovery ledger by vertical and workflow. Compare committed
actions and paid diagnostics, not counts of positive conversations.

## Advanced Version

The discovery corpus becomes proprietary market intelligence: repeated workflow
patterns, buying triggers, objections, workarounds, decision structures, and
conversion rates by vertical.

## Revenue Connection

Good discovery prevents unpaid building, improves audit qualification, sharpens
the owner's problem statement, and exposes the exact words that convert outreach.
The first revenue effect is fewer false opportunities; the second is a higher
audit close rate because the offer mirrors a demonstrated problem.

## Human-Agent Management Connection

AI may organize notes, find repeated claims, and prepare follow-up questions.
Chris owns the relationship, interprets context, asks the uncomfortable question,
and decides whether a commitment is real.

## Risks / Failure Modes

- Turning discovery into a disguised pitch and collecting compliments.
- Treating a feature request as a requirement without finding the underlying job.
- Counting willingness to "use" something as willingness to change or pay.
- Recording opinions without the event, frequency, consequence, and owner.
- Letting one large prospect distort the evidence from the target segment.
