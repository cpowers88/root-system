# One-Year Plan

> Year 1: from proof to a real practice — $10K+/month blended revenue, a retainer base, vertical credibility, and delivery that doesn't require heroics.

## Purpose
Define what the end of year 1 looks like and the quarter-by-quarter path there, so the year is spent deepening one working model instead of chasing three new ones.

**Date anchor:** "Year 1" is relative to the first client, not today. The current
strategy targets a first paid consulting conversation for March 2027; review the
actual start in `CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`/CASTLE before using these dates. School remains
the fixed spine, so this plan fits capacity rather than claiming it.

## Key Idea
Year 1 is the **repetition year**. The model was proven in the [[first-90-days|first 90 days]]; the remaining nine months multiply it: more audits, faster delivery through templates, higher prices justified by case studies, and a deliberate push of every client toward the [[retainer-model|retainer]]. The strategic temptation to resist all year: new offers, new markets, new tools. The leverage is in the boring loop, run harder.

## Why It Matters
Year 1 determines which of two businesses exists at month 13: a freelancer with income that stops when they stop, or a practice with recurring revenue, proof assets, and playbooks — the only version that can execute the [[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]].

## End-of-Year-1 Scorecard

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Revenue (year total) | $100,000–$180,000 |
| Recurring monthly revenue (retainers) | $5,000–$10,000/mo |
| Clients served | 12–20 |
| Retainer clients | 6–10 |
| Case studies | 6+ (3+ in primary vertical) |
| Audit → project conversion | 50%+ |
| Project → retainer conversion | 50%+ |
| Referral share of new business | 30%+ |
| Delivery playbooks | Audit + top 3 project types fully documented |

## Quarter by Quarter

### Q1 — Prove (covered by [[first-30-days|First 30 Days]] and [[first-90-days|First 90 Days]])
Loop completed twice, first retainer, first case studies.

### Q2 — Repeat and raise
- Volume: 2 audits + 1–2 projects per month; raise audit price to $2,500–$3,500 on the strength of case studies
- Add the second pathway offer properly (usually [[crm-and-sales-ops-pathway|CRM & sales ops]] or [[document-automation-pathway|document automation]]) sold from audit findings
- Formalize retainer tiers on paper ([[retainer-model|Retainer Model]]); convert earlier project clients retroactively
- Build the monitoring backbone across all client automations before client count makes it painful ([[tool-stack|Tool Stack]])

### Q3 — Deepen the vertical
- Push to 3+ case studies in the primary vertical; start the vertical talk circuit (associations, chambers — [[sales-system|Sales System]])
- Introduce [[data-and-dashboard-pathway|dashboard]] offers to existing clients (expansion revenue at zero CAC)
- First delegation: a VA or contractor for admin/bookkeeping, and possibly a first subcontracted build under your review — the [[fulfillment-system|fulfillment playbook]] gets its first external test
- Kill underperformers: any offer, channel, or client type with poor economics after two quarters of data gets cut

### Q4 — Stabilize and decide
- Target run-rate: $12K–$18K/mo blended; retainers covering baseline personal costs — the "floor" that de-risks year 2
- Quarterly business reviews with top retainer clients (sells next year's projects — [[data-and-dashboard-pathway|Data & Dashboard Pathway]] QBR motion)
- Write the year-2 hiring decision memo with real data: which role first (delivery vs. sales support), funded by which margin
- Annual price revision across the board; clean the books; set year-2 targets against the [[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]]

## Operating Disciplines (All Year)
- Weekly sales minimums never pause — the [[risks-and-failure-modes|feast/famine cycle]] is the year's main killer
- Every project: baseline → measured result → retainer offer → case study, no exceptions ([[fulfillment-system|Fulfillment System]])
- Monthly numbers review: revenue, recurring revenue, pipeline coverage, margin per engagement, hours per delivery type
- Playbook time is scheduled, not leftover: 2–4 hours/week harvesting reusables

## Practical Actions
- Translate the scorecard into monthly milestones now; review monthly, adjust quarterly.
- Pick the *one* new offer allowed per quarter (Q2 second pathway, Q3 dashboards) and refuse the rest.
- Start the "hire #1" file in Q2: every task you do that someone at $25–$40/hr could do goes on the list. That list is the job description.

## Intermediate Version
The scorecard's mid-range plus the quality markers: every delivered system has gates, monitoring, and a named client-side owner; retainer reports show the [[quality-control-and-risk-gates|calibration loop]] working; and at least two clients have staff formally trained into [[agent-manager-job-design|operator roles]] — the delivery depth that makes year-2 pricing increases stick.

## Beginner Version
The scorecard's lower bounds are the beginner track: $100K year, $5K/mo recurring, 12 clients — achievable solo with the templates working. If working part-time until revenue supports the jump, set the full-time trigger explicitly (e.g., "3 consecutive months ≥ $8K").

## Advanced Version
The upper bounds plus early team: a delivery contractor handling 30–50% of build hours by Q4, you shifting toward audits, sales, and architecture — the beginning of the owner/operator split the [[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]] completes.

## Revenue Connection
The year's real product is the **revenue quality shift**: from 100% project income in Q1 to 30–40% recurring by Q4. Same headline number, completely different business — recurring revenue is what compounds, survives slow sales months, and eventually gets valued at a multiple.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
Year 1 is when the [[progressive-operating-thesis|operating loop]] starts running at portfolio scale: by Q4 you're the standing [[agent-manager-job-design|agent manager and quality auditor]] for 6–10 clients' systems, and the exception logs across those clients tell you exactly which patterns to productize in year 2. Your own firm should be running the same loop — AI drafting proposals, reports, and builds; you reviewing and improving.

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **New-offer temptation** — the year's discipline is one new offer per quarter, maximum; the leverage is in the boring loop run harder.
- **Recurring share stalling** — hitting revenue targets on pure project income rebuilds the treadmill; watch the retainer conversion metric monthly.
- **Heroic delivery instead of playbooks** — every undocumented project is a brick *not* laid for the [[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]].
