# Service Offer Ladder

> The offer stack: how a client climbs from a $1.5K audit to a $60K/year systems partnership — and why every rung exists.

## Purpose
Define the exact offers sold at each stage of the client relationship, so pricing, sales, and fulfillment all operate on the same named products instead of improvised scopes.

## Key Idea
Never sell one big thing to a stranger. Sell a **small diagnostic** to a stranger, a **scoped project** to a believer, and a **retainer** to a client with working systems. Each rung de-risks the next for both sides, and each rung's deliverable *is the sales material* for the rung above.

## The Ladder

### Rung 0 — Starter Diagnostic ($500–$1,500, optional entry)
A half-day workflow review producing a short opportunity list — no full task inventory, no quantified report. Use it only when a prospect is warm but audit-hesitant, or for your very first clients; its single job is converting into Rung 1. Don't let it cannibalize the audit: it identifies leaks, the audit prices them.

### Rung 1 — Operational AI Audit ($1,500–$5,000, one-time)
The front door. 1–2 weeks. Process mapping, waste quantification, prioritized automation roadmap with ROI estimates. Full method: [[smb-ai-audit-method|SMB AI Audit Method]].
- **Client risk:** trivial. **Your goal:** demonstrate diagnostic skill and produce a roadmap they want executed.
- Deliverable doubles as the proposal for Rung 2.

> **The current first-offer hypothesis is narrower than this rung.** Per
> `CURRENT_STRATEGY.md`, begin with an observation session and concise written audit;
> the report must earn any implementation or retainer conversation. This ladder is
> an option set for later evidence, not permission to bundle unproven scope.

### Rung 2 — Quick-Win Implementation ($3,000–$10,000, 2–4 weeks)
The top 1–3 items from the audit roadmap — highest ROI, lowest complexity. Usually [[workflow-automation-pathway|workflow automation]] or [[crm-and-sales-ops-pathway|CRM fixes]]. Where processes are too messy to automate directly, this rung is **workflow redesign + SOP buildout** — converting undocumented human work into step-based, documented workflows. That's a legitimate paid deliverable in its own right, and it's the foundation every later automation stands on.
- **Your goal:** a measurable win inside 30 days ("intake now takes 4 minutes, not 40").
- This rung creates the numbers for your [[case-study-template|case study]].

### Rung 3 — Core Systems Project ($10,000–$50,000, 1–3 months)
The substantial rebuild: full CRM/sales ops overhaul, [[document-automation-pathway|document automation]] pipeline, [[data-and-dashboard-pathway|data & dashboard]] layer, or an [[internal-ai-assistants-pathway|internal assistant]] on clean data.
- Sold almost exclusively to clients who completed Rungs 1–2. Cold-selling this rung is how projects blow up.

### Rung 4 — Systems Retainer ($1,000–$8,000/month, ongoing)
Maintenance, monitoring, optimization, small enhancements, quarterly re-audit, priority support. Full model: [[retainer-model|Retainer Model]].
- **This is the business.** Rungs 1–3 exist to earn this rung.

### Rung 5 — Fractional Systems Partner ($5,000–$15,000/month, mature clients)
Advanced tier: you function as the client's outsourced automation/ops-engineering department — roadmap ownership, vendor decisions, new-system builds included. Only for clients with 12+ months of history and multiple systems under management.

## Why It Matters
Without a ladder, every deal is custom: custom scope, custom price, custom risk. That caps close rates, destroys margins, and makes delegation impossible. With a ladder, sales conversations become "which rung are you on?" and fulfillment becomes repeatable — the precondition for the [[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]].

## Ascension Mechanics
- Audit → Quick-Win conversion target: **50–70%** (the audit is designed to convert; see its page).
- Quick-Win → Core Project: **40–60%** within 6 months.
- Any implementation → Retainer: **60%+** (offer it at project handoff, framed as "who keeps this running").
- Price each rung so the client feels the previous rung "paid for itself" — that feeling, not persuasion, drives ascension.

## Practical Actions
- Write a one-page description of Rungs 1, 2, and 4 (skip 3 and 5 until you have clients). Name them, price them, fix their deliverables.
- Build the audit report template so its final section is literally the Rung 2 proposal.
- Add a retainer paragraph to every project handoff document.

## Beginner Version
Sell only Rungs 1 and 2. Your ladder is: paid audit → one quick win → ask for a testimonial → ask for a referral. Introduce a modest retainer ($500–$1,500/mo) with client #3–5 once you've seen what "maintenance" actually involves.

## Intermediate Version
Rungs 1–4 all live with written one-pagers and fixed scopes; ascension conversions tracked monthly against the targets above; every Rung 2–3 delivery includes [[human-role-redesign|role cards]] and [[quality-control-and-risk-gates|gate designs]] so the retainer conversation at handoff is about *who runs the system*, not whether one is needed.

## Advanced Version
All five rungs productized with fixed scopes, margin targets, and owner-of-record on the team. Ladder metrics (conversion per rung, revenue per client per year, time-to-retainer) reviewed monthly. New service ideas must slot into an existing rung or justify a new one.

## Revenue Connection
The ladder converts one $2,500 audit into a client worth $30K–$100K over 24 months. Client lifetime value — not audit price — is what funds serious customer acquisition and what an acquirer eventually pays for. See [[pricing-models|Pricing Models]] for the numbers at each rung.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
Each rung moves the client further up the [[progressive-operating-thesis|operating loop]]: the audit classifies their work ([[human-agent-operating-model|operating model]]), implementations install AI-first production with human gates, and the retainer staffs the [[agent-manager-job-design|system-improvement roles]] they can't staff themselves. The ladder is the thesis, priced.

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **Cold-selling Rung 3+:** unscoped big projects to strangers is how builds blow up — the ladder's whole point is earned ascension.
- **Skipping the retainer ask:** every handoff without the "who keeps this running?" conversation leaks the most valuable rung.
- **Rung 0 becoming the business:** starter diagnostics that don't convert to audits are cheap consulting, not a ladder.
