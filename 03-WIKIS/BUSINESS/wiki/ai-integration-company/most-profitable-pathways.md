# Most Profitable Pathways

> The eight service pathways, ranked head-to-head, and the recommended sequence for entering them.

## Purpose
Compare every viable service line on the criteria that matter — profit, speed, difficulty, defensibility, SMB fit, fit with an engineering/systems background, retainer creation, agent-manager conversion, and risk — so effort goes to the highest-yield pathways in the right order.

## Key Idea
There is no single "best" pathway; there is a **best sequence**. The audit is the universal front door. Workflow automation and CRM/sales ops are the fastest profitable follow-ons. Document automation, dashboards, and internal assistants are higher-ticket expansions sold into existing clients. The retainer is the destination all pathways feed, and industry-specific operating systems are what the retainer base eventually productizes into. See [[service-offer-ladder|Service Offer Ladder]].

### Reading the matrix
- **Audits win on speed, positioning, and safety**, not on absolute profit — they cap at a few thousand dollars each but convert 50–70% into projects worth 5–20× more, and nothing can go operationally wrong delivering a diagnosis.
- **Workflow automation is the workhorse.** Broadest demand, clearest ROI story, fastest delivery reps, best training ground, and the most natural feed into both retainers and operator/agent-manager role creation.
- **CRM/sales ops has the easiest ROI conversation** (revenue recovered beats cost saved) but involves more people/adoption work than pure automation — which is exactly where [[human-role-redesign|role redesign]] earns its keep.
- **Document automation and dashboards are the most defensible builds** — deeper integration, harder to rip out, most engineering-flavored — but need a warm client and real data access, so they're rarely the first sale. Document automation carries the most delivery risk (errors reach the client's books), which is why its [[quality-control-and-risk-gates|gate architecture]] is the sales pitch.
- **Internal assistants rank last as an entry point**: hardest to scope, hardest to guarantee quality, most hype-contaminated — but note their two 5s: near-100% retainer attach and the strongest agent-manager conversion (someone must maintain the knowledge base). Strong as an *expansion* offer in year 1+, weak as a door-opener.
- **Retainers score highest on what builds company value** (profit + defensibility + both human-agent columns) but you can't sell one until you've delivered something worth maintaining.
- **Industry-specific operating systems are the endgame, not the entry**: the highest profit and defensibility on the board, and the slowest possible speed to first revenue. They only exist as the productized residue of many delivered engagements in one vertical — attempting them cold is the [[what-not-to-do|venture-fantasy trap]].

## Recommended Sequence
1. **Months 0–3:** Audits + small workflow automations. Learn to diagnose and deliver. ([[first-30-days|First 30 Days]], [[first-90-days|First 90 Days]])
2. **Months 3–9:** Add CRM/sales ops and document automation projects sold out of audit findings. Convert first clients to retainers.
3. **Months 9–18:** Add dashboards as the reporting layer on systems you've already built. Introduce internal assistants only for retained clients with clean data. ([[one-year-plan|One-Year Plan]])
4. **Year 2+:** Every pathway productized; retainer base is the core P&L. ([[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]])
5. **Year 3+:** Repeated patterns in the dominant vertical get packaged as an industry operating system — audit framework, workflow maps, dashboard kit, assistant templates, role designs — sold as a productized offer. ([[ten-year-scale-plan|Ten-Year Scale Plan]], Route B feeds from here.)

## Why It Matters
Sequencing errors are the most common early failure: leading with the hardest offer (assistants), skipping the audit (unscoped projects), delaying retainers (treadmill economics), or productizing before patterns exist (venture cosplay). The matrix exists to prevent all four. See [[risks-and-failure-modes|Risks & Failure Modes]].

## Practical Actions
- Commit to the sequence above; don't sell pathway 4–6 offers to cold prospects.
- For each audit delivered, tag findings by pathway — this tells you which pathway your market is actually buying.
- Revisit this matrix quarterly with your own data (close rates, margins, delivery hours per pathway).

## Beginner Version
You need exactly two things live: the audit offer and the ability to deliver 2–3 standard workflow automations. Ignore everything else until five clients have paid you.

## Intermediate Version
Three pathways live (audit, automation, CRM/sales ops), each with a scoped offer and at least one case study; document automation and dashboards quoted only from audit findings into warm clients; first retainers running. Your own close-rate and margin data starts overriding the default scores above.

## Advanced Version
Each pathway has a named productized offer, a delivery playbook, reference case studies, and known unit economics. New pathways (voice AI, agentic ops, vertical products) are evaluated against this same matrix before launch.

## Revenue Connection
The matrix is the revenue model in miniature: audits create pipeline, automation and CRM work create project revenue, documents/dashboards/assistants create expansion revenue, retainers convert all of it into the recurring base that makes the company valuable, and the industry operating system converts the recurring base into IP-grade enterprise value.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
The two new columns are strategy, not decoration: pathways that create [[agent-manager-job-design|agent-manager roles]] on the client side (assistants, retainers, industry systems) are the stickiest, because the client's own staff now work *inside* the system you engineered. Every pathway page's Human-Agent Management Connection section explains its column score.

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **Ranking without your own data:** the default scores are starting assumptions; two quarters of actuals beat them. Update quarterly or the matrix becomes decoration.
- **Sequence-jumping under revenue pressure:** a cold prospect waving a big assistant budget is how the worst quarters start — see [[internal-ai-assistants-pathway|the assistant pathway's]] prerequisites.
- **Spreading across all eight early:** each live pathway costs learning, templates, and tooling; before year 2, three is the maximum that compounds.
