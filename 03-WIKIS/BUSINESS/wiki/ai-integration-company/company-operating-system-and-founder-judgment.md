# Company Operating System and Founder Judgment

> Build the minimum management system that makes responsibility, decisions,
> knowledge, metrics, and difficult tradeoffs visible without creating bureaucracy
> before the company earns it.

## Purpose

Provide an operating reference for the point when Chris has real clients,
repeatable delivery, and people to coordinate. This is not a current instruction
to hire, fundraise, or install corporate process.

## Key Idea

Early companies need focus and direct communication; growing companies need
explicit responsibility and repeatable management. Add structure when a real
coordination failure appears, not because a mature-company book lists a practice.

## Minimum Operating System

### Focus and commitments

- Name one top company goal and expose the vital few activities that drive it.
- Treat 80/20 as a measurement hypothesis, not a universal ratio: analyze profit,
  client value, defects, delays, and support load by customer, offer, and workflow.
- Every commitment has an owner and date. Repeated instructions become a written
  process; repeated exceptions become an improvement queue.

### Decisions

- Match the method to consequence and reversibility: owner decision, consultation,
  consensus, or delegated decision with a clear decider.
- Use a written issue statement, evidence, alternatives, recommendation,
  confidence, dissent, decision owner, and next action for important choices.
- Collect independent views before the highest-status person speaks.
- After debate, record the decision and execute; reopen only on new evidence or a
  named review trigger.

### Responsibility and resilience

- Maintain areas of responsibility with a primary and backup owner.
- Remove single points of failure through documentation, access control, training,
  and practiced handoffs.
- Separate goals, projects, recurring responsibilities, and reference knowledge so
  no task disappears inside a wiki or inbox.

### Metrics and meetings

- Use a small KPI set with countermetrics. Speed without quality, sales without
  margin, or automation without adoption creates false success.
- Meetings exist for decisions, conflict, learning, and coordination, not status
  recitation that a written update could replace.
- Review actual commitments and exceptions; end with owners and dates.

### Hard leadership

- Communicate bad news early and accurately; hiding reality prevents coordinated
  response.
- Take care of people, products, and profits as a system, while acknowledging that
  survival decisions can still be painful.
- Use clear role expectations, feedback, and performance decisions. Delaying a
  necessary decision transfers the cost to the whole team.
- In crisis, narrow the objective, increase decision cadence, and preserve enough
  dissent to avoid confident error.

## Judgment Failure Checklist

Before an exciting hire, client, partnership, investment, or product decision,
check for interacting biases:

- incentives and self-interest;
- liking, disliking, and relationship pressure;
- commitment/consistency and sunk cost;
- social proof and authority;
- scarcity or fear of missing out;
- vivid anecdotes overwhelming base rates;
- stress, fatigue, and urgency;
- reciprocation and obligation;
- confirmation seeking and avoidance of disconfirming evidence.

Countermeasures: write the decision before advocacy, state base rates, ask what
would disprove it, obtain independent estimates, run a pre-mortem, assign a
challenger, and conduct a post-mortem against the original reasoning. Biases
combine; the dangerous state is rarely one tendency acting alone.

## Practical Actions

- Do not activate this entire page now. Add one operating practice only when a
  repeated coordination failure or phase transition justifies it.
- At the first delivery hire, define responsibilities, backup coverage, written
  playbooks, decision rights, and quality gates before expanding workload.
- Add the judgment checklist to the bad-fit-client and major-opportunity gate.

## Beginner Version

One top goal, one weekly scorecard, written commitments, and a short decision log.
No elaborate meeting or management system.

## Intermediate Version

Areas of responsibility, backup owners, documented delivery playbooks, KPI
countermetrics, structured one-on-ones, and decision/post-mortem discipline.

## Advanced Version

A leadership team runs the system; Chris focuses on strategy, critical
relationships, capital allocation, culture, and exceptions rather than routine
coordination.

## Revenue Connection

The operating system protects margin, delivery quality, client retention, and
founder capacity. Focus analysis identifies the clients, offers, and workflows
that actually create profit; resilience prevents one absence or departure from
breaking delivery.

## Human-Agent Management Connection

Agents can prepare written briefs, status summaries, KPI exceptions, and decision
records. Humans own commitments, personnel decisions, conflict, ethical judgment,
and accountability. The same gates sold to clients should govern the firm.

## Risks / Failure Modes

- Installing mature-company process before product/market and offer evidence.
- Treating an 80/20 pattern as a law without measuring the actual distribution.
- Founder authority silencing independent judgment.
- Documentation without ownership or use.
- Metrics without countermetrics.
- Heroic crisis behavior becoming the normal operating model.
