# Outreach Scripts

Scripts for the [[sales-system|Sales System]] channels. Rules that apply to all of them: name **their process**, never your technology; one clear, small ask; personalize the first line or don't send; track every send and reply in your CRM.

---

## 1. Warm Network (Channel 1 — start here)

### The soft ask (text/DM/email to people you know)
> Hey [Name] — quick one. I've started a business helping [vertical/local] companies find and fix the manual work that quietly costs them $50–200K a year (think: re-typing quotes, leads nobody calls back, invoice chasing). I'm looking for owners drowning in that kind of admin. Who's the first person that comes to mind?

*Ask "who comes to mind," not "do you know anyone" — it produces names instead of "I'll think about it."*

### The direct version (someone who owns/runs a business)
> [Name] — you run [company], so you'll know instantly whether this is relevant: I diagnose where businesses lose money to manual work — I run a 2-week audit that puts a dollar figure on every leak and a prioritized fix list. Even if you never hire me to fix anything, you'd know exactly what your admin chaos costs. Worth 15 minutes this week?

### Referral request (after every delivered project — verbatim, at handoff)
> Before we wrap — you've seen how this works now. Who are two other [vertical] owners you know who are drowning in the same [quotes/scheduling/paperwork] mess you had? A direct intro from you means they'll actually take the call.

## 2. Cold Email (Channel 4 — 10–15/day researched, never blasted)

### Sequence structure: 4 touches over ~3 weeks. Personalized first line every time.

**Email 1 — the process problem**
> Subject: [Company]'s quote turnaround
>
> [Name] — [one specific, researched line: "saw you're hiring a second office admin" / "noticed quotes go through your info@ inbox"].
>
> Most [vertical] companies take 2+ days to get quotes out; the ones responding within 2 hours win a disproportionate share of jobs. Usually the delay isn't people — it's the manual assembly behind every quote.
>
> I run a short paid audit that maps exactly where your [quote/intake/invoicing] process loses time and money, priced in dollars. Two weeks, and you keep the findings either way.
>
> Worth a 15-minute call to see if there's anything there?

**Email 2 (+3 days) — the proof nudge**
> One example of what this looks like: [one-sentence case study result — "a 35-person HVAC contractor cut quote turnaround from 2 days to 2 hours and closed 22% more jobs"]. Full write-up attached. Relevant to [Company]?

**Email 3 (+7 days) — the specific observation**
> [Name] — I did 10 minutes of homework: [specific, verifiable observation — "I submitted a quote request through your site Tuesday morning; no response yet as of Thursday"]. That gap is usually worth $[X]K/yr in lost jobs. Happy to show you the math on a short call.

*(Email 3's test-inquiry move is the highest-converting message in the sequence — use it whenever the client has a public intake channel.)*

**Email 4 (+7 days) — the clean close**
> Last note from me — timing may just be wrong. If admin chaos ever hits the "we can't keep doing this" point, my audit is the fastest way to see the whole picture in dollars. I'll leave you with [case study link] in the meantime. Door's open.

## 3. Referral Partners (Channel 3 — bookkeepers, MSPs, agencies, fractional CFOs)

### First approach
> [Name] — you see inside [N] local businesses' books, which means you see the manual chaos I fix: double entry, invoice lag, systems that don't talk. I run operational audits and build the automation to fix what they show — work your clients need and you probably don't want to do.
>
> I'd like 20 minutes to see if a referral relationship makes sense. It makes you the person who solved a problem outside your lane — and I pay [10–15%] of first-project value on referred work / refer bookkeeping work back, whichever you prefer.

### The maintenance loop (monthly, keep 5 partners warm)
> Quick monthly note: just wrapped [anonymized result — "a distributor now saving ~14 hrs/wk on order entry"]. If any client mentions [drowning in paperwork / missed leads / late invoicing], that's my exact lane. How's your quarter going?

---

## 4. LinkedIn (support for cold email, same rules)
Connection note (no pitch):
> [Name] — I work with [vertical] companies on operations/automation and try to know the serious operators in [region]. No pitch, just building the network.

Then engage genuinely with their posts before any DM. The DM, when earned, is Email 1 compressed to three sentences.

---

## Tracking Discipline
Every touch logged: date, channel, message variant, reply Y/N. Review monthly: which channel and which variant books conversations. Kill the losers, double the winners — this is the [[financial-model|funnel math]] in practice.
