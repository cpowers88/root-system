# Ten-Year Scale Plan

> Years 4–10: compounding a $1M firm into an asset worth tens of millions — the realistic routes, the math behind each, and what to build toward regardless of route.

## Purpose
Describe how the firm built in the [[three-year-plan|Three-Year Plan]] compounds into serious enterprise value, without venture-scale fantasy: every route here is an extension of the same diagnose–build–maintain engine, funded by its own margins.

**Date anchor:** "Years 4–10" run roughly 2030–2036 per NORTH_STAR.md — past graduation (Dec 2029) and past the October 8, 2031 North Star date itself. The $500K–$1M revenue floor is targeted *by* Oct 2031, inside this page's Years 4–6 band; everything past that point is the ratchet already having turned at least once.

## Key Idea
Enterprise value in this business comes from four properties, in rough order of weight: **contracted recurring revenue, growth without the founder, client retention, and proprietary method/IP**. The ten-year plan is simply maximizing those four properties at increasing scale. There are three realistic routes — services scale-up, platform hybrid, and roll-up/acquisition — and they share the same foundation, so the route decision can be made late (year 5–6) with real data.

## The Math That Makes "Tens of Millions" Honest
Value ≈ EBITDA × multiple. Services firms trade at ~4–8× EBITDA; recurring-heavy tech-enabled services at 8–12×; software at higher still. Working backward:

| Scenario | Revenue | EBITDA (25–30%) | Multiple | Enterprise value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong services firm | $8M | $2.2M | 6× | ~$13M |
| Recurring-heavy hybrid | $12M | $3.5M | 9× | ~$30M+ |
| Services + software product | $15M | $4.5M | 10×+ | ~$45M+ |

So the target is concrete: **$8–15M revenue with 50%+ recurring and 25%+ EBITDA by year 8–10.** Aggressive but arithmetic — roughly 150–400 retained clients at $2.5–4K/mo average, or fewer at higher tiers, plus project revenue. No unicorn required.

## Years 4–6 — Regional/Vertical Dominance ($1.5M → $4M)
- Scale the proven engine: 3–4 verticals, each with a dense [[case-study-template|case-study]] moat and a dedicated vertical playbook
- Management layer: delivery lead, sales lead, ops lead — founder moves to CEO work (strategy, key accounts, hiring, capital allocation)
- Client size creeps upward: the $50M-revenue client with a $10K/mo [[retainer-model|Partner-tier retainer]] becomes normal; the method scales up-market before the brand does
- **IP hardening:** internal tooling (monitoring platform, template libraries, audit software) treated as products-in-waiting — built once, deployed to every client
- Financial: maintain 25%+ EBITDA while growing; fund everything from margin; establish clean books and legal hygiene (an eventual buyer's diligence starts being cheap now)

## Years 6–8 — Choose the Route ($4M → $8M+)
Evaluate with real data; the routes are not mutually exclusive:

### Route A — Services scale-up
More geographies/verticals, possibly small acqui-hires of local automation shops. Simplest; value ceiling ~6–8× EBITDA. Right if delivery leadership is the firm's standout strength.

### Route B — Platform hybrid (usually the best value route)
The internal IP becomes client-facing software: the monitoring/reporting platform every retainer client already depends on becomes a licensed product; vertical automation packages become configurable products with an implementation fee. Services revenue seeds software revenue with zero CAC. Shifts the multiple from 6× toward 10×+.

### Route C — Consolidator
Use cash flow + modest debt to acquire small MSPs/automation shops and convert their clients to the retainer model — buying at 3–4× and operating into a firm valued at 8×+ is the arbitrage. Right only if year-4–6 proved strong management-layer and integration capability.

## Years 8–10 — Harvest Position
Whatever the route, engineer the same end-state properties:
- **Founder-optional:** management team runs operations; founder could exit day-to-day with <10% revenue impact
- **Contracted and diversified:** 50%+ recurring, no client >5% of revenue, multi-year agreements on top tiers
- **Documented everywhere:** playbooks, financials, contracts, security posture — permanently diligence-ready
Then choose from strength: sell (strategic or PE buyer), majority recap (partial liquidity, keep upside), or hold a compounding cash machine. **Optionality is the goal, not exit for its own sake.**

## What Stays True the Whole Decade
- The [[north-star-alignment|North Star]] doesn't change: diagnose waste, engineer the fix, stay accountable. AI toolsets will turn over 4–5 times in ten years; each turnover is a re-audit and upgrade cycle sold to the installed base — technology churn is a *revenue feature* of the retainer model.
- The [[north-star-alignment|alignment filter]] still gates every initiative, including acquisitions and products.
- Proof still compounds: by year 10 the case-study library and aggregate results data are themselves a moat no entrant can shortcut.

## Practical Actions (Relevant From Year 3 Onward)
- Track the four value properties (recurring share, founder-dependence, retention/NRR, IP assets) as a quarterly "enterprise value dashboard."
- Build all internal tooling as if it might ship to clients someday — clean, documented, multi-tenant-ish.
- Get an annual informal valuation conversation (broker or M&A advisor) from ~$3M revenue onward; it calibrates decisions and builds the relationships early.

## Intermediate Version
From year 3 onward, this page becomes quarterly reading: the enterprise-value dashboard (recurring share, founder-dependence, NRR, IP assets) gets reviewed with real numbers, internal tooling gets built multi-tenant-ish, and the vertical operating systems — audit frameworks, workflow maps, dashboard kits, [[agent-manager-job-design|role designs]] packaged per industry — start accumulating as the productizable IP that Route B eventually ships.

## Beginner Version
Ignore this page operationally until year 3 — but let it settle one thing now: **the retainer model, playbook discipline, and vertical focus you adopt in month 1 are the same decisions that make this page possible in year 10.** Nothing here requires a different company; it requires the same company, compounded.

## Advanced Version
This entire page is the advanced version. The year-6 route decision memo — Routes A/B/C evaluated against actual margins, NRR, IP maturity, and management depth — is the single most consequential document of the decade.

## Revenue Connection
This page is where revenue becomes wealth: operating income along the way, plus a terminal asset worth 6–12× EBITDA. The difference between the $13M and $45M scenarios is decided by three numbers this wiki has been optimizing since page one — recurring share, retention, and margin.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
At scale, the [[progressive-operating-thesis|thesis]] is the moat itself: a decade of exception logs, gate calibrations, role designs, and vertical task inventories is proprietary knowledge about *how work actually gets redesigned* that no entrant can shortcut. Each AI toolset turnover (expect 4–5 in ten years) re-monetizes it — the installed base needs re-auditing, re-gating, and re-training every cycle, purchased from the firm that already manages their operating loop.

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **Route decision made on ambition instead of data** — the year-6 memo needs real margins, NRR, and management-depth evidence, not preference.
- **IP that only exists in people** — undocumented method walks out with employees; the playbook discipline is what makes the multiple real.
- **Chasing the software multiple prematurely** — Route B before repeated service patterns prove demand is the venture fantasy this wiki exists to prevent ([[what-not-to-do|What NOT To Do]]).
