# Strategic Diagnosis and Coherent Action

> A strategy is a diagnosis of the decisive challenge, a guiding policy for
> dealing with it, and coordinated actions that concentrate effort.

## Purpose

Keep the business and client recommendations from becoming lists of goals,
projects, or slogans. Use a compact strategy kernel whenever choices compete
for limited time, money, or attention.

## Key Idea

Good strategy identifies what matters most and commits resources to it. Bad
strategy avoids the hard diagnosis, mistakes ambition for method, uses vague
language, or labels every desirable outcome a priority.

## The Strategy Kernel

1. **Diagnosis:** What is the underlying structure of the challenge? Name the
   bottleneck, conflict, change, or advantage that makes this situation hard.
2. **Guiding policy:** What approach constrains and directs the response? It
   should rule out attractive alternatives, not merely describe success.
3. **Coherent actions:** What mutually reinforcing actions apply the policy now?
   Actions that pull in different directions reveal that the policy is not real.

For the current Advisor-Builder strategy, the first-offer kernel is:

- **Diagnosis:** owners feel operational pain but cannot see or price the real
  workflow leakage, and they do not trust unscoped AI implementation.
- **Guiding policy:** earn trust through observation and a paid, quantified
  diagnosis before recommending technology.
- **Coherent actions:** practice observation, run evidence-safe discovery,
  produce a concise audit report, attach dollars and gates to findings, and let
  delivered proof earn implementation.

## Sources of Strategic Power

- **Leverage:** concentrate strength where a small action changes a large outcome.
- **Proximate objective:** choose a difficult but achievable next target that
  makes later options possible.
- **Chain-link integrity:** strengthen the weakest critical link; improving other
  links cannot compensate for it.
- **Design:** coordinate policy, resources, timing, and actions as one system.
- **Focus:** serve a narrower need better rather than spreading capability thin.
- **Advantage:** deepen an asymmetry others cannot cheaply copy.
- **Dynamics:** exploit a change already in motion instead of forecasting every
  detail.
- **Inertia and entropy:** expect organizations and processes to resist change
  and decay without maintenance.

## Practical Actions

- Add a three-line kernel to every major offer, initiative, and audit roadmap.
- In a client audit, distinguish the symptom list from the decisive constraint;
  recommend a proximate objective that improves the critical chain.
- When a plan contains more than three priorities, ask which one makes the others
  easier or unnecessary.
- Test the kernel against evidence: what observation would change the diagnosis,
  policy, or action sequence?

## Beginner Version

Write one diagnosis, one policy, and no more than three coherent actions. If the
policy does not exclude anything, it is probably a goal rather than a strategy.

## Intermediate Version

Maintain kernels at the business, offer, and client-engagement levels. Review
them when field evidence changes the challenge, not whenever a new idea appears.

## Advanced Version

Use strategy kernels to allocate teams and capital across a portfolio while
preserving chain-link dependencies and explicit stop rules.

## Revenue Connection

Focus protects scarce selling and delivery hours. For clients, a sharp diagnosis
creates a more credible audit, a smaller first implementation, faster payback,
and a natural roadmap for later work.

## Human-Agent Management Connection

AI can organize evidence, compare alternatives, and test coherence. Humans own
the diagnosis, tradeoffs, guiding policy, and accountability for consequences.

## Risks / Failure Modes

- Goals presented as strategy without a causal diagnosis.
- Refusing to choose and calling every initiative a priority.
- Confusing growth with advantage.
- Copying a competitor's actions without the conditions that make them coherent.
- Keeping a diagnosis after evidence has changed the problem.
