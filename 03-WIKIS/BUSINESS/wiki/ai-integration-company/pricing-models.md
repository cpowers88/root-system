# Pricing Models

> How to price audits, projects, and retainers: value-anchored, fixed-fee, and never by the hour.

## Purpose
Set the pricing rules for every offer so quotes are fast, consistent, defensible, and profitable — and so pricing decisions never happen improvisationally on a sales call.

## Key Idea
**Price against the quantified problem, not against your hours.** The [[smb-ai-audit-method|audit]] exists partly to produce the anchor: once a problem is documented as costing $60K/yr, a $15K fix is self-evidently cheap — regardless of whether it takes you 30 hours or 100. Hourly billing caps income at your calendar, punishes you for getting faster, and reframes you as labor instead of an engineer of outcomes.

## The Three Pricing Structures

### 1. Fixed-fee diagnostics (starter diagnostic + audits)
Flat price, fixed scope, fixed timeline. The optional **starter diagnostic** ($500–$1,500, half-day review + opportunity list) exists purely as a low-friction entry for audit-hesitant prospects and first clients — include the walkthrough and prioritized list, exclude quantified waste math and process maps (those are the audit's job, and giving them away kills the upgrade). The **full audit**: $1,500–$2,500 solo/early → $3,500–$7,500 established. Priced low enough to be an easy yes, high enough to filter non-buyers and pay for your time. See [[smb-ai-audit-method|SMB AI Audit Method]]. The [[cash-flow-audit-method|Cash Flow Audit]] (Profit First) is a second diagnostic offering at similar price bands — sellable standalone or paired with the operational audit.

### 2. Value-anchored fixed-fee projects
Rule of thumb: **price the build at 25–50% of the documented first-year benefit**, floored by your cost (estimated hours × your internal loaded rate × 2.5–3× margin multiplier). Take the higher of the two; if the value anchor can't clear your floor, the project is too small — bundle it or decline.

| Offer | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Quick-win automation (single workflow) | $2,000–$5,000 |
| Speed-to-lead / follow-up system | $3,000–$7,000 |
| Multi-workflow automation package | $8,000–$20,000 |
| CRM implementation + sales ops | $8,000–$25,000 |
| Document pipeline (single doc type) | $5,000–$15,000 |
| Owner KPI dashboard | $5,000–$12,000 |
| Full BI layer / multi-document platform | $15,000–$40,000 |
| Internal assistant (scoped) | $8,000–$20,000 |

Payment terms: **50% to start, 50% at delivery** (or 40/30/30 with a milestone above ~$15K). Never 100% on completion.

### 3. Recurring retainers
Tiered flat monthly fees ($500–$8,000/mo across the core tiers, $5,000–$15,000/mo at the Strategic Operations Partner tier for larger SMBs — full detail in [[retainer-model|Retainer Model]]), plus optional volume-based components where your cost scales with usage (per-document processing, per-seat assistant access). Include named responsibilities (monitoring, gate calibration, prompt/SOP updates, reporting); exclude unlimited support and new builds beyond the defined size — undefined inclusion is how retainers become all-you-can-eat contracts. Annual 3–5% escalator, 30-day terms, in writing.

## Market Check (2026 Data)
Independent 2026 market research validates these ranges — useful both for confidence and for client conversations when a prospect claims "that's above market":
- Standard AI workflow automation builds: **$2,500–$15,000+** per project, with moderately complex operational agents at **$10,000–$50,000** ([Taskip](https://taskip.net/ai-automation-agency-pricing/), [Digital Agency Network](https://digitalagencynetwork.com/ai-agency-pricing/))
- Ongoing monitoring/management retainers: **$500–$5,000+/mo**; median SMB-to-mid-market retainer **$2,800–$7,000/mo**; small businesses automating 2–3 workflows budget **$1,000–$3,500/mo** ([Monetizebot](https://monetizebot.ai/blogs/ai-automation-agency-pricing-2026), [CueBytes](https://cuebytes.com/blog/ai-automation-agency-cost))
- Specific service benchmarks: lead-qualification workflow setup **$2,500–$6,000** + **$800–$2,000/mo** management; email automation **$1,500–$4,000** setup + **$500–$1,000/mo**

Two implications: our rate card sits comfortably inside market range (room to move *up* as proof accumulates), and prices for basic builds fell ~35% from 2024–2026 as tooling commoditized — which is why the [[smb-ai-audit-method|audit]], [[retainer-model|retainer]], and reliability engineering (not the build itself) carry the margin. See [[claude-code-leverage]] for how agentic delivery keeps costs falling faster than prices.

## Pricing Rules (Non-Negotiable)
1. **Never bill hourly for delivery work.** The only acceptable hourly is a short paid discovery when scope is genuinely unknowable.
2. **Never quote on the call.** "I'll have a proposal to you in 48 hours" — pricing under social pressure is how margins die.
3. **Quote from the rate card, adjust for risk** (messy data, many stakeholders, legacy systems = up, never down).
4. **Discount scope, not price.** If the budget is $5K and the quote is $8K, remove a workflow — don't cut the number. Price integrity is reputation.
5. **Third-party costs pass through** (software subscriptions, API usage) — itemized, client-paid, never absorbed.
6. **Raise prices every 3–5 clients** in the first two years. If nobody ever balks, you're underpriced.

## Why It Matters
Underpricing is the most common self-inflicted wound in this business: it attracts the worst clients, makes retainers feel expensive by comparison, and starves the business of the margin that funds skill-building and hiring. The failure mode isn't "too expensive" — it's *too cheap to survive delivering well*. See [[risks-and-failure-modes|Risks & Failure Modes]].

## Practical Actions
- Build your rate card from the table above; put it in your proposal template.
- After every project, log actual hours vs. price — this data is how quotes get accurate.
- Script the value-anchor sentence for proposals: "This process currently costs you ~$X/yr; this build is $Y, paying for itself in Z months."

## Beginner Version
Start at the bottom of each range — but *at* the ranges, not below them. A $2,000 first automation project is legitimate; a $500 one attracts clients who treat you accordingly. Your first 2–3 clients may get "founding client" pricing in exchange for a written case-study/testimonial agreement.

## Intermediate Version
The rate card is real and used: quotes come from it in under an hour, actual-hours-vs-price data exists for every engagement type, audit pricing has climbed to $2,500–$5,000 on case-study strength, and every proposal carries the value-anchor sentence plus the [[quality-control-and-risk-gates|gate diagram]] that justifies premium-over-freelancer pricing.

## Advanced Version
Published rate card, ROI-modeled proposals generated from audit data, risk-adjusted pricing multipliers, retainer escalators enforced, and margin per engagement tracked in your own dashboard. Pricing reviewed quarterly against close rate (target 50–70% on warm proposals — much higher means raise prices).

## Revenue Connection
Pricing is the profit lever with no delivery cost: moving a $10K project to $13K is pure margin. Combined with the [[service-offer-ladder|Service Offer Ladder]], correct pricing turns an average client into $30K–$100K of lifetime value — the number the whole growth model runs on.

## Human-Agent Management Connection
The human-agent design work is *priced work*: role redesign and staff training are line items in implementations, gate calibration and prompt/SOP updates are retainer deliverables, and the [[agent-manager-job-design|staffed roles]] inside retainers (you as the client's agent manager and quality auditor) are what justify the tier prices over commodity "maintenance." Price the redesign, not just the build.

## Risks / Failure Modes
- **Chronic underpricing** — the most common self-inflicted wound; the rate card and the raise-every-3–5-clients rule are the countermeasures.
- **Quoting on the call** — pricing under social pressure; 48-hour proposals, always.
- **Scope-creep by generosity** — every unpriced favor reprices the relationship; written change requests even at $0.
