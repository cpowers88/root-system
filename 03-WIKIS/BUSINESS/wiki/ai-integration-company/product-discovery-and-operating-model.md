# Product Discovery and Operating Model

> Productize only after real use proves a repeated problem. Empower a small team
> to solve for outcomes while testing value, usability, feasibility, and business
> viability before scaling delivery.

## Purpose

Govern the standalone-application and productized-IP path without letting product
building outrun client evidence. This page activates after repeated audit or
delivery patterns exist; it does not authorize a speculative build.

## Key Idea

Feature delivery is not product success. A product team owns a measurable problem
and discovers a solution across four risks:

1. **Value:** Will customers choose or use it?
2. **Usability:** Can the intended users understand and operate it?
3. **Feasibility:** Can it be built reliably with available technology, data,
   skills, time, and constraints?
4. **Business viability:** Can the business sell, support, secure, fund, govern,
   and profit from it?

Discovery reduces these risks cheaply; delivery builds, tests, releases, and
operates the proven solution. Both continue together.

## Productization Gate

Do not open a product build until all six are true:

- The same costly problem has appeared in multiple real engagements or repeated
  internal use.
- At least one user has adopted a manual or service-assisted version.
- The target user and buyer are explicit, including who controls access and money.
- A narrow outcome and baseline exist.
- The four risks have bounded tests, including a no-build or concierge option.
- Product work does not displace fixed school commitments or the current proof
  step without an explicit CASTLE decision.

## Operating Model

- **Outcome over roadmap:** leaders define the customer/business problem and
  constraints; the team discovers the solution.
- **Empowered cross-functional team:** product/value and viability judgment,
  design/usability judgment, and technical/feasibility judgment work together.
- **Continuous discovery:** interviews, observation, prototypes, demand tests,
  concierge tests, and live-data tests retire the largest uncertainty first.
- **Continuous delivery:** small releases, instrumentation, monitoring, and rapid
  rollback keep evidence connected to production.
- **Strategy as focus:** product strategy selects the few problems that matter;
  it is not a feature list.
- **Leadership as coaching and context:** leaders supply vision, constraints,
  talent, and accountability rather than prescribing every output.

## Disruption and Organizational Fit

Strong incumbent practices can reject a real emerging opportunity because current
customers, margins, planning systems, and resource-allocation rules correctly favor
the existing business. Treat disruption as a specific pattern, not a label for any
new technology:

- the early product is worse on attributes mainstream customers value;
- it is better on a different dimension such as simplicity, accessibility, or cost;
- the first market is small or unattractive to the incumbent;
- performance improves until the new value network can serve mainstream needs.

When this pattern is supported by evidence, use a bounded team, economics, and
customer set suited to the emerging market. Do not force the opportunity through a
delivery system whose success metrics require mature-market margins and volume.
Discovery plans must be plans for learning; forecasts for nonexistent markets are
assumptions, not facts.

## Product Management Capability

The reusable PM capabilities are customer and data insight, analytical problem
solving, product/design/technical literacy, written decisions, scoping, launches,
strategy, prioritization, goals, influence, communication, and organizational
learning. For this business, they are activated only when a repeated service pattern
passes the productization gate. Career ladders, job-search tactics, and interview
preparation are not part of the current company method.

## Transformation Method

1. Define the outcome the current operating model cannot reliably produce.
2. Assess competencies, team structure, discovery, delivery, strategy, culture,
   stakeholder relationships, and leadership support.
3. Establish one credible pilot team with a real problem and access to users.
4. Coach the four-risk method; do not merely rename project roles.
5. Demonstrate an outcome, publicize the evidence, and expand only after the
   operating behavior is real.
6. Expect objections from sales, finance, HR, operations, executives, and
   customers; resolve them through shared outcomes and working evidence.

## Practical Actions

- Apply this gate to every proposed standalone app or reusable client system.
- Use [[customer-discovery-and-evidence|Customer Discovery and Evidence]] before
  writing a roadmap.
- Prototype the riskiest assumption first; do not begin with the easiest feature.
- Keep services as a learning channel until product economics and adoption are
  independently proven.

## Beginner Version

No standalone product. Use manual, concierge, spreadsheet, or service-assisted
tests inside a real workflow.

## Intermediate Version

One proven repeated pattern becomes a narrow productized service or internal tool
with named users, instrumentation, gates, and support ownership.

## Advanced Version

Dedicated product teams own outcomes across a vertical platform while the service
business supplies evidence, distribution, and high-value exceptions.

## Revenue Connection

The model prevents speculative software spend while preserving the highest-leverage
asset path. Productization becomes a margin and scale move after services prove
demand, not a substitute for customer acquisition.

## Human-Agent Management Connection

AI accelerates research synthesis, prototypes, code, tests, analysis, and support.
Humans own product judgment, customer relationships, priority, design tradeoffs,
release accountability, and exception handling.

## Risks / Failure Modes

- Roadmap theater: shipping features without an outcome.
- Product-manager-as-project-manager: coordination without value/viability ownership.
- Transformation by title change without competencies or coaching.
- Scaling a pilot before adoption and operating responsibility are proven.
- Using product work to avoid client discovery and sales.
