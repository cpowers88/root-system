## Research Strategy

### Phase 1 — Establish the live system

Read the target design completely.

Then establish the controlling architecture from the minimum live authority chain, including:

- North Star direction.
- Capability and Information Flow Contracts.
- CASTLE’s operating contract and current state.
- File-placement and metadata authority.
- AIAS’s research/self-evolution contract.
- Shared wiki intake rules.
- Current system flags and relevant prior proposals.
- Existing validation, lint, coverage, and health mechanisms.

Do not rely on remembered maps when live files can verify the claim.

### Phase 2 — Build a source-candidate register

Search all non-private `.ROOT` realms for material that could materially affect the design.

Start with indexes, coverage ledgers, source maps, logs, proposal registers, and filenames before opening large raw sources.

Classify every candidate:

- Directly relevant—must inspect.
- Supporting—inspect if it closes a named design question.
- Challenge source—could falsify or materially qualify the current plan.
- Already represented—use the durable wiki extraction instead of rereading raw.
- Lookup only—not activated for this investigation.
- Out of scope—record why.

AIAS is the probable primary evidence hub, but do not assume it contains every relevant source. Specifically test SYSTEMS, TECHNOLOGY, BUSINESS, EDUCATION, REVENUE_LAB, CASTLE, North Star contracts, active projects, and sanitized field evidence for distinct contributions.

Do not inventory `88-JOURNAL`.

### Phase 3 — Parse activated sources

For every activated source:

1. State the exact design question it can answer.
2. Identify source type, date, authority, limitations, and likely volatility.
3. Use existing compiled wiki knowledge when coverage is adequate.
4. For uncompiled books or long sources, read in complete 10–15-page, chapter, or named-section chunks.
5. Record every chunk or section covered.
6. Extract claims with precise source locations.
7. Separate transferable principles from vendor-, era-, or context-specific advice.
8. Record contradictions, missing evidence, and negative findings.
9. Do not manufacture relevance to `.ROOT`, Chris’s education, or profit.
10. Update no live wiki or ledger during this report-only run; instead produce an exact proposed return list for each owner.

### Phase 4 — Evaluate the design through eight lenses

Assess `vault-skeleton-design.md` through:

1. Information acquisition and provenance.
2. Parsing, chunking, classification, synthesis, and retrieval.
3. Education, explain-back, practice, transfer, and mastery evidence.
4. Workflow and systems analysis.
5. Technical architecture, software, data, integrations, security, reliability, and lifecycle operation.
6. Implementation sequencing, acceptance tests, migration, rollback, and maintenance.
7. Business problems, economic consequences, willingness to pay, delivery proof, and reusable assets.
8. Human-governed self-evolution, validation, observability, and fresh-session discoverability.

For every material recommendation, state:

- Current design claim.
- Supporting evidence.
- Challenging evidence.
- Verdict: keep, modify, test, defer, or reject.
- Exact reason.
- Smallest proposed change.
- Owner.
- Acceptance test.
- Approval boundary.
- Return path.
- What evidence would reverse the verdict.

### Phase 5 — Pressure-test the major architecture questions

Explicitly test:

- Whether the ten functional roles are collectively exhaustive and mutually distinguishable in real routing cases.
- Whether CASTLE’s elevation reduces ambiguity without creating copied authority.
- Whether Watchtower should remain separate from CASTLE.
- Whether one universal intake door is sufficient.
- Whether CASTLE can oversee cross-wiki investigation without becoming a knowledge-refinery hub.
- Whether the proposed instruction-register distinction needs metadata, validation, companion documents, or no structural change.
- Whether path-move integrity, broken references, canonical-copy violations, heading-anchor integrity, and instruction-register regressions require one tool or separate validators.
- Whether the design defines migration order, dependency discovery, rollback, performance limits, failure recovery, and fresh-session acceptance clearly enough.
- Whether the structure makes the knowledge-to-value path visible without forcing premature monetization.
- Whether the proposed architecture reduces real friction enough to justify its migration cost and stale-reference risk.

Use concrete `.ROOT` cases wherever possible.

### Phase 6 — Independent challenge

Before finalizing, perform an adversarial review from four positions:

- Minimalist: What should not change?
- Operator: What will fail during normal weekly use?
- Engineer: What interfaces, dependencies, failure modes, or acceptance checks are missing?
- Business challenger: What creates measurable value, and what is merely internal elegance?

Resolve the challenges with evidence. Preserve genuine disagreement as an open decision for Chris.
