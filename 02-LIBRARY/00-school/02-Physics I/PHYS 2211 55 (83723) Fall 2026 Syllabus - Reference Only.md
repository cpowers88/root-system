## Course Information

This course is an introductory calculus-based course on classical mechanics, waves, and special relativity. The student will be able to apply Newton’s laws and conservation of energy and momentum to various problems in kinematics and dynamics, use the law of universal gravitation to analyze the behavior of falling objects and objects in orbital motion, describe simple harmonic motion, oscillations, and waves, and explain the basic ideas of special relativity. The student must have a strong grasp of algebra, trigonometry, and calculus without exception, as these subjects will be heavily utilized throughout the course. Calculus will aid in developing concepts, which will subsequently be applied to problem-solving.

## Course Learning Outcomes

- Explain and interpret physical situations as stated in a word problem.
- Analyze and solve kinematical and dynamics problems using pictorial, graphical, physical, or mathematical representations (including calculus and vectors).
- Apply the laws of motion and conservation laws of energy and momentum to address problems in mechanics.
- Use principles of physics, algebra, and calculus to solve problems in mechanics symbolically and numerically.
- Solve complex real-world problems by constructing one or more physics models that can be classified and analyzed.

## Course Requirements and Assignments

**Homework**

Homework assignments and grading will be done through WebAssign online homework tool. Assignments will consist of a series of numerical, mathematical, or conceptual problems pertaining to physical concepts recently covered during lecture. Homework will be assigned weekly and is due by the following Monday at 11:59 pm. Due dates will be listed on WebAssign in tentative schedule. You are responsible for checking the due date and plan to submit it on time. Late submissions will not be accepted, as answer keys will be available through WebAssign after the due date. Extensions for assignments can be granted but with a penalty. Homework submitted after the deadline will be subject to a 10% deduction per calendar day (including weekends). Extension requests should be made before the original deadlines through email ([sbehera@kennesaw.edu](mailto:sbehera@kennesaw.edu)). Working on homework problems with groups is allowed but copying from others’ work is not. You also can find my help. One of the best practices is to solve the homework problems in a separate notebook. For any problem, you have worked but did not receive credit from WebAssign, you are allowed to show it to me before the deadline to get feedback and credit if appropriate.

**Quizzes**

There will be a 15-min weekly quiz available to you each Friday at 11:59 pm. The quiz will be available for 48 hours, that is, each quiz is due on Sun at 11:59 pm. It consists of several multiple-choice questions covering the core concepts in recent lectures and there will be only one attempt for each question. To access a quiz, you must click on the “Activities” tab in D2L and select Quizzes. Then click on the active quiz available. The Quizzes will require the lockdown browser and webcam. You are responsible for checking the due date and submitting your quiz on time. No extension will be given as the answer will be available after the due date. Check module 1 in D2L to learn about the quiz format in D2L. A sample “Syllabus Quiz” is available to familiarize you with the quiz environment.

**Participation**

Students will participate in in-class activities during lectures and breakout sessions. They serve to assess your understanding and encourage continuous engagement. Active engagement—such as contributing to discussions, responding to polls, and participating in group activities during lectures, as well as small-group problem-solving during breakout sessions—will earn you participation points.

**Exams**

There will be three unit-exams and one cumulative final exam. The exams will assess both theoretical understanding and problem-solving skills. Exam dates are listed on the tentative schedule and will not change. You must mark the exam dates on your calendar. Exam content will be announced at least one week in advance. The lowest score among the four unit-exams will be dropped. If an exam is missed, that missed exam will count as the dropped score. If a second exam is missed due to illness or another unforeseeable circumstance, students must notify the instructor by email as soon as possible, preferably before the exam. Documentation may be required to be eligible for a make-up exam. Do not make any plans that conflict with the final exam. The final exam is mandatory and will not be rescheduled on an individual basis to accommodate other commitments.

## Course Policies

Active, engaged participation is essential for mastering the complex concepts in this calculus-based physics course. Students are expected and required to attend lectures and recitation (breakout) sections. Below are the specific policies regarding attendance, assignments, and examinations:

**Attendance Recording and Grading**: Attendance is not graded as a standalone percentage; however, it is recorded through your participation in **In-Class and Recitation Problems (10%)** and **Quizzes (5%)**. To ensure success, you are expected to arrive on time, remain for the entire session, and actively engage in all lectures and breakout sessions.

**Policy on Missing In-Class Graded Assignments**: To accommodate unforeseen circumstances, the **lowest score** for In-Class/Recitation problems and the **lowest Quiz score** will be dropped from your final grade calculation. This "drop" policy serves as the primary accommodation for occasional absences.

**Policy on Late Assignments**: All homework must be submitted via WebAssign by the specified deadline. While extensions may be requested through WebAssign before the original deadline, they are subject to a penalty. Once answer keys are released after the due date, late submissions will no longer be accepted and will receive a score of zero.

**Policy on Missed Exams:** This course includes three-unit exams, and the lowest score among them is automatically dropped.

- **Dropping a Missed Exam:** If you miss a unit exam for any reason, that zero will become your dropped score.
- **Rescheduling:** No make-up examinations will be scheduled on an individual basis for personal plans or non-emergencies.
- **Exceptions**: For documented unavoidable conflicts (such as jury duty or military deployment) or verified medical emergencies, please contact the instructor as soon as reasonably possible to discuss potential arrangements.

**AI Usage Policy:**

You are expected to **generate your own work** in this class. When you submit any graded work (including homework, quizzes, lab reports, projects, or exams), you are affirming that it was **created entirely by you** unless you have explicitly indicated otherwise using **quotation marks** and provided **proper citation** for any external sources you used.

**Permitted Use of AI:** You may use AI tools only as a learning aid, not as a substitute for your own thinking. Appropriate uses include:

- Reviewing explanations of course concepts
- Practicing problem-solving techniques (Use AI as a tutoring resource to guide you through the problem-solving process, rather than to generate final answers)
- Exploring examples or seeking clarification on difficult topics

When used appropriately, AI can support learning and help deepen your understanding of the material.

**Prohibited Use of AI:**

- AI-generated content may not be submitted as part of any graded assignment, quiz, lab, project, or exam.
- Using AI to generate answers, solutions, calculations, explanations, or written content for submission is considered cheating and constitutes a violation of the KSU Code of Academic Integrity.
- Prohibited use includes, but is not limited to:
	1. Copying or paraphrasing AI-generated text into submitted work
		2. Using AI to complete problems or write explanations that are submitted as your own

Although it may be possible to receive homework credit by submitting work that is not your own or that is generated using AI, doing so will significantly impair your ability to succeed on unit exams and the final exam and may ultimately result in failure of the course. Solving problems independently and submitting your own work is essential for developing the skills and conceptual understanding required for success in this course.

If, after reviewing the course syllabus, a student anticipates or should have anticipated a need for accommodation, he or she must submit documentation requesting an accommodation and permitting time for a determination prior to submitting assignments or taking course quizzes or exams. Students may not request retroactive accommodation for needs that were or should have been foreseeable. Students should contact the office as soon as possible in the term for which they are seeking accommodations. Student Disability Services is located in Kennesaw Hall, Room 1205 on the Kennesaw campus and Building A in Suite 160G on the Marietta campus. Please visit the [Student Disability Services (SDS) website](https://campus.kennesaw.edu/current-students/student-affairs/dean-of-students/student-disability-services/) for more information, or call the office at 470-578-2666 (Kennesaw campus) or 470-578-7361 (Marietta campus).

### Copyright Law:

It is the responsibility of KSU faculty and students to respect the rights of copyright holders and complying with copyright law. The University System of Georgia recognizes that the exclusive rights of copyright holders are balanced by limitations on those rights under federal copyright law, including the right to make a fair use of copyrighted materials and the right to perform or display works in the course of face-to-face teaching activities.

The University System of Georgia facilitates compliance with copyright law and, where appropriate, the exercise in good faith of full fair use rights by faculty and staff in teaching, research, and service activities. The University System of Georgia ensure compliance with copyright law in the following ways.

1. The USG informs and educates students, faculty, and staff about copyright law, including the limited exclusive rights of copyright holders as set forth in 17 U.S.C. § 106, the application of the four fair use factors in 17 U.S.C. § 107, and other copyright exceptions.
2. The USG develops and makes available tools and resources for faculty and staff to assist in determining copyright status and ownership and determining whether use of a work in a specific situation would be a fair use and, therefore, not an infringement under copyright law;
3. The USG facilitates use of materials currently licensed by the University System of Georgia and provides information on licensing of third-party materials by the University System; and
4. The USG identifies individuals at the University System and member institutions who can counsel faculty and staff regarding application of copyright law.

### Withdrawal from Classes:

A student who officially withdraws from a course by the end of the last day to withdraw without academic penalty will receive a grade of “W” and receive no credit.

A student will receive a refund only when the student withdraws from ALL courses for the applicable semester and only by the schedule outlined in the University refund policy found in the Tuition, Expenses, & Financial Aid section of the undergraduate and graduate university catalogs.

Students should be aware that a reduction in their hours might result in the loss of full-time student status and thus affect their financial aid, scholarships, athletic and ticket eligibility, University housing accommodations, use of University resources and access to University facilities, visa for international students, and Veterans Educational Benefits. Students should contact the appropriate office and their academic advisor with questions about the impact of their withdrawal from a course before initiating a withdrawal. Veterans and dependents of veterans who receive educational benefits must notify the Veterans Education Benefits Area in the Office of the Registrar of any course load reductions.

#### Hardship Withdrawals

If a student experiences significant personal hardship (e.g., medical or family emergency, prolonged illness), the Dean of Students can approve a hardship withdrawal from all courses in the term for which the student is currently registered. In the case of an approved hardship withdrawal from all courses, the Registrar will assign grades of “W” for those classes. The deadline for final approval of a hardship withdrawal by the Dean of Students is the last day of class for which the hardship withdrawal is sought. If the hardship withdrawal process is not complete by the last day of class for which the hardship withdrawal is sought, a student must appeal for a retroactive hardship withdrawal from the Academic Standing Committee.

Appeals for retroactive hardship withdrawals must be directed to the Academic Standing Committee. Retroactive hardship withdrawals are rarely granted if it has been more than one year since the last day of class for which the withdrawal is sought. Extraordinary justification must be shown. In the case of approved retroactive hardship withdrawals, the Registrar will assign a grade of “W.”

#### University Initiated Withdrawals

If a student is suspended by the Office of Student Conduct following a violation of the University’s Code of Conduct not related to academic dishonesty, the Office of Student Conduct may facilitate a University-initiated withdrawal from courses for which a student is registered for the term. The Registrar will assign grades of “W” for those classes.

#### Military Withdrawals

A student will receive a “WM” symbol for all courses and a full refund of tuition and mandatory fees and a pro rata refund of other fees for military and other service, as defined by [BOR Policy Manual, Section 7.3.5.3](https://www.usg.edu/policymanual/section7/C453/#p7.3.5_refunds) To request a military withdrawal, the student must submit a copy of official orders to the Office of the Registrar.
