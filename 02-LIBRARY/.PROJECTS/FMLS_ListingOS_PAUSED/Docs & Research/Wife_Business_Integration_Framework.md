# Bringing Her Into the Business — A Conversation Framework
#FMLS #projects0
## For Chris's Eyes Only | Internal Planning Doc
### Last updated: May 24, 2026

---

## THE HONEST SITUATION

She is excited but skeptical. That is exactly the right starting point.
Skepticism from someone with domain expertise is not resistance — it is quality control.
Her skepticism will make the product better if you listen to it correctly.

Do NOT try to talk her out of the skepticism.
DO let the working product answer it.

---

## THE THREE ROLES — KNOW WHICH ONE YOU ARE ENGAGING

### Role 1: Domain Expert (starts today)
She knows the workflow. You do not. She is the authority in the room on
what the FMLS process actually looks like, what the pain is, and what
"working well" means. Treat her accordingly.

How to engage this role:
- Ask questions. Do not explain things.
- Write down everything she says. Literally everything.
- Say "show me" more than you say anything else.
- Never tell her how something should work — ask her how it does work.

### Role 2: First Client / Validator (starts when the first version works)
She is not doing you a favor by testing the tool.
You are building something for her because she is the person with the problem.
The tool needs to earn her daily use before it earns anything else.

How to engage this role:
- Give her a specific, limited thing to test — not the whole vision.
- Watch her use it. Do not explain it. If she needs instructions, the tool is wrong.
- Every friction point she finds is a bug report, not a criticism of you.
- Log everything she says while using it.

### Role 3: Business Partner / Future Asset (starts when the product earns it)
This conversation happens AFTER the tool saves her real time on real listings.
Not before. Not during the build. After.
When she says "this actually works" unprompted — that is when you have it.

## THE CONVERSATION TO HAVE THIS WEEK

**Frame it as:** "I need your expertise to build this right."
Not: "I want to show you what I'm working on."
Not: "I need your help."

The first framing makes her the expert. The other two make her the helper.
She is not helping you. You are serving her.

**What to cover:**
1. You want to build the PDF filler for her listings first
2. To do it right you need to understand her exact process
3. You have a set of questions — can you sit down for 30–45 minutes?
4. The first version will do one specific thing: fill [the most painful form] automatically
5. You want to have something to show her within [realistic timeline — 2-3 weeks]

**What NOT to say yet:**
- The full 5-year plan
- The agency concept
- Selling it to other agents
- How much money this could eventually make
- That she might someday run part of the business

None of that until the product earns the conversation.

## THE SKEPTICISM — HOW TO USE IT CORRECTLY

Her skepticism likely lives in one of three places:

**"Can you actually build this?"**
The answer is not a speech about how committed you are.
The answer is showing her something that works.
Timeline: 2-3 weeks for a working PDF filler on one form.
That is the proof. Nothing else is.

**"Will this actually save me time or create more work?"**
Valid concern. The intake step adds friction.
The time savings has to exceed the time cost by a meaningful margin.
One manual form that takes 15 minutes should take 90 seconds with the tool.
Anything less than a 5x time reduction on the most painful form
is not good enough for daily adoption.

**"What happens if something goes wrong with a real listing?"**
Also valid. Real estate paperwork has legal implications.
The tool assists — it does not replace her review.
She always has final eyes on every document before anything is signed or submitted.
Build that into the product framing from day one.

## WHAT SHE BRINGS TO THE BUSINESS — ASSETS YOU HAVE NOT FULLY ACCOUNTED FOR

**Active MLS access and license:**
She can access forms, comps, and listing data that you cannot.
This is infrastructure the product depends on. Treat it as such.

**Credibility with the agent community:**
When the tool works, her word carries more weight than any marketing.
One agent telling another agent "my husband built this and I actually use it"
closes more deals than any sales pitch you will ever write.

**Domain knowledge that cannot be replicated:**
She knows the edge cases. The weird listings. The problem sellers.
The forms that change every year. The MLS rule updates.
This knowledge is the product's competitive moat.

**Direct access to the target customer:**
She works alongside the exact people you want to sell this to.
Every complaint she hears from a colleague is market research.

---

## LONGER TERM — IF AND WHEN THE CONVERSATION IS EARNED

When the product proves itself and the business starts to take shape,
there is a real conversation to be had about her role.

Possible structures down the road:
- She is the product manager for ListingOS — she owns the roadmap
- She is the sales channel to the agent community — referral or partnership structure
- She operates the client-facing side while you operate the build side
- She becomes a licensed partner in the agency for real estate specific clients

None of these conversations happen before the product works.
All of them become natural once it does.

---

## THE TIMELINE THAT EARNS THE TRUST

**Week 1–2:**
Run the requirements session. Get her answers to all questions.
Build the digital intake form based on what she tells you.

**Week 2–3:**
Build the PDF filler for the most painful form she identified.
Show her a working version on a test listing.

**Week 3–4:**
She uses it on one real listing alongside her normal process.
You watch. You log every friction point.
You fix what broke.

**Month 2:**
She uses it as her primary workflow — not alongside, instead of.
That is the validation moment.

**Month 3:**
She mentions it to one colleague without you asking.
That is the product-market fit signal.

---

## ONE FINAL NOTE

She is already bought in on the shared goal.
She does not need to be sold.
She needs to see you execute.

The questionnaire is the first act of execution.
Show up prepared. Take good notes. Fix what she tells you is wrong.
That is the whole job right now.
